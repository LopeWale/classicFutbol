# Commitment and Urgency Drive Off-Ball Teammates

EA and Konami drive the AI teammates on a human's side with four mechanisms that the current sim either lacks or inverts: a **space-ownership field** (who reaches a point first) that picks support points and run end-points, **committed decisions with hysteresis** rather than per-tick re-selection, a **per-player, threat-driven urgency state** gated by work rate in the defensive transition instead of a flat cruise cap, and a **one-plus-one engagement rule** (the controlled player plus at most one AI presser, who shadows rather than tackles) with zone handover, a shared back line and marking by danger rather than proximity. EA's own FIFA 13 producers said FIFA 12's runners "might start running and change their minds maybe three or four times during your run" and could only run in straight lines to an end point ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/tech/post/_/id/570/fifa-13-five-things-you-need-to-know)), which is exactly the failure the sim's pocket search and per-tick support-triangle pick reproduce. Real-football tracking fixes the transition numbers the games never publish: defensive transitions average **9.3 s**, counter-pressing is a **5–6 s** window, four or more players behind the ball after one second is the decisive threshold, and a high-intensity recovery run is **about 26 m in 3 s**, so forwards should sprint briefly to a holding line and stop, not jog for ten seconds ([Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7); [Ju et al. 2023](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/17199/1/BS_Art_46976-10.pdf)). Steering oscillation has three distinct causes (symmetric head-on, disagreement about passing side, discrete sample switching) and each needs a different cure: reciprocity plus a deterministic tiebreak, a per-pair remembered side, and a lateral-sign penalty with a dwell time, backed by a critically damped filter and incumbent bonuses on the decision side. The sim's audit and the r4 baseline locate the gaps precisely: **50 side flips per player-minute in 11v11 and 83 in 5v5**, with the highest per-minute rate in class 0 (carrier and chaser, which bypass avoidance entirely); **2.05 forwards still ahead of the ball six seconds after a turnover** in 11v11 while counter-press non-engagers are hard-capped at 70% speed; **0.83 runs in behind per ten minutes** because every run target is clamped onside; and three simultaneous engagers on the human side because teammate contain is invisible to the team plan. The design below is ordered for payoff per unit of risk: steering hysteresis first (cheap, deterministic, measurable), then transition urgency, then the support and run scorer, then cover shadows, marking handover and the shared line, with every parameter labelled by evidence grade and tied to an existing `psfc-replay analyze` metric.

## FIFA chose run end-points from owned space and paid for re-deciding them

The only first-party description of the mechanism behind FIFA's off-ball attack is EA Canada's own 2002 patent by Jason Rupert and Geoffrey Harrower, which divides the pitch into **Voronoi cells with players as sites**, computed from forecast positions ("offsetting the player's position forward a bit, in the direction of motion, and in proportion to the player's speed"), and uses the result for "where offensive and defensive players should position themselves… when and where a player should make an offensive run… when and where a player should pass the ball… defensive marking logic… collision avoidance/detection" ([Google Patents US7588493B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7588493B2/en)). Its run rule is a single sentence: "If any player on the attacking team has a Voronoi vertex… that is closer to Team B's net than the offside line, then that player makes an offensive through run to that vertex. This models a player's behavior when the player realizes that he/she can get to that piece of space before anyone else." Support is lane-seeking in the same vocabulary: lines from the ball holder to his Voronoi neighbours define passing lanes, "and then another Voronoi diagram might be used to calculate which teammates are closest to these passing lanes. Based on that information, teammates move towards these lines." The pass target is "the intersection of BH's facing vector and the Voronoi cell of receiver(s)", led as far as the receiver can still reach first; offside is a post-filter on candidate actions ([US7588493B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7588493B2/en)). Nothing in this model caps the number of runners, and nothing states a support distance in metres: distance falls out of the tactics sliders, where Short build-up passing means "players closest to the ball will come to support" and Long means "players farther from the ball possessor will attempt to find space" ([TheGameReviews, FIFA 09 Custom Tactics](https://www.thegamereviews.com/article-781-fifa-09-custom-tactics.html)). The same guide separates the knobs a sim should expose: Build Up Speed "alters the urgency that teammates have getting into forward positions", Chance Creation Passing shifts between "safe options" and "runs into space and behind the defence", Crossing sets "the timing of when players make runs into the box, and how many players will look to get into the box", and Positioning Free Form permits "fullbacks… overlapping runs, wide players… cut in more, and central midfielders… runs to the corner or overlap the strikers" ([TheGameReviews](https://www.thegamereviews.com/article-781-fifa-09-custom-tactics.html)). Per player, the FIFA 09–13 formation editor's "Attacking Work Rate influences how often the player considers making forward runs", and the Positioning offsets "are not runs. They merely instruct the player to favour adjusting his position in a direction when the team is either on offense or defence" ([TheGameReviews, Editing Formations](https://www.thegamereviews.com/article-786-fifa-09-editing-formations.html)). FIFA 12 added a global "Run frequency" slider that "increases/decreases the number of runs teammates will make" ([Neoseeker](https://www.neoseeker.com/forums/54562/t1676903-gameplay-slider-list/)); testers found 100 "effectively leaving large parts of the team out of position" and settled near 80 ([Wootball](https://wootball.blogspot.com/2012/08/fifa-12-wootballs-recommended-slider.html)).

What FIFA 10–12 did wrong is documented by the people who rewrote it. Aaron McHardy, selling FIFA 13: "In 'FIFA 12,' they might start running and change their minds maybe three or four times during your run… Players were taking themselves out of the game because they couldn't decide what to do. Now, you have players analyzing the entire length of the run… when a player decides to go for a run, he sticks with it", and "What they would do last year, they would just run to a different spot, even if it wasn't the best spot for them to go to, but that's all we could do when players were forced to run in straight lines" ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/tech/post/_/id/570/fifa-13-five-things-you-need-to-know)). A fan transcript of EA's FIFA 13 presentation adds the onside failure: a teammate "standing 6 yards offside… now decides he's going to start running in the opposite direction", fixed by having players "hold their runs accordingly… curve their runs to stay onside and open up their bodies", with run behaviour "dependent on players attributes (for example Attacker Positioning)" and short-term fatigue ([UltimateFIFA, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20130826080611id_/http://ultimatefifa.com/2012/fifa-13-ai-improvements-attacking-intelligence/)); GamesRadar reported that teammates "will stutter step, if necessary, to keep the run onside" and that a full-back "is already starting to get 10 meters forward" during build-up instead of "hold[ing] position until called upon" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/fifa-13-first-look-what-comes-after-revolution/)). FIFA 10 had already claimed curved onside runs and an urgency system ("When players aren't involved in immediate 'action' they're more relaxed. As play gets closer they become more alert") ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2009/06/16/ea-talk-fifa-10/)), and FIFA 14 added run types: "attacking players will watch the backline to avoid being caught offside before breaking free… Attackers will also now perform checked runs" ([EA, FIFA 14 Teammate Intelligence](https://www.ea.com/news/fifa-14-teammate-intelligence)). The lesson for a FIFA 10–12-style sim is that the authentic era behaviour included end-point-only runs, straight-line locomotion and passive support, and that EA itself judged those to be defects: a sim should keep the ownership-based target selection and adopt the FIFA 13 commitment and hold-at-the-line fixes.

Konami ships the same idea as a geometric rule with explicit gates. Its 2011 patent (the "Active AI" era) places "a plurality of positions in an area around the position of the user character as the receiving position candidates… around a perimeter of a geometric shape" at a reference distance **L** and **L√2** on the diagonals, carried along with the carrier; a candidate is open space when "neither of player characters… is positioned around the receiving position candidate" and "a player character belonging to the user team… is able to reach the receiving position candidate earlier than a player character belonging to the opponent team", with arrival time read from "the running parameter of the player character"; the candidate "having the longest distance to a player character belonging to the opponent team" wins, may be displaced away from the nearest opponent, and the nearest teammate is sent toward it before the pass unless he is farther than a predetermined distance or "the base position of a teammate player character" (his formation slot) is too far from the candidate ([Google Patents US9180371B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9180371B2/en)). That formation leash is why PES support stays local and shape-anchored, and it is what the "Support Range" slider stores: at 100 "player will keep a far distance from ball carrier to play a long passing game", at 0 "players will stay close", while "Player Support" at 100 moves options ahead of the carrier and at 0 behind him ([PES Mastery, Team Style](https://pesmastery.com/pes-team-style/)). PES 2017–2021 then scripted group patterns in plain words, for example Wing Rotation ("a teammate will head to the touchline to give them passing options. Other teammates will then run into the space that has been created") and False No.9 ("As he comes back for the ball, his teammates will push forward to fill the space he has created") ([PES 2021 myClub Guide](https://pesmyclubguide.com/advanced-instructions)). eFootball's patch notes show the same de-duplication and onside problems being fixed as late as 2024: "players running towards the goal in anticipation of a cross may end up running into the same position as other teammates" and "players sometimes stopped running in an inadequate timing when trying to run behind the opponent's defence line" ([Konami v4.0.0](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en/page/v4/versioninfo_v4-00)); v3.0.0 made offside players "quickly make themselves onside" and raised in-behind run frequency for Poachers ([Konami v3.0.0](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en-us/page/2024/versioninfo_v3-00)).

Real-football analytics supplies the geometry the games leave as parameters. In the J1 League, "coordinated support play is possible when three or more players form a [diamond] at a distance of approximately **13 to 24 m** from the ball carrier", with three-or-more support rising from 10.9 m ([Matsubara et al. 2022](https://content.scirp.org/pdf/ape_2022053016232546.pdf)); passing lanes stay open only **0.91 ± 1.00 s** for penetrative options and 1.27 s for support options ([Gómez-Jordana et al. 2019](https://static.capabiliaserver.com/frontend/clients/barca/wp_prod/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/a41ec6ba-final_paper_barcelona-luis-gomez-jordana.pdf)), and availability is best evaluated at the receiver's position one second ahead when he moves faster than 2 m/s ([Gómez-Jordana et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8105376)). Who gets passed to is decided by proximity first, then lane openness, then defensive coverage, then forwardness ([Steiner et al. 2018](https://ciss-journal.org/article/download/7564/10559/29272)); completion falls from 96% backward to 72% forward and 37% into the box ([Power et al. 2017](https://mirkwood.cs.edinboro.edu/~bennett/class/csci480/fall2017/notes/p1605.pdf)), and the strongest xPass features are "the possible speed window in which the pass could be completed… followed by the distance of the closest opponent to the receiver", with completed passes spanning a direction window of 32.7° ± 16.3° ([Anzer & Bauer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00810-3)). The computable core that every pitch-control model shares is a time-to-intercept race with a **0.7 s reaction and 5 m/s top speed**, a logistic with **σ ≈ 0.45–0.54 s**, a control rate **λ ≈ 4 s⁻¹** with defenders at **1.72×**, and offside attackers zeroed ([LaurieOnTracking](https://github.com/Friends-of-Tracking-Data-FoTD/LaurieOnTracking/blob/master/Metrica_PitchControl.py); [Spearman 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327139841_Beyond_Expected_Goals)); Voronoi ownership, EA's 2002 choice, is its zero-reaction special case. Robot soccer closes the loop on de-confliction: B-Human assigns Queen/Knight/Bishop roles through a single captain because "re-planning in each behavior cycle bears the risk of oscillations" ([Röfer et al. 2017](https://www.informatik.uni-bremen.de/kogrob/papers/RC-Roefer-etal-18.pdf)), and Gliders2d picks receiver points from Voronoi vertices of opponent positions and their intersections with the offside line ([Gliders2d](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.10202)).

## Defensive teammates run on threat urgency, one-plus-one engagement and zone handover

FIFA never published a recovery timer for FIFA 10–12, but it did publish the structure. FIFA 10's producer: "When players aren't involved in the action, they're a little more relaxed, but as soon as they see a threatening position, they'll run with a lot more urgency… That works for both defenders and attackers", alongside a threat analysis so that "if there's a gap somewhere behind the defenders, then they won't switch out their positions" and a "position priority" so Defensive Midfielders "cover at the back if a Centre back has gone up… and they won't leave their post until it's safe" ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/fifa-10-impressions/1100-6211111); [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2009/06/16/ea-talk-fifa-10/)). FIFA 11 made who comes back a per-player attribute: separate attacking and defensive work rates, with EA's own contrast that Rooney "will run back to help the defense as well as make offensive runs" while Berbatov "will barely make a forward run, never mind run back and help his defenders" ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/fifa_soccer_2011/b/ps3/archive/2010/06/09/fifa-11%3A-the-players-take-the-pitch.aspx)). That matters for the sim's "forwards jog back" complaint: a low-DWR forward staying high is correct FIFA behaviour, and the fix should target medium/high-DWR forwards. The first official time window arrives only in FIFA 19's "Pressure After Possession Loss", under which "your teammates will press the ball for approximately **7 seconds**" before reverting ([FIFPlay, FIFA 19 Tactics](https://www.fifplay.com/fifa-19-tactics/)); FIFA 21 then capped the D-pad Team Press at 20 s (from 30 s), made it affect "only your AI teammates", and raised stamina decay under pressing styles from 2.2× to **3×** ([EA, FIFA 21 TU5](https://www.ea.com/games/fifa/pitch-notes/news/fifa-21-pitch-notes-title-update-5)). Stamina is the balancing cost of urgency in every EA pressing lever.

Engagement is one-plus-one. FIFA 11's auto-press "would follow it to the other side of the pitch if the button was held for long enough" and "formations would be all over the place", so FIFA 12 "stripped out the overwhelming pressure feature, where we'd launch defenders up the pitch like homing missiles" and replaced it with a second man who "won't launch a tackle, it's just coverage—it's cover off… the AI will track the player with the ball and try and put him into a disadvantageous position while you can maneuver yourself to try and close down passing lanes" ([EA, Tactical Defending Help](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/the-backpage-tactical-defending-help); [IGN, FIFA 12 Developer Tips](https://www.ign.com/wikis/fifa-12/Developer_Tips)). McHardy described what the contain player actually does: "position himself in a good spot and wait and be in a good position to intercept potential passes or pick up a ball that the dribbler's given a bad touch" ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/fifa-12-mega-interview-with-lead-gameplay-director-aaron-mchardy)). Holders are described in football terms that amount to a cover shadow by construction: "facing the player in possession while goal-side of them", wide midfielders "show attackers infield where they know they'll have more bodies in the way to intercept", "force a player on to his weak foot by standing to the attacker's stronger side", and "all defenders will stay on their feet until they're as certain as they can be that they'll get something on the ball" ([EA, Tactical Defending Explained](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/the-backpage-tactical-defending-explained)). The handover rule EA used as its model is zonal: "A Barça player will press the ball possessor if he enters his zone of the pitch, but as soon as he leaves he passes him on to a team-mate and so retains the overall shape" ([EA, Tactical Defending Help](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/the-backpage-tactical-defending-help)), with pressure beginning "just before the edge of the centre circle" and rising toward goal ([EA, FIFA 13 Defending Tips](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/fifa-13-defending-tips-the-backpage)). The era's documented marking failure is also the sim's: teammate contain would make a centre-back "abandon his man" ("the other centre-back is automatically tracking the run of his strike partner… so your other centre-back comes across to help out… leaving the striker's team-mate totally free") ([EA, FIFA 12 Slow Down](https://www.ea.com/en-au/news/backpage-fifa12-back-to-basics)), and FIFA 14's fix replaced nearest-opponent marking: "Rather than always covering the player closest to them, AI defenders will better position themselves to reduce the chance of someone being left wide-open. Defensive decision-making will be based on multiple frames enabling defenders to continually analyze the play to search for the most dangerous player" ([EA, FIFA 14 Teammate Intelligence](https://www.ea.com/news/fifa-14-teammate-intelligence)). "Multiple frames" is temporal smoothing of the mark target, the same device a deterministic sim needs.

Line height was three levers in FIFA 09–13 (formation base positions, mentality as a unit shift, Cover vs Offside Trap) and the human was expected to drop a centre-back manually when a counter began ([EA, FIFA 13 Defend Against Pace](https://ea.com/en-gb/news/fifa-13-defend-against-pace)). FC 25 finally wrote the policy down: Deep "1–30, default 25, line will drop with runs"; Balanced "31–60, default 50, line is flexible to track runs"; High "61–90, default 70, line rarely tracks runs"; Aggressive "91–100, default 95, line will never track runs; line steps up to keep highest line possible, pressure on heavy touch, extra stamina drain" ([EA, FC 25 FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)). Width is FIFA's zonal-versus-man dial: Narrow "pinch in on the far side to cover teammates", Wide "players on the far side will mark opponents tightly, sacrificing cover" ([FIFPlay, FIFA 19 Tactics](https://www.fifplay.com/fifa-19-tactics/)). The one explicit statement of who drops back first is FIFA 20's Striker Drop Back, which "will consider defensive work rates and the marking attribute before selecting which player will drop back" ([EA, D-Pad Tactics Deep Dive](https://ea.com/news/pitch-notes-d-pad-tactics)).

Konami's patents give the same roles in frame-exact form, all on a 1/60 s loop. The press controller selects defenders by distance to the ball and makes them "maintain, for a reference period of time after the instruction, a threshold distance or longer" before closing, with the period read from a five-entry table "in units of 1/60th of a second" that is longer when the pressing team leads and shorter when it trails ([USPTO US8333663](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8333663)); pressing is inhibited inside a strip near the opponent's own goal line whose depth grows with the lead and lifts after "a predetermined time (e.g., 10 seconds)" ([USPTO US8398487](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8398487)). The marker patent is the cheapest deterministic "jump the lane only if covered" rule in the record: default position "between the marking target player character and the predetermined position" (goal), switching to the target–ball intercept line only when the carrier's facing is within a reference angle θa of the marked man and a non-keeper teammate lies within a reference distance La of the man-to-goal line ([USPTO US8597094](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8597094)). eFootball's "Call for Pressure" states the two-tier block outright: "only 1 teammate will be chosen to apply pressure in a given moment", and the others, "based on their distance from the ball… will either position themselves to block passing lanes or push up the defensive line", deactivating on a completed pass or timeout with stamina draining most near the ball ([Konami v1.0.0](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en-us/page/2022/season1_patch-notes_v1-0-0)). Konami's own failure modes are instructive for the sim's forwards: PES "tended to go into a shape" the moment the ball was lost ([GamingBolt, PES 2018](https://gamingbolt.com/pes-2018-interview-our-focus-is-to-keep-the-action-on-the-pitch)), and v6.0.0 still had to make "a forward… more likely to track back near their own goal when the opponent has launched a deep attack" and differentiate AI defender reaction timing by Defensive Awareness ([Konami v6.0.0](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en-us/page/v6/versioninfo_v6-00)).

## Real transitions last nine seconds and recovery runs are three-second sprints

Labelled Bundesliga tracking puts "the average duration of all transitions phases" at **9.34 s**, counter-pressing at 9.89 s and pure fall-back phases at **18.30 s**; counter-pressing is the minority choice (about 27% of transitions, with 62.5% of ball losses assignable to neither strategy), success is "regained within five seconds", and the decisive features are "the speed of the regaining team two seconds after the turnover" and the count of players behind the ball: "If less than four players are behind the ball, this feature on average decreases the chance of a counterpressing classification. The number of four defenders—almost half of the team—seems to be a decisive threshold" ([Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)). Numerical superiority within **10 m** of the ball at the turnover raises the five-second regain rate from **30.2% to 36.2%** (same source); StatsBomb uses the same five-second cut-off and reports a 4–5 yard pressure radius ([StatsBomb](https://blogarchive.statsbomb.com/articles/soccer/how-statsbomb-data-helps-measure-counter-pressing/); [Pressing Intensity](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.04712)); coaching's version is "press at top speed and with maximum intensity for five seconds… If the team hasn't recovered the ball within five seconds… fall back into their defensive formation" ([Spielverlagerung](https://spielverlagerung.com/?p=352)). GPS data on elite players shows how hard: during transitions "high-velocity activities were 7-9-fold greater than the 90-min demands", mean transition duration about 10 s with peaks of 22–27 s ([Bortnik et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9536371)), and by position full-backs and central midfielders sprint most in defensive transitions while "Attackers (A) had significantly lower TD and HSRD compared to all other positions… during defensive transitions", though still about 53 m/min of high-speed running, roughly seven times their whole-match rate ([Bortnik et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10955741/)).

The shape of an individual recovery is a burst. EPL video coding defines a Recovery Run as "Player runs back towards their own goal to be goal side when out of position" above 19.8 km/h and finds it averages **26–27 m over 3 ± 2 s**, 4–8 times per match depending on position, while Close Down/Press actions are **about 15 m in 1 s**; forwards do the fewest recovery runs and the most closing-down ([Ju et al. 2023](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/17199/1/BS_Art_46976-10.pdf)). After peak periods "players/teams performed 20-53% less high-intensity distances for 'Covering' and 'Recovery Run'" ([Ju et al. 2022](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/15958/1/BS_Art_45951-10.pdf)), which licenses a fatigue multiplier on recovery urgency. Rest defence is measured too: at the moment of a ball loss, Bundesliga teams keep **3.70 ± 0.76 defenders against 2.01 ± 0.93 attackers** in the 10 m band in front of the deepest defender, **28 m wide**, about **44 m from their own goal line**, with the nearest defender **5.1 m** from each attacker; "regaining possession as fast as possible after a ball loss is the most important success factor", and deeper, more compact rest defences do better ([Forcher et al. 2023](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf)). The games' own numbers bracket these: FIFA 19's 7 s press window ([FIFPlay](https://www.fifplay.com/fifa-19-tactics/)), and a fan datamine of eFootball's playstyles that reports a "post-regain window duration" of 3 s or 5 s during which the playstyle's transition pattern runs ([eFHUB](https://efhub.com/articles/261021_3-the-six-team-playstyles-underlying-parameters-revealed); community, unverified).

Put together, the sources describe a two-window transition with a three-way split of players. For about five seconds the nearest player presses, one or two more close lanes, the rest-defence core holds a line goal-side of the deepest opposing forward, and everyone ahead of the ball sprints until goal-side or until the behind-ball count is restored; after five seconds without a regain the pressers also fall back, and the team re-forms over roughly ten seconds. Forwards drop at high intensity for two to four seconds to a holding line, then stop and shadow a lane, which is what produces "lowest total distance but seven times normal high-speed running". A flat 70% cruise for every uninvolved player, held for the whole counter-press window, is the opposite of this profile and is what the audit identifies as the direct cause of slow forwards.

## Steering flips come from symmetry, side disagreement and discrete switching

The velocity-obstacle literature separates three oscillation mechanisms. Plain velocity obstacles "generate oscillations when… used for multi-agent navigation" because each agent assumes the other will not react; RVO fixes the symmetric head-on by having each take half the effort ([van den Berg et al., ICRA 2008](https://www.cs.unc.edu/~geom/RVO/icra2008.pdf)), yet RVO "frequently causes agents to end up in a 'reciprocal dance' as they cannot reach agreement on which side to pass each other", which HRVO fixes by enlarging the obstacle on the side they should not pass ([Douthwaite et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/141072/1/Manuscript.pdf)); the HRVO reference code chooses the side from the sign of `det(relativePosition, prefVelocity_ - other->prefVelocity_)`, that is from the agents' **preferred** velocities ([snape/HRVO Agent.cc](https://github.com/snape/HRVO/blob/main/src/Agent.cc)). ClearPath states the same constraint formally: each agent should pass "on the side closest to its current velocity" ([Guy et al., ClearPath](http://gamma-web.iacs.umd.edu/CA/ClearPath.pdf)), and the robotics literature treats the passing side as a topological commitment, "robot motion that avoids switching between passing sides as it approaches a human" ([Mavrogiannis et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.05084v4)), with learned policies showing a side preference "robust to a small offset in the initial condition" ([Chen et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.08862)). ORCA's smoothness comes from a continuous linear program with `line.point = velocity_ + 0.5F * u` and no smoothing step at all ([snape/RVO2 Agent.cc](https://github.com/snape/RVO2/blob/main/src/Agent.cc)); a sampled method cannot inherit that continuity. The third mechanism is the one that matters for the sim's DetourCrowd-style sampler: its author says "the sampled velocity obstacle will always have some jitter, as the velocity is chosen from one of the samples. The more samples you have, the less jitter", and "the end of the path is especially nasty" ([recastnavigation thread](https://groups.google.com/g/recastnavigation/c/daSIkbEiByo)). Detour's only hysteresis is in the penalty itself, `weightDesVel·|vcand−dvel|/vmax + weightCurVel·|vcand−vel|/vmax + weightSide·side + weightToi·1/(0.1 + tmin/horizTime)`, and in the pattern geometry, a search disc of radius `vmax·(1−velBias)` centred on `velBias·dvel` that halves per depth level, with shipped defaults **velBias 0.4, weightDesVel 2.0, weightCurVel 0.75, weightSide 0.75, weightToi 2.5** (0.5 for velBias in the demo presets) ([DetourObstacleAvoidance.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourObstacleAvoidance.cpp); [DetourCrowd.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourCrowd.cpp); [Tool_Crowd.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/RecastDemo/Source/Tool_Crowd.cpp)). With the sim's 1 s horizon a 0.1 s threat carries a penalty near 5·weightToi, far above the 2.75 maximum of the two velocity terms, so near-symmetric threats make the minimum jump between mirror-image samples; nothing in Detour penalises a lateral sign change specifically, which is why flips survive at any sample count (derivation from the cited formula). Human data say the horizon should stay short: pedestrian interaction energy saturates below about 200 ms and vanishes beyond about 3 s ([Karamouzas et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.1082)).

Force models fail for a related reason. Linearised, the social force model is a damped oscillator "ξ̈ + r ξ̇ + ω0² ξ = 0" that "does not oscillate if… (a/b) τ² < 1/4", and the common parameters "lead to oscillations" ([Chraibi 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.1133)); Millington's description of plain Seek is that the character "will overshoot, reverse, and oscillate through the target, or… orbit around the target" ([Millington & Funge, AI for Games, §3.3.4](https://dl.booksee.org/genesis/826000/3dd67e19636fe692b294142e3b1d014b/_as/%255BIan_Millington,_John_Funge%255D_Artificial_Intelligen%28BookSee.org%29.pdf)). The classic cures are the same four devices every time: an arrival behaviour that ramps speed inside a slowing radius, with the radius set from kinematics as v²/(2a) ([Charles University lecture](https://ksvi.mff.cuni.cz/~dingle/2025-6/ai_games/lecture_4.pdf)); a target radius inside which steering is zero; a one-shot side choice from predicted nearest approach, as in OpenSteer's `steerToAvoidNeighbors` ([OpenSteer SteerLibrary.h](https://github.com/meshula/OpenSteer/blob/master/include/OpenSteer/SteerLibrary.h)); and heading averaged over about ten frames for rendering, Buckland's `NumSamplesForSmoothing 10` ([Buckland params.ini](https://github.com/wangchen/Programming-Game-AI-by-Example-src/blob/master/Buckland_Chapter3-Steering%20Behaviors/params.ini); [WPI slides](http://web.cs.wpi.edu/~imgd4000/d16/slides/movement.pdf)). Animation-driven systems add a fifth: path stability. Hitman: Absolution's rule was "Path needs to be stable for planning to work… We can't constantly modify paths while performing CA" ([Anguelov et al., GDC 2012](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2012/slides/Summit_AI/Anguelov_Bobby_AnimationDrivenLocomotion.pdf)), and EA's motion-matching pipeline feeds the animation a trajectory of "1 second samples in the future, 1 second samples in the past" rather than the raw per-frame steering ([Zinno, GDC 2019](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2019/presentations/Zinno_Fabio_FromMotionMatching.pdf)), with a Root Motion Weight that at 0 is "driven by physics or motion model – consistent motion feel" ([Delannoy, GDC 2023](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2023/Slides/Motion+Matching+at+EA_Delannoy_JC.pdf)); FIFA 2010's own answer to responsiveness was to warp turn animations to "reach a specific point at a specific time" rather than re-steer each frame ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012884/Animation-Warping-for-Responsiveness-in)).

Control practice supplies the decision-side toolkit and its sizing rules. A hysteresis band "must be bigger than the noise on the signal… set the band at several times the peak-to-peak noise", with "a minimum run or off time" for anything that must not chatter ([plccode.ai](https://plccode.ai/hysteresis-and-deadband-in-structured-text)); dwell-time switching separates decisions by "a prespecified time interval… sufficiently large so that the switching does not destabilize the system", at the cost that "the currently active controller might deteriorate to an unacceptable level before the next switch is permitted", so a safety override is required ([Hespanha, Liberzon & Morse 2003](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0005109802002418)); utility AI uses "a current-behavior bonus that multiplies the active behavior's score" plus "a minimum commit time so a marginally higher score cannot interrupt an in-progress action", typically about 1.2× ([bugnet.io](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-utility-ai-oscillating-between-actions)). For moving targets, an alpha-beta filter with "positive and small" gains is two multiply-adds per axis and stable for `0 < α < 2, 0 < β < 4 − 2α` ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_beta_filter); [Tenne & Singh](https://code.eng.buffalo.edu/tracking/papers/tenne_optimalFilter2000.pdf)). For heading, a critically damped spring "will reach equilibrium as fast as possible without oscillating" and has a closed-form per-tick update with four precomputed coefficients ([Juckett](https://www.ryanjuckett.com/damped-springs); [harmonica spring.go](https://github.com/charmbracelet/harmonica/blob/master/spring.go)), whose lag equals the smoothing time when tracking constant motion ([Unreal CriticallyDampedSmoothing](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Core/FMath/CriticallyDampedSmoothing)); Holden notes that "even springs with quite a small halflife will do really well at removing any sudden jitters" ([Holden](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)). The sim's existing first-order low-pass is overshoot-free but carries no rate state, so it cannot pre-empt a reversal; a second-order filter can.

## The audit and the r4 baseline put numbers on each gap

The internal audit of `sim/psfc-sim` at d95d1b7 (treated here as ground truth for what exists) and the r4 baseline (`docs/match-analytics-baseline-r4.md`, 10 min × 3 seeds) agree on five problems. First, forwards jog back after a turnover: in COUNTER_PRESS "the whole non-engager defensive branch is skipped (`else if phase != AI_PHASE_COUNTER_PRESS`, 3732), so no recovery sprint, no retreat sprint, no mark", and every non-engager becomes class 3 with "sprint forced off, 70% cap (4110-4121), for the whole `cp_window` of 40-150 ticks" (audit §2). In DEFEND and RETREAT the Striker, Pivo and Winger slots can never mark (3200), the 11v11 striker's defensive slot is pinned at ball + 6 m up to halfway (2737-2740), and the recovery-sprint threshold `2.5 m + recovery × 2.5 mm` means "faster players wait longer" (3792-3800). There is no work-rate attribute; `work_rate` is stamina attribute × current stamina and "plays no part in recovery-run decisions or class assignment". The baseline confirms the symptom: **forwards_staying_high_mean 2.05** in 11v11 normal (both forwards still ahead of the ball at t0 + 6 s), **pct_outfield_behind_ball_at_3s 66.7%** and **at_6s 68.7%**, with **in_shape_at_turnover 52%**. The measured **forward_recovery_speed_mps_0_5s of 6.36 m/s with 68% of forward-ticks above 6 m/s** looks healthy but is an artefact: the class-3 cap is 70% of sprint speed, which for a 9 m/s sprinter is about 6.3 m/s, so the 6 m/s "sprint" threshold sits almost exactly on the cruise cap and cannot tell a capped jog from a sprint (derivation from audit 3317/4117 and the metric definition; the literature's sprint band is 25.2 km/h = 7.0 m/s per [Bortnik et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9536371)). The sim's counter-press window itself is short by the real-football yardstick: Press 150 / Possession 110 / Counter 55 ticks × team work rate / 1000, so **0.7–2.5 s** against the 5–7 s of the sources, and RETREAT hands over to DEFEND after 75 ticks (1.25 s) when real fall-back phases last 18 s.

Second, the avoidance frame is built before CPU-side AI intents, so "on a CPU side avoidance only sees the baseline classes and never class 3" (audit §3, sim.rs:2862 versus 334); the side-flip-by-class split in the baseline is therefore measuring classes the solver did not see on CPU sides. Third, the pocket search flip-flops: candidates 1 and 2 are mirror images at ±2.6 m and the keep-my-pocket bias (+0.9 within 1.5 m) is measured against the lagging smoothed `prev_target`, not the previously chosen candidate, "so the choice can flip sides" (3866-3916); the support-triangle pick is re-made every tick; `yield_target`, `keeper_corridor_yield`, `reserve_destination` (a near-tie swaps who moves "with a jump up to 6 m"), the curved-run `bend_sign`, the second-presser lane and the cutback mark all choose a side from a sign test with no memory; `passer_ready` toggles the run push and class 2/3 on the carrier's facing crossing −0.15; and the chase, receive and contain paths write no target memory, so returning from them snaps the target to `raw` (audit §3). The baseline's smoothness block shows the cost: **side_flips_per_player_min 50.2 (11v11) and 82.7 (5v5)**, **heading_sign_flips 19.6 and 36.1**, and, decisively, the highest per-minute rate is in **class 0 at 185 (11v11) and 177 (5v5)**, with class 1 at 91–97, class 2 at 42–72 and class 3 at 36–46. Class 0 is the carrier, the elected chaser and the committed keeper, none of whom run the sampled avoidance; those flips are decision-side (chase intent re-aimed every tick, CPU dribble input every 5–16 ticks, no target memory). The autopilot preset pushes the controlled side's class-0 rate to **290 per player-minute** (11v11 autopilot home), consistent with the audit's note that the human-controlled player gets `human_adjust`, "no command filter and no realistic acceleration" (sim.rs:3818-3833). Flips above 3 m/s run at **58–108 per player-minute** against 25 below 3 m/s, so this is sprint-speed wobble, not stationary jitter.

Fourth, attacking support is thin and runs never go in behind. The pocket search is "the only lane-aware support logic" and it runs before `support_spacing`, the run push and `reserve_destination`, so "the final target is never re-checked for an open lane"; `passer_ready` "only means the carrier's facing has x × attack_sign > −0.15"; "No run ever goes beyond the offside line, and the number of runners is not capped" (every run target is clamped to `compute_onside_limit_x` − 0.35 m, 3950-3955); `BTN_CALL_RUN` picks the most advanced outfielder with no target, lane or offside logic (audit §1). The baseline: **open_options_mean 3.68, forward_options_mean 0.74, ticks_with_le1_option_pct 20%, runs_in_behind_per_10min 0.83** in 11v11; in 5v5 hard **open_options 1.35, forward_options 0.28 and 53% of carrier ticks with at most one option**; the autopilot away side (CPU facing a human-path team) drops to pass_target_open_pct 50% and 46% of ticks with ≤ 1 option. The nearest open option sits at 12.1–12.7 m, which matches the real 10.9–12.7 m onset of coordinated support ([Matsubara et al. 2022](https://content.scirp.org/pdf/ape_2022053016232546.pdf)); the problem is the count ahead of the ball and the absence of penetration, not the radius.

Fifth, coordination invariants leak on the human side. "Teammate contain can produce three engagers" because "the contain mate is invisible to the team plan" (audit §4, sim.rs:835-866), and auto-switch and press election "use different metrics, causing presser re-election churn". The baseline shows **engagers3_pct_of_carrier_ticks 0** in CPU-only 11v11 but **0.29%** (1.75 ticks/min) in 5v5 autopilot and 0.06% in 11v11 autopilot; **dual_chaser_pct_of_loose 12–16%** everywhere with up to 4–5 simultaneous chasers, against the earlier report's target of zero for the exclusivity invariant; and **cruise_over72_pct about 35%**, meaning a third of the time an eligible "uninvolved" player exceeds 72% of top speed anyway, which with urgency-based recovery should rise during transitions and fall in settled play rather than sit flat.

## Prioritised design for the sim

Everything below is for the deterministic fixed-point 60 Hz sim; tick counts assume 60 Hz; distances are metres unless stated. Evidence labels: **[official]** EA or Konami statements, manuals and patch notes; **[patent]** mechanism documented in a granted patent, shipped use not confirmed; **[peer-reviewed]**; **[code]** open-source reference implementation or library default; **[community]** fan measurement or datamine; **[audit]** internal code audit; **[baseline]** r4 numbers; **[estimate]** my derivation to be tuned against the baseline. Work areas are ordered by payoff per unit of risk and are separately implementable; within each, the first items are the cheapest. A small number of cross-cutting fixes come first because every area depends on them: compute refined move classes before `build_frame` on both sides (or consume the previous tick's refined classes consistently), so avoidance sees class 3 on CPU sides [audit, sim.rs:2862/334]; make every intent path that currently bypasses the low-pass (chase, receive, teammate contain, non-open-play) write `prev_target`/`target_tick` so returning to shape does not snap [audit, 1331-1408, 3543-3559]; and register the teammate-contain mate in `update_team_plan` as the second engager so the two-engager cap can see him [audit, sim.rs:835-866].

### Work area 1: steering oscillation and side flips

The avoidance-side fix is a fifth penalty term and a remembered side, not more samples. Add to the sampler's penalty `fpen = weightFlip · [sign(lat(vcand)) ≠ sign(lat(prev_nvel)) ∧ |lat(prev_nvel)| > ε]`, where `lat()` is the 2-D cross product with the previous heading (integer), with **weightFlip 0.75** (equal to Detour's `weightSide` and `weightCurVel`, below `weightDesVel` 2.0 and `weightToi` 2.5 so safety still wins) and **ε 0.05 m/s** [code for the Detour scale; estimate for the term]; break exact ties lexicographically on (penalty, distance² to `prev_nvel`), never by evaluation order [estimate; ClearPath "side closest to current velocity" [peer-reviewed]]. Store per (agent, neighbour) pair a side bit from HRVO's test `sign(det(p_j − p_i, v_pref_i − v_pref_j))` at first contact [code], penalise candidates whose reciprocal relative velocity `2·vcand − v_i − v_j` would pass on the other side with **weightSideMem 0.75**, resolve exact symmetry by priority class then lower player index (the deterministic replacement for RVO2's random perturbation) [estimate], and clear the bit when OpenSteer's `predictNearestApproachTime` goes negative or after 60 ticks without interaction [code]. Hold the committed lateral sign for a **dwell of 9–12 ticks** (half a stride at 3–9 m/s) with a safety override when time-to-collision falls below 0.3 s or penetration occurs, per Hespanha's caveat [peer-reviewed for the caveat; estimate for the numbers]. Shrink the discrete jumps first, because it is a two-constant change: **velBias 0.5 → 0.6, weightCurVel 0.75 → 1.25**, keeping weightDesVel 2.0; revert weightCurVel first if agents stall [code for the knobs; estimate for the values]. Replace the first-order velocity low-pass (`k = 0.25 + 0.002 × agility`, audit 4307-4329) with a critically damped second-order filter on steering direction, **smoothing time T = 0.15 s** (ω = 2/T ≈ 13.3 rad/s, e^{−ω·dt} ≈ 0.80 at dt = 1/60, well inside the `dt < 0.5T` validity bound), with the four Juckett coefficients precomputed in Q16.16 per (T, dt) so no exp or sqrt runs at 60 Hz; use **T = 0.08–0.10 s for the carrier only** [code for the closed form; estimate for T]. Keep the acceleration and turn-rate caps as hard limits after the filter. For static or slow targets, replace Detour's `2·radius` slow-down with a stopping-distance slow radius **d_slow = v²/(2·a_brake)** (4 m at 8 m/s and 8 m/s²) and a **target radius of 0.4–0.5 m** inside which steering is zero and the agent only decelerates; the sim's arrive dead zone of 160–510 mm by discipline (audit 4046-4062) sits at the low end and doubles only below 0.4 m/s [lecture derivation of Reynolds' arrival; estimate]. Finally, decouple the rendered heading with a **10-sample (≈ 0.17 s) moving average**, Buckland's `NumSamplesForSmoothing 10` [code], so residual sim jitter never reaches the feet, and drive turn poses from the filtered trajectory sampled at +0.33/+0.66/+1.0 s, EA's 1 s trajectory features [official].

The decision-side fixes target the audit's list of sign sources directly, and matter more than the sampler because class 0 carries the highest flip rate. Store the **chosen pocket index**, not the smoothed target, and give the incumbent pocket a **1.15× score bonus and an 18-tick (0.3 s) dwell** unless it becomes invalid (occupied, offside, lane shadowed); the ±2.6 m mirror pair (3866-3916) then cannot alternate on a lagging `prev_target` [audit; utility-AI inertia 1.2× and minimum commit [community practice]; estimate for the values]. Give `designated_support_triangle_idx` (1298-1318) the same incumbent bonus and dwell, and require a challenger to be 2 m closer to the 9.5 m target before taking the role [audit; estimate]. Make every side sign sticky per pair or per episode: `yield_target` (3578-3612) and `keeper_corridor_yield` (4248-4269) keep their sign until the pass is complete or the pair separates by more than the yield radius; the curved-run `bend_sign` (4076-4081) holds for the run; the second-presser lane target and the cutback mark (3686-3695, 3753-3770) keep their nearest-opponent choice unless a challenger is 2 m nearer for 10 ticks [audit; estimate]. Convert `reserve_destination` (4214-4236) from a per-tick distance comparison into an assignment with memory: whoever yielded last tick keeps yielding unless the other is farther by **1.5 m**, and when targets coincide within 0.2 m resolve by the stored assignment rather than index parity [audit; Konami's one-character-per-destination rule ([Google Patents US8616946B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8616946B2/en)) [patent]; estimate for the margin]. Replace the single `passer_ready` threshold (facing x × attack_sign > −0.15) with a **Schmitt pair: on above −0.10, off below −0.30**, held at least 12 ticks, so the run push and class 2/3 do not toggle as the carrier turns [audit 3920-3927; hysteresis sizing [control practice]; estimate for the thresholds]. Filter the moving targets themselves with an **alpha-beta filter, α = 0.2, β = 0.02** at 60 Hz (inside `0 < α < 2, 0 < β < 4 − 2α`), or the same critically damped spring with **T = 0.25 s**, and apply a **re-target deadband of 0.4 m** (one body radius) so a slot that jitters by less than a body does not move the player [peer-reviewed stability region; estimate for gains]. For class 0, give the chase intent (3543-3559) an intercept point that is accepted only if it lies within a few ticks of the previous one, UvA Trilearn's anti-dither rule ([keepaway WorldModelPredict.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tjpalmer/keepaway/master/player/WorldModelPredict.cc)) [code], and give the human-controlled and autopilot player the same turn-rate and acceleration limiter as every other mover [audit sim.rs:3818-3833; EA's own FIFA 14 note that players had been "limited to turns within 23 degrees" shows the controlled player is constrained too ([EA, FIFA 14 Precision Movement](https://www.ea.com/news/fifa-14-precision-movement-first-look)) [official]]. Instrument before tuning: split the flip metric by cause (sampler `nvel` flips while `dvel` steady; `dvel` flips; flips surviving the filter) and add a Schmitt variant of the metric at ±0.05 m/s with a 4-tick dwell alongside the raw 0.02 m/s count, since the raw threshold is below the 0.13 m/s per-tick quantum of an 8 m/s² cap and counts sub-quantum noise [estimate].

### Work area 2: transition urgency and work-rate-driven recovery runs

Replace the flat class-3 cruise during COUNTER_PRESS with a per-player urgency computed from threat, FIFA 10's "relaxed until threatened" [official], and lengthen the windows to the measured football values. At the turnover tick f0, after a **6–12 tick reaction**, decide press-or-fall-back from two local counts: own players versus opponents within **R_press = 10 m** of the ball, and own outfielders behind the ball; counter-press only if own ≥ opponents within 10 m **and** behind-ball ≥ 4 (11v11; 2 in 5v5) or will be within 60 ticks at sprint [peer-reviewed thresholds; 5v5 scaling estimate]. Expect this to fire on **25–35%** of turnovers; the current gate (own within 12 m ≥ theirs, progress > 35%, team work rate > 450) is in the same spirit but the baseline's regain-within-6 s rate of 45% in 11v11 is well above the real 30–36%, so tighten the superiority condition if the trigger rate stays high [peer-reviewed; baseline]. Set **COUNTER_PRESS to 300 ticks** (5 s) with a style factor (Press 1.2, Possession 1.0, Counter 0.6) scaled by team work rate and a floor of 180 ticks, replacing the 40–150 tick `cp_window` [peer-reviewed 5 s; official 7 s; estimate]; exit early on regain, ball out of play, or when the ball leaves the pressing cluster by a pass longer than about 16 m, the length that beats a counter-press [peer-reviewed]. Let **RECOVER/RETREAT run until shape is reached or 540–600 ticks** (9–10 s), not 75 ticks [peer-reviewed; audit]. Charge stamina at **×2.2–3** while the counter-press window is active so it self-limits [official FIFA 21].

Inside the window, split the team three ways. The presser is the elected chaser at sprint; **two lane-closers** (the counter-press second presser already exists at 45% along the carrier→nearest-opponent line) take the nearest points on the two best opponent lanes at the high-speed band [official eFootball tiers; peer-reviewed lane variant; count estimate]. Everyone else runs the recovery rule by deficit **d = (x_player − x_ball) × attack_sign**: if d > 0 (ahead of the ball) sprint goal-side until d ≤ −3 m or until behind-ball ≥ 4 and the rest-defence line is set, capping each burst at **180–240 ticks** (3–4 s, about 26 m) before dropping to the high-speed band; if d ≤ 0 but more than **10–12 m** from the defensive slot, high-speed band (5.5–7 m/s); only players goal-side and inside the slot radius cruise at 70%, the fraction Konami states for uninvolved AI players ([Konami eFootball v5.0.0](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en-us/topic/news/3585)) [peer-reviewed burst length; official 70%; radii estimate]. This requires that the COUNTER_PRESS branch at ai.rs:3732 stops skipping the non-engager refinements, that `force_sprint` applies to any player ahead of the ball in any defensive phase, and that such a player is class 2 (triggered), never class 3 [audit]. Fix the inverted recovery threshold: measure the deficit as **ticks to the slot** against a constant **30–45 tick** threshold rather than a distance that grows with the player's own speed [audit 3792-3800; estimate]. Multiply burst cap and speed band by **0.5–0.8** for players whose high-intensity distance in the last 60 s exceeds a team-typical peak (about 70 m), the measured 20–53% post-peak drop [peer-reviewed].

Forwards get the profile the data describe: the forward nearest the ball joins the press as a lane-closer; other forwards drop at high speed only until level with the ball or to a **holding line about 10 m goal-side of the ball's x**, then stop and shadow the nearest passing lane in 1–2 s closing bursts of about 15 m [peer-reviewed; estimate for the line]. Add a per-player **defensive work rate (Low/Medium/High)**, FIFA 11's attribute [official], which (a) gates whether the player is ever marked "threatened" on a turnover and (b) sets how deep his defensive slot sits (High: own third and box; Medium: midfield to the edge of the own box; Low: no deeper than the attacking-midfield zone, per the FIFA 23 fan description ([FIFAUTeam, FIFA 23 Work Rates](https://fifauteam.com/fifa-23-work-rates/)) [community]); replace the 11v11 striker slot pinned at ball + 6 m (2737-2740) with a DWR-dependent ball-relative depth, and allow Medium/High-DWR Striker, Pivo and Winger slots to mark or screen a lane (lift the exclusion at 3200) [audit]. Keep exactly **one Low-DWR forward high** as the rest offence by default so `forwards_staying_high_mean` should land near 1, not 2 [official Berbatov example; baseline 2.05; estimate for the target]. Rest defence while attacking should be **3–4 players** (CBs plus one FB/CM by slot) holding a line **5–10 m goal-side of the deepest opposing forward**, **about 28 m wide**, never further than about **45 m** from the own goal line when the ball is in the final third, with loose marks of about **5 m** tightening to 2–3 m as the ball comes within 20 m or carrier pressure rises; these players never join the counter-press [peer-reviewed geometry; estimate for the 5–10 m offset]. Give the presser a **zone handover**: release him when the carrier moves more than a zone width (about 12 m laterally or beyond his line) from his slot and let the nearest player in the new zone take over, instead of following across the pitch [official FIFA 12 Barça rule; estimate for the width]. Konami's score-aware refinements, a contain-before-close timer that lengthens when leading and a no-press strip near the opponent's goal line with a 10 s timeout, are optional later additions [patent].

### Work area 3: off-ball attacking support and runs

Replace the five fixed pocket offsets with a candidate set scored by ownership, lane and value, re-evaluated at a fixed cadence with commitment. Per supporter build **6–8 candidates**: the ball-attracted formation slot; PES-style ring points at **L and L√2** around the carrier with L set by a Support Range style parameter (**default 14 m**, diamond points at 14 and 20 m: ahead, left, right, behind) [patent for the ring; peer-reviewed 13–24 m support band; L estimate]; the nearest Voronoi vertex of opponent positions within 25 m; and the point on the offside line nearest the slot [code, Gliders2d; patent, EA]. Reject candidates within **10 m** of another teammate's reserved destination unless it is the player's own slot (real play rarely has three supporters inside 10 m) [peer-reviewed]. Score each as **Control × Value × LaneOpen**: Control from a time-to-intercept race for the supporter and every opponent with **t = 0.7 s + d / 5 m/s** (fixed-point LUT), logistic **σ = 0.45 s**, control rate **λ = 4.3 s⁻¹ for attackers and ×1.72 for defenders**, zero if offside [peer-reviewed; reference implementation [code]]; Value as forward progress plus a negative-exponential distance-to-goal curve (**0.93·exp(−0.14·d) + 0.02** as the starting shape) or a coarse 25×16 xT table [peer-reviewed]; LaneOpen as the fraction of **5 pass speeds × 5 angles within ±16°** of the lane that reach the supporter first, and no defender within **4.6 m** of the supporter at arrival unless Value is very high [peer-reviewed window and pressure radius; sampling counts estimate]. Re-score every **15–30 ticks** (half a lane lifetime), switch only if the new score beats the current by **1.15×**, commit to a new run for at least **60 ticks**, and evaluate candidates at the supporter's **1 s look-ahead position when moving faster than 2 m/s** [peer-reviewed lane lifetime and look-ahead; margins estimate]. Re-check the lane on the **final** target after `support_spacing`, `reserve_destination` and the run push, since today the only lane check runs first and is then overridden [audit §1]. Keep the GRF-style spacing (six nearest teammates, weight 0.4, 10.5 m scale) as the fallback for players with no scored candidate ([gfootball elizacontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp)) [code].

Runs need a trigger, a cap, a target beyond the line and a release rule. Trigger a run-in-behind when the carrier faces forward (the Schmitt `passer_ready` of area 1), the carrier is under no more than moderate pressure (no defender within **4.6 m**, or the sim's existing 1.85 m "tight" radius for the CPU carrier), the runner is onside, and his 1 s look-ahead point has **Control > 0.5** [peer-reviewed thresholds; estimate]. Cap simultaneous runners by role, mirroring Queen/Knight/Bishop: **one penetrate runner and one support-from-behind** at a time, assigned by one arbiter (highest score takes penetrate, second takes support), plus up to **three box runners on a cross** (near post, back post, centre, FIFA 09's own description) [code; official]. Pick the run end-point the FIFA way, the owned point beyond the offside line (a Voronoi vertex the runner reaches first) [patent], but hold the runner's **position** at the onside limit −0.35 m until a release event: our pass launched toward him (the existing `run_boost` 45 on a through-ball receiver), or, for the CPU carrier, the tick its pass decision selects him; at the line he "stutters" at ≤ 3 m/s rather than reversing, FIFA 13's stated fix [official; estimate for the speed]. Make the CPU carrier's pass scorer (1953-2127) evaluate committed runners at their **post-release look-ahead** rather than only current position plus a 5.2 m lead, so pass choice and run commit together; offside is judged at the kick, so a runner at −0.35 m who sprints on release is legal [audit; patent model]. Allow a **decoy** whose own Control is low if his marker (a defender within 3 m moving with him) would raise a teammate's Control by more than 0.1, and let the decoy reserve his destination so no second runner takes the vacated lane [peer-reviewed space-generation; estimate]. Gate run consideration by an **attacking work rate** (High: every re-score; Medium: every second; Low: every fourth), FIFA's "how often the player considers making forward runs" [official; cadence estimate], and expose Build Up Speed, Support Range, CC Passing risk and Crossing count as weights on urgency, L, the Control/Value trade-off and the box-runner cap [official slider semantics]. Rework `BTN_CALL_RUN` to pick the teammate the carrier is **facing** (FIFA's L1 rule) with the best look-ahead Control, give him an actual end-point and the same hold-and-release logic, and keep the 90-tick commit [official; audit sim.rs:2832-2854]. Scripted group patterns (Wing Rotation, overlap when the winger comes inside, False 9 drop-and-fill) are the PES layer to add after the scorer works [official text].

### Work area 4: off-ball defending

Four changes, in order of cheapness. First, cover shadows: angle the presser so he stands between the ball and the goal **and** cuts the most dangerous lane, shifting his point laterally toward the best opponent passing lane by up to **0.5–1 m** (FIFA 12's "show infield", Konami's intercept line, Pressing Intensity's lane variant) [official; patent; peer-reviewed; shift estimate]; give zonal holders without a mark a lane-blocking role when within about **12 m** of the ball (stand on the nearest point of the best opponent lane within a **3 m leash** of the slot) and a line-pushing role when farther, eFootball's distance-based split [official; distances estimate]. Second, marking: replace nearest-opponent greedy assignment with **danger-ranked assignment by one arbiter** (danger = Value × Control of the opponent's 1 s look-ahead point), with an incumbent bonus of 3 m and a dwell of **20–28 ticks**, processing incumbents before challengers so a mark can no longer be stolen within a tick through `taken[]` (3225-3235) [official FIFA 14 "most dangerous… multiple frames"; audit; values estimate]. Add explicit handover for a runner in behind: the back-liner who owns him tracks him regardless of the block anchor until a deeper teammate lies within **La ≈ 3 m** of the runner-to-goal line, Konami's cover check; switch a marker from goal-side shadowing to the intercept line only when the carrier faces the marked man within **θa ≈ 25°** and that cover exists [patent; θa, La estimate since the patent leaves them unspecified]. Third, the line: hold **one shared line value per team** that back-liners track with a small lag, replacing the independent per-player judgement noise (3737-3745) that leaves "no shared line value" [audit; estimate]; couple it to a run-tracking policy by style using FC 25's bands (Deep: drop with any runner in behind; Balanced: drop only when the runner's look-ahead beats the nearest cover; High: rarely; Aggressive: never drop, step up to the highest legal line when the carrier is pressured or facing back) [official bands; trigger estimate]; keep the existing drop of 3.5 m when the carrier is unpressured and facing goal and the −0.8 m offside tie; under a Cover setting let the ball-far full-back tuck inside when a CB is beaten [community description of FIFA's Cover]. Fourth, second-man coordination: register the human's contain mate in the plan as the second engager (excluded from presser, cover and mark election), demote the AI presser to cover while contain is held, enforce the two-engager cap including him, make the second man a shadow at **3–4 m** behind-side of the presser rather than a tackler, never pull a centre-back who currently owns a runner, and prefer a midfielder as presser in the middle third [official FIFA 12–13; audit; distance estimate]. Add press triggers that fire the sprint and the second man outside counter-press: heavy touch, carrier facing his own goal, carrier within 5 m of the touchline (FIFA 19's "Pressure on Heavy Touch" style presses "when the opposition takes a heavy touch, it's a loose ball, or controlling a difficult pass facing their own goal", [FIFPlay, FIFA 19 Tactics](https://www.fifplay.com/fifa-19-tactics/)) [official; touchline radius estimate].

### Work area 5: what to measure with the existing psfc-replay analyze metrics

The r4 harness already covers each area, and most of the work is reading the right metric with the right threshold. For steering, `move.side_flips_per_player_min` with its `by_class` and `speed_lt3/ge3` splits is the primary gate, read **per class** so that a decision-side fix (class 0) and a sampler fix (class 2) are credited separately; `move.heading_sign_flips_per_player_min` is the second gate because it ignores the 0.02 m/s dead band; `speed_oscillation_pct`, `accel_gt10_pct`, `heading_change_p95_deg_per_tick_gt3mps` and `turn_rate_mean_deg_per_s` guard against over-damping; `collision.any.ticks_per_min`, `teammate.ticks_per_min` and `correction_vel_corrected_p99_mps` guard against dwell times holding a side into contact. The class split is only meaningful once the avoidance frame sees refined classes on CPU sides, so the cross-cutting ordering fix must land first and be noted in the before/after. For transitions, `trans.forwards_staying_high_mean`, `pct_outfield_behind_ball_at_3s/_at_6s`, `time_to_shape_static_s` (the AI-independent variant) and `in_shape_at_turnover_pct` are the gates; `forward_sprint_share_0_5s` and `mid_sprint_share_0_5s` should be re-run with the threshold moved from 6 m/s to **7.0 m/s or 0.85 × top speed**, because 6 m/s coincides with the 70% cruise cap and currently reports capped jogging as sprinting; `counterpress_pct` is a regain rate, not a trigger rate, and should be compared with the peer-reviewed 30–36%; `coord.cruise_over72_pct` should be read as a lower bound that is expected to rise in the first five seconds after a turnover. For support, `support.open_options_mean`, `forward_options_mean`, `ticks_with_le1_option_pct`, `nearest_option_dist_m`, `pass_target_open_pct`, `cluster_pct` and `runs_in_behind_per_10min` are the gates, with `match.offsides` and `match.passes_completed_pct` as the cost side, and the autopilot presets as the human-side check since the away side there shows the worst option counts. For defending, `coord.engagers3_*`, `dual_chaser_*`, `max_simultaneous_chasers`, `shape.defending.line_x_std_m`, `swarm2_pct/swarm3_pct` and `pct_outfield_behind_ball` are the gates. Three additions would close real blind spots and are small: a side-flip split by cause (sampler versus desired-velocity versus post-filter) and a Schmitt-band variant; a **mark-steal rate** (mark target changes per defender-minute while the previous target is still within 1.35× the zone) and a **run-abandon rate** (committed runs ended before 60 ticks for a reason other than possession loss or a stoppage), the two FIFA 12 failure modes EA described; and a per-phase split of `cruise_over72_pct` (first 5 s of a transition versus settled defence). Every change must be reported against `docs/match-analytics-baseline-r4.json` with the three seeds, with final hashes expected to change and a second run proving they are stable.

## Conclusion

The research reframes "teammate intelligence" as two cheap properties rather than one expensive one. Neither EA nor Konami runs a sophisticated planner for the ten players off the ball; both run a space-ownership race and a handful of role slots, and both discovered, publicly and painfully, that the quality players perceive comes from **commitment** (FIFA 13's "he sticks with it", B-Human's captain, PES's formation leash) and **urgency** (FIFA 10's threat state, FIFA 11's work rates, the five-second counter-press that coaches, vendors and tracking studies all converge on). The sim already has the ownership skeleton and the exclusivity arbiter from the previous round; what it lacks is memory in its decisions and a reason for anyone who is not the presser to hurry. That is why the side-flip problem and the jogging-forward problem turn out to be the same engineering problem viewed from two sides: a system that re-decides every tick without inertia both wobbles and never commits to a 26 m sprint.

The second shift is in what the numbers mean. The baseline's apparently healthy forward recovery speed is the 70% cruise cap in disguise, the class-0 flip rate shows the biggest oscillation source sits outside the avoidance solver entirely, and the autopilot presets are where the human-side invariants break. Those three readings should change how future rounds are judged: measure with thresholds that separate cruise from sprint, credit fixes by class and by phase, and treat the autopilot runs as the acceptance preset for anything that touches the controlled player's teammates. What remains open is calibration, not architecture: no franchise publishes a support radius, a dwell time or a line-height in metres, so every value above that is not peer-reviewed or shipped code is a starting point for the before/after loop against the r4 baseline, which is exactly the loop the project has already built.
