## Key Question 1 — RoboCup 2D: how top teams position players (SBSP, Delaunay formations, Voronoi, role assignment, fastest-to-ball arbitration)

### Takeaway
RoboCup 2D teams separate (a) a *strategic/formation layer* that maps the (predicted) ball position to a home position per role — FC Portugal's SBSP (situation-specific formations + ball-dependent offsets), HELIOS's Delaunay-triangulation lookup table, and Voronoi-vertex refinements (Gliders2d, AUT MasterMinds, Kaden et al.) — from (b) an *active layer* where exactly one player intercepts, chosen by comparing simulated cycles-to-intercept for self, fastest teammate and fastest opponent with explicit margins (agent2d: `self_min <= mate_min && self_min < opp_min + 3`). No learned model is needed at runtime; everything is table lookup, integer cycle counts and comparisons.

### Cited Findings

**HELIOS / agent2d / librcsc (Akiyama & Noda) — Delaunay formation**
- "An input value of our positioning mechanism is a focal point on the soccer field, usually the ball position"; the field is split into triangles and output positions are interpolated inside each triangle; the 2009/2010 versions use *Constrained* Delaunay Triangulation with constrained edges — [HELIOS 2010 TDP](https://wrighteagle2d.github.io/robocup/2010/2D_TDP_HELIOS.pdf), [HELIOS 2009 TDP](https://wrighteagle2d.github.io/robocup/2009/helios-rc09-tdp.pdf), [HELIOS 2008 TDP](https://wrighteagle2d.github.io/robocup/2008/2D_TDP_helios.pdf) (search excerpts; PDFs not fetchable from this session).
- Delaunay was chosen because it "maximizes the minimum angle of all the angles of the triangles in the triangulation", giving well-shaped, stable interpolation regions — [HELIOS 2008 TDP](https://wrighteagle2d.github.io/robocup/2008/2D_TDP_helios.pdf).
- Interpolation uses a shading-style linear scheme borrowed from computer graphics (Gouraud-type) — [Akiyama & Noda, JSAI 2007](https://www.ai-gakkai.or.jp/jsai2007/data/pdf/100194.pdf); a 2008 Springer chapter compared the method against a Normalized Gaussian Network and reported robustness to unevenly distributed training samples — [Springer chapter](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-68847-1_38); a 2007 JACIII paper on learning positioning from human instruction found Delaunay + linear interpolation performed best among tested models — [Fuji Press JACIII](https://www.fujipress.jp/jaciii/jc/jacii001100080998/).
- Actual librcsc implementation (`rcsc/formation/formation_dt.cpp`, method name `"DelaunayTriangulation"`): the triangulation is built once over a rectangle with origin (-60,-45) and size 120×90; each training sample's ball position becomes a vertex and the 11 player positions for that sample are stored with the vertex. `getPositions(focus)` locates the triangle containing the focus (ball) point *once*, then for each of players 1–11 (a) draws the line from vertex 0 through the focus to its intersection with the opposite edge (v1–v2), (b) linearly interpolates the stored positions of v1 and v2 by distance along that edge, then (c) linearly interpolates between v0's stored position and that edge result by the distances v0→focus and focus→intersection (i.e. barycentric interpolation). Degenerate tolerance: squared distance 1.0e-5; fallback = average of the three vertex positions. If the focus is outside the triangulation, the *nearest vertex's* stored position is returned with no blending — [librcsc formation_dt.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/helios-base/librcsc/master/rcsc/formation/formation_dt.cpp).
- Formation switching in agent2d `strategy.cpp` (`updateSituation`): during PlayOn, with `self_min`, `mate_min`, `opp_min` = cycles-to-intercept and `our_min = min(self_min, mate_min)`: `opp_min <= our_min - 2` → Defense_Situation; `our_min <= opp_min - 2` → Offense_Situation; otherwise Normal_Situation (margin of 2 cycles). Separate formations exist for offense/defense/normal, our/opp kick-in & corner, our/opp indirect free kick, goal kick, before kick-off, and set plays; fouls near the penalty area (ball x inside penalty line + 1.0, |y| < half-width + 1.0) switch to the indirect-free-kick formation — [agent2d strategy.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/helios-base/helios-base/master/src/player/strategy.cpp).
- Formation focal point is the *predicted* ball position: `ball_step = min(teammate, opponent, self intercept steps)` (capped at 1000) in PlayOn/GoalKick modes, and the ball's inertia-point after `ball_step` cycles is passed to `getPositions`. Offside clamp: `max_x = offsideLineX`; if the teammate's intercept step < 50, `max_x = max(max_x, predicted ball x at that step)`; then `max_x -= 1.0`; every home position with x > max_x is clamped to max_x. Role numbers map 1:1 to uniform numbers initially; `exchangeRole(unum0, unum1)` swaps two roles; roles 2–5 are "marker type" — [agent2d strategy.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/helios-base/helios-base/master/src/player/strategy.cpp).

**agent2d ball-chasing arbitration (the canonical "only one player goes")**
- `Bhv_BasicMove`: first tries `Bhv_BasicTackle(0.8, 80.0)`; then reads `self_min = wm.interceptTable()->selfStep()`, `mate_min = teammateStep()`, `opp_min = opponentStep()`; it intercepts iff **no teammate is already kickable** and (`self_min <= 3` OR (`self_min <= mate_min` AND `self_min < opp_min + 3`)); otherwise it goes to `Strategy::i().getPosition(unum)` with arrival tolerance `dist_thr = max(1.0, 0.1 × distance-to-ball)` and dash power from `Strategy::get_normal_dash_power`. Neck: turn to ball if an opponent is kickable and ball within 18.0 m — [agent2d bhv_basic_move.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/helios-base/helios-base/master/src/player/bhv_basic_move.cpp).
- Because every agent evaluates the same world-model intercept table (self vs. fastest teammate), the comparison is symmetric: only the agent whose cycle count is minimal (ties → self, hence occasional double-chase when counts are equal) commits to intercept; the `opp_min + 3` margin lets a player still chase a ball an opponent will reach slightly earlier (to pressure) — [agent2d bhv_basic_move.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/helios-base/helios-base/master/src/player/bhv_basic_move.cpp).

**UvA Trilearn interception machinery (used verbatim by many 2D teams; keepaway code is a direct derivative)**
- 2003 TDP: the intercept skill "also takes opponents into account to determine the best interception point" and "computes the interception time of an agent to the ball with a numerical algorithm" — [UvA Trilearn 2003 TDP](https://wrighteagle2d.github.io/robocup/2003/kok03rcteam.pdf) (search excerpt).
- `createInterceptFeatures()`: for `iCycles = 0 .. PlayerWhenToIntercept`, predict ball position after `iCycles`; a player is a candidate only if `dist(player, posBall)/PlayerSpeedMax < iCycles + 1` (cheap straight-line pre-filter); candidates are scored with `predictNrCyclesToPoint`; the loop records fastest player, fastest teammate, fastest teammate **without goalie**, fastest opponent, and self (`FEATURE_INTERCEPT_CYCLES_ME`), sentinel 100; opponent search horizon default 30 cycles — [keepaway WorldModelHighLevel.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tjpalmer/keepaway/master/player/WorldModelHighLevel.cc).
- `getFastestInSetTo(ball, OBJECT_SET_PLAYERS)` returns the faster of fastest opponent and fastest teammate (`if (iCyclesFastestOpp < iCyclesFastestTeam)`); point-target variant advances the target by `pos += vel; vel *= decay` per cycle and stops when `|vel| < EPSILON` or 100 cycles — [keepaway WorldModelHighLevel.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tjpalmer/keepaway/master/player/WorldModelHighLevel.cc).
- `predictNrCyclesToPoint`: returns 0 if already kickable; counts turn commands as cycles (`while (cmd == CMD_TURN || (|ang| > 20 && power < 0))`); then `predictNrCyclesForDistance`: `while (dist > minDist && (|speed - prevSpeed| > EPS || speed < 0.3) && cycles < 40) { speed += maxPower*dashRate; speed = min(speed, speedMax); dist = max(0, dist - speed); speed *= decay; cycles++ }`, then `cycles += ceil((dist - minDist)/speed)` for the remainder at terminal speed. Turn-threshold ratio `dRatioTurn` = 2.0 (>10 m), 3.0 (>20 m), 0.90 otherwise, max 5 turns. Ball prediction uses the geometric series `dist = sum(v·decay^k, k=0..n)`, `vel *= decay^n` — [keepaway WorldModelPredict.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tjpalmer/keepaway/master/player/WorldModelPredict.cc).
- Interception-point hysteresis: `predictCommandToInterceptBall` prefers the *previous* interception point and only accepts a new one if `iCyclesBall <= iMinCyclesBall + 3`; a "safer" point must satisfy `dist(me, ball) < 0.70 × maxKickDist`, ball speed > 0.6, `|y| < 32`, `|x| < 50`, `interceptDistance > 0.50 × maxKickDist` — [keepaway WorldModelPredict.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tjpalmer/keepaway/master/player/WorldModelPredict.cc).
- Kok, Spaan & Vlassis (2005) coordination graphs: roles are assigned first, then coordinated; "the coordination graph allows an agent to predict the optimal action of its neighboring agents, making communication unnecessary"; 2005 used max-plus as an approximate alternative to variable elimination — [Kok05roas (TU Delft mirror)](https://www.st.ewi.tudelft.nl/mtjspaan/pub/Kok05roas.pdf), [UvA Trilearn 2004 TDP](https://wrighteagle2d.github.io/robocup/2004/TDP_2D_UvA_Trilearn.pdf) (search excerpts).

**FC Portugal — Situation Based Strategic Positioning (SBSP) and DPRE**
- "One of the main strengths of FC Portugal is its positioning mechanism based on the clear distinction between strategic and active situations"; "If an agent is not involved in an active situation then it tries to occupy its strategic positioning that change according to the situation of the game. Situation is a concept on a high-level analysis of the game (attacking or defending for example)" — [FC Portugal TDP, Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-45324-5_2.pdf) (search excerpt).
- SBSP defines "the positioning of all agents which is the function of the actual ball position and velocity, game situation and player type"; formations "are used for different situations and assign each agent a default spatial positioning and an agent type"; the original paper is Reis, Lau & Oliveira, "Situation Based Strategic Positioning for Coordinating a Team of Homogeneous Agents" (LNCS, DOI 10.1007/3-540-44568-4_11), whose abstract ties SBSP to "a flexible common team strategy and dynamic positioning with role exchange" (DPRE) — [Springer SBSP paper](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2F3-540-44568-4_11.pdf), [U.Porto repository record](https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/handle/10216/443?mode=full) (search excerpts).
- The team using SBSP won RoboCup 2000 (European and World) "scoring a total of 180 goals and conceding none", and later papers state SBSP "is now used directly or as the base for the positioning systems of many simulated soccer teams" — [FC Portugal 2005/2006 TDPs](https://wrighteagle2d.github.io/robocup/2006/2D_TDP_FCPortugal.pdf) (search excerpt).
- CMUnited-98 "SPAR" (Strategic Positioning using Attraction and Repulsion), the main pre-SBSP alternative: a multi-objective function with attraction (ball, goal) and repulsion (opponents, teammates) points, "subject to several constraints" (stay in role's region, within a distance of home position, onside) — [CMU SPAR page](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/usr/pstone/public/papers/98springer/final-champ/node18.html) (search excerpt).
- 2024 SSL TDP (TurtleRabbit) describes the same pattern still in use: "for each formation, a dynamically calculated 'home' position for each robot, dependent on ball position and player role", where "the roles influence how much the robot position will change with the position of the ball" — [TurtleRabbit 2024 TDP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.08205) (search excerpt).

**Voronoi-based positioning (space-finding for off-ball attackers)**
- Gliders2d v1.4 "adjusts the positioning scheme by selecting points according to suitably constructed Voronoi diagrams"; candidate points are chosen among Voronoi vertices; reported goal difference vs agent2d rose from 1.616 to 2.387 — [Gliders2d arXiv 1812.10202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.10202) (search excerpt).
- AUT MasterMinds 2013 applies Voronoi-diagram vertices to offensive positioning without the ball — [AUT MasterMinds 2013 TDP](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/a.visser/activities/robocup/RoboCup2013/Symposium/TeamDescriptionPapers/SoccerSimulation/Soccer2D/TDP_AUT%20Masterminds.pdf) (search excerpt).
- Dashti et al. (2006) "use Voronoi cells to position and distribute players in the field for RoboCup 2D SSL"; each agent computes its own cell and moves toward it — cited in [Ulusoy & Sariel-Talay, ICAART 2012](https://web.itu.edu.tr/~sariel/publications/OnuralpICAART2012.pdf) (search excerpt).
- Kaden et al., "Voronoi Based Strategic Positioning for Robot Soccer" (Humboldt Univ. Berlin) notes HELIOS encodes player positions as a function of ball position over a Delaunay triangulation of the field, and proposes Voronoi-based strategic positioning — [CEUR Vol-1032 paper 23](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1032/paper-23.pdf), [Kaden 2013 thesis](https://www2.informatik.hu-berlin.de/~naoth/docs/theses/2013-bachelorarbeit-kaden.pdf) (search excerpts).

**CYRUS (RoboCup 2021 champion) — unmarking**
- CYRUS splits "unmarking" (off-ball movement to escape marking and receive a pass) into *decisioning* (which teammate is the likely passer) and *positioning*; the 2022 TDP adds a pass-prediction DNN to pick the sender; its 2014 TDP stresses that "at any moment one player has the ball and the other 21 do not", so off-ball movement dominates; Cyrus2D base = Helios + Gliders bases + CYRUS 2021 strategies — [CYRUS 2022 TDP arXiv 2205.10953](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.10953v1.pdf), [CYRUS 2014 TDP](https://wrighteagle2d.github.io/robocup/2014/TDP_CYRUS_2D_2014OK.pdf), [CYRUS 2024 arXiv 2401.03406](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.03406v1) (search excerpts).

### Inferences
- The agent2d pattern is directly portable to a deterministic 60 Hz fixed-point sim: compute an integer `ticksToBall[i]` for every player with a closed-form or table-based speed model (UvA's loop is pure integer arithmetic if speeds/decays are fixed-point), then each off-ball player goes to its formation slot unless `self <= 3 || (self <= bestMate && self < bestOpp + 3)`; ties should be broken by a stable key (shirt number or current chaser) to avoid two chasers — the `kickableTeammate()` check is the second safeguard.
- UvA's "accept new intercept point only if within +3 cycles of the previous one" is an explicit hysteresis that prevents a chaser from flip-flopping between interception targets; combined with the `+3` margins it is the 2D league's standard anti-dithering.
- HELIOS's Delaunay table is a pure lookup: given a ball cell and barycentric weights, each player's target is a weighted sum of three stored 11-vectors. In fixed point, weights can be computed with integer cross products; the "outside triangulation → nearest vertex" fallback and "ball predicted to interception step" are the two important details for stability.
- The agent2d 2-cycle situation margin plus separate offense/defense/normal formations is the simplest documented way to get a whole-team "transition" without a learned model.
- Voronoi vertices are the natural deterministic candidate set for "find space"; both Gliders2d and AUT MasterMinds pick among Voronoi vertices of (mostly opponent) positions, then score them (passability, distance to goal). This can be done at low frequency (e.g., every 10–20 ticks) for only the 2–3 attackers.

## Key Question 2 — RoboCup 3D / humanoid / SPL: dynamic role switching, priority to ball, inter-robot distance rules and local avoidance (UT Austin Villa, B-Human, NimbRo)

### Takeaway
UT Austin Villa's documented system is "formation = fixed offsets from the ball, assignment = minimise the *maximum* distance any robot travels (MMDR), tie-break recursively", which they prove gives collision-free straight-line paths and dynamic consistency; SCRAM (AAAI-15) made this polynomial (O(n^3)–O(n^5)) and it beat static assignment in both 2D and 3D. The public 3D release ships a simpler greedy assignment plus a cone-based local avoidance routine (90° cone, 1 m proximity, 0.5 m collision thresholds). SPL/humanoid teams use "closest/fastest robot plays the ball, with a time bonus for the incumbent" and request-based hand-overs between defender and striker.

### Cited Findings

**UT Austin Villa — Positioning to Win (2011) and SCRAM (2015)**
- "Positions within a formation are determined by predefined offsets from the ball"; "each agent is assigned to a role so as to minimize the longest distance any agent has to travel"; dynamic programming computes the optimal mapping; agents synchronise their choice "through a simple voting system and the simulator's communication channel"; the brute force n! search is reduced to n·2^(n−1) by building mappings from subset mappings; the onBall role goes to the player closest to the ball; "the goalie positions itself independently of teammates so it can dive and stop shots", and if the goalie takes the onBall role "a third role is added"; from 2013 the team used SCRAM — [UT Austin Villa positioning page](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~AustinVilla/sim/3dsimulation/AustinVilla3DSimulationFiles/2011/html/positioning.html), [LNAI12 MacAlpine details](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~AustinVilla/details/LNAI12-MacAlpine.html), [Positioning to Win PDF (UCF mirror)](https://www.eecs.ucf.edu/~gitars/cap6671/Papers/macalpine.pdf) (search excerpts).
- SCRAM (MacAlpine, Price & Stone, AAAI 2015) assigns interchangeable robots to targets "to minimize the time until every robot arrives while avoiding collisions"; two role-assignment functions, MMDR and MMD+MSD2, have polynomial-time implementations; MMDR (Minimal Maximum Distance Recursive) is the lexicographic bottleneck assignment problem; MMD+MSD2 runs in O(n^3) time and O(n^2) space; the poster's table marks MMDR as *dynamic and consistent*, which MMD+MSD2 is not; SCRAM scales to thousands of agents and beat static assignment in both RoboCup 2D and 3D — [AAAI SCRAM paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9424), [SCRAM poster](https://cs.utexas.edu/~AustinVilla/sim/3dsimulation/AustinVilla3DSimulationFiles/2013/files/scram/poster.pdf), [AAMAS14 MacAlpine](https://cs.utexas.edu/~AustinVilla/sim/3dsimulation/AustinVilla3DSimulationFiles/2013/papers/AAMAS14-MacAlpine.pdf) (search excerpts).
- Independent re-implementation (SCRAMJulia) summarises the algorithms: MMDR O(n^5) "converts the bottleneck assignment problem into a standard linear assignment problem by scaling edge weights to powers of 2" (needs 64-bit integers inside the Hungarian algorithm); MMDR O(n^4) uses Ford-Fulkerson (BFS) + Hungarian to find the bottleneck edge, using dual potentials to remove "tight edges" and shrink the problem iteratively; MMD+MSD2 O(n^3) "first finds the bottleneck using Ford-Fulkerson, then applies a 'Soft-Infinity' penalty to edges exceeding the bottleneck" and solves min-sum-of-squared-distances; stated objectives: "Minimizing the maximum distance any single agent must travel" and "Producing a valid assignment that naturally prevents trajectory intersections" — [SCRAMJulia README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opeanc/SCRAMJulia/master/README.md).
- Public 3D code release (`behaviors/simplesoccer.cc`, "a basic formation and dynamic greedy role assignment"): role targets are offsets from the (field-clamped) ball — ON_BALL (0,0); SUPPORTER (−2,0); BACK_LEFT (−10,+3); BACK_RIGHT (−10,−3); MID_LEFT (−1,+2); MID_RIGHT (−1,−2); WING_LEFT (0,+7); WING_RIGHT (0,−7); FORWARD_LEFT (+5,+3); FORWARD_RIGHT (+5,−3); GOALIE fixed at (−HALF_FIELD_X+0.5, 0). ON_BALL = player with smallest straight-line distance to the ball (strict `<`, scanned in ascending uNum, so ties go to the lower number); GOALIE = uNum 1 unless it is closest to the ball. Greedy assignment: build all (distance(agent, roleTarget), (agent, role)) tuples, sort lexicographically, walk the list assigning any pair whose agent and role are both free. Recomputed every cycle, no hysteresis. Off-ball players call `goToSpace(target)`: if within `SPACE_THRESH = 0.5` m they stop and face the ball (`POSITION_CENTER_THRESH = 10°`), else `collisionAvoidance(true, false, true, 1, .5, space, true)` then `goToTarget` — [utaustinvilla3d simplesoccer.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LARG/utaustinvilla3d/master/behaviors/simplesoccer.cc), [utaustinvilla3d DOCUMENTATION.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LARG/utaustinvilla3d/master/DOCUMENTATION.md).
- `NaoBehavior::collisionAvoidance(avoidTeammate, avoidOpponent, avoidBall, PROXIMITY_THRESH, COLLISION_THRESH, target, fKeepDistance)`: an obstacle counts only if it lies inside a 90° cone toward the target (`|angle(target, obstacle)| < 90`) and (unless `fKeepDistance`) is nearer than the target; the nearest qualifying teammate/ball is taken; opponents are considered only if no closer obstacle is within PROXIMITY_THRESH. If the closest obstacle is within PROXIMITY_THRESH, `collisionAvoidanceCorrection` builds two points 1.0 m perpendicular to the obstacle direction (cross product with Z) and picks the one closer to the target; if the obstacle is within COLLISION_THRESH it additionally backs away 1.0 m (`target += (start-obstacle).normalize()*1.0`). `collisionAvoidanceApproach` projects the obstacle onto the path and, if the path passes within PROXIMITY_THRESH, moves the target to obstacle + PROXIMITY_THRESH away from the path — [utaustinvilla3d naobehavior.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LARG/utaustinvilla3d/master/behaviors/naobehavior.cc).
- The 2011 demo behaviour excludes the kicker from the ring and recomputes slot indices from uniform numbers, so "a change of kicker reshuffles the slots of other players" (an example of what *not* to do without hysteresis) — [utaustinvilla3d strategy.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LARG/utaustinvilla3d/master/behaviors/strategy.cc).

**Humanoid league (NimbRo, 2017 TeenSize winner) and MSL**
- "The RoboCup Humanoid League has a special rule that prohibits more than one robot to be in the own area for more than 10 s"; NimbRo's defender "is not meant to touch the ball but stays ready to take it over. If the defender estimates that it is the closest to the ball, it will request a task change"; only the defender can request the change, not the striker; a `WaitClearOut` task handles the penalty-area rule — [NimbRo arXiv 1809.05361](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.05361) (search excerpt).
- Tech United (MSL) intercept coordination: "the fastest robot can intercept, even if the fastest is a defender, without compromising the defense" — [TU/e InterceptCoordination.pdf](https://robocup.wtb.tue.nl/svn/techunited/trunk/dev/Koen/InterceptCoordination.pdf) (search excerpt).

### Inferences
- The MMDR rationale transfers directly to 11-a-side: with 10 outfield slots, a Hungarian solve on integer costs (distance in cm, or ticks-to-reach) is ~1000 operations and trivially deterministic; MMDR's "minimise the maximum" objective is what prevents long cross-field runs and (per the AAAI paper's claims) path crossings when agents move on straight lines at similar speeds. MMD+MSD2 (bottleneck + min-sum-of-squares) is the cheaper O(n^3) variant if dynamic consistency is enforced separately with hysteresis.
- UT Austin Villa's public greedy assignment (sorted pairs, first-fit) is a reasonable fallback but, as their own code shows, has no hysteresis — slot churn will appear unless costs include a bonus for the currently held slot (gfootball does this for the ball-chaser; see Q3).
- The 1 m proximity / 0.5 m collision / 1 m sidestep constants are tuned for 0.57 m-tall Nao models on a 30×20 m field; scaled to a 105×68 m pitch with ~0.5 m player radii, 2–3 m proximity and ~1 m sidestep are the proportional analogues (estimate, not measured).
- The SPL "+1000 ms incumbent bonus" and NimbRo's "only the non-ball robot may request a hand-over" are the two simplest documented anti-oscillation rules for striker switching.

### Gaps

## Key Question 3 — Google Research Football / Gameplay Football built-in AI: off-ball placement, who presses, shape keeping (actual engine logic)

### Takeaway
The GRF engine (Bastiaan Konings' Gameplay Football, Apache-2.0, bundled in `third_party/gfootball_engine`) is a fully rule-based, parameter-driven system: a *team controller* (a) computes an "adapted formation position" per role from a possession-biased focal point with depth/width/offside-trap bounds, (b) re-solves a **Hungarian assignment** of the 10 outfielders to formation slots every 400 ms using rounded distance costs, (c) maintains a **designated possession player** with a ratio-based hysteresis (`switch only if timeRating < 0.8`) so only one player chases, and (d) assigns man-markers to the three most dangerous opponents; a *per-player controller* then blends formation target, a force-field of attractors/repellers (teammates repel at scale ~10.5 m, ball and possessor repel/attract), hunting/pressing gates (10–20 m, limited to the two closest), and a "laziness" speed model.

### Cited Findings

**Provenance**
- The GRF environment "is based on open-source game Gameplay Football"; "there is a single action per 100 ms reported to the environment" — [google-research/football README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/README.md). Google's blog states the engine is a heavily modified Gameplay Football in optimised C++ and that stochastic mode adds randomness to both environment and opponent AI — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/introducing-google-research-football-a-novel-reinforcement-learning-environment/) (search excerpt).
- Built-in AI difficulty is a scalar in (0,1]: an ICML 2022 MARL paper notes "The difficulty level of the right team is 0.05. Although the highest difficulty level is 1.0, beating a 0.05 difficulty-level opponent team is not that easy" — Wang et al., "Individual Reward Assisted Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning", ICML 2022 (retrieved via Lune full-text corpus; URL not retrieved). Difficulty enters the engine as `GetAiDifficulty()` scaling the hunting threshold (`0.3 + difficulty*0.7`) and the mental-image reaction delay (`+ (1 − difficulty) × 100 ms`) — [elizacontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp), [playercontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/playercontroller.cpp).
- Source layout: `src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp` (team tactics), `team.cpp` (possession arbitration), `AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp` (formation mapping, time-to-ball, offside), `player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp` (per-player AI), `playercontroller.cpp` (shared movement/defence helpers), `strategies/offtheball/{default_def,default_mid,default_off,goalie_default}.cpp` — [controller folder](https://github.com/google-research/football/tree/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller), [offtheball folder](https://github.com/google-research/football/tree/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/strategies/offtheball).

**Mentality / tactics (teamAIcontroller.cpp)**
- `UpdateTactics()` every 1000 ms: `goalFactor = clamp(0.5 + (oppGoals − goals)·0.25)`; `timeFactor = 0.5 + 0.5·clamp(matchTime/6300000)`; `offenseBias = clamp(0.5 + (goalFactor − 0.5)·timeFactor)`; `recentPossessionBias = 0.5 ± 0.5·possessionFactor_60s`; `offensivenessBias = 0.5·offenseBias + 0.5·recentPossessionBias` (default 0.5) — [teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp).
- Base tactics: depth factors 0.9 (offense)/0.75 (defense); width 0.9/0.8; own-half factors 0.52/0.54; midfield focus 0.6/0.5; micro-focus strengths 0.7/0.8; side-focus 0.1/0.4; constructor `depth = 0.45`, `width = 0.95`; user sliders apply `(o − 0.5)·2·multiplier` (0.1 depth/width, 0.3 midfield focus, 0.15 micro-focus). Role offsets ("mixup"): CB offense width +0.2; LB/RB defense own-half −0.075, offense width +0.2, offense own-half −0.1; LM/RM defense own-half −0.05, offense own-half −0.1; AM/CF defense depth +0.125 — [teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp).

**Formation position per player (team shape)**
- `GetAdaptedFormationPosition`: urgency from distance to possession player mapped over 2–30 m (closer → shorter ball-averaging windows, 3500/4000 ms scaled down); `possessionAmountBias` clamped over 0.3–0.7; `ballBias` from ball x clamped ±0.7, weighted more when possession is unclear (cap 0.6); shifted by `(offensivenessBias − 0.5)·0.3`; focal point = blend of 3000 ms ball average (possession) and 2000 ms average (no possession) anchored to the possession player; depth/width give back/front X and low/high Y bounds; side focus shifts centre X by possession side and role mindset; back bound cannot pass `offsideTrapX`; micro-focus pulls players toward the focal point (in defence limited to a point between trap line and own goal); midfield focus = 70% manual + 30% ball-following; result clamped to pitch — [teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp).
- `AI_GetAdaptedFormationPosition`: normalised formation coords are mapped `x = backXBound + (fx·0.5 + 0.5)·xLength` (same for y); midfield stretch `stretchBias = clamp(1 − |fx·1.2|, 0, 1)`; x-focus bias `pow(bias, 0.8)`; y-focus `bias *= 0.2 + 0.8·|yFocus|/pitchHalfH`; micro-focus: `homogeneousYInfluenceBias = 0.2`, `homogeneousYPositionBias = 0.4`, distance normalised by 50 m, applied only when < 1, shaped by `curve(microFocusBias, 0.3)` with a peak at 0.15 (width 0.25, height 0.1) — [AIfunctions.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp).
- Offside trap line `offsideTrapX` = max of: start distance `30 + 20·offensivenessBias` from own goal; force distance 6 m; ball position with offset `20 + 10·(1 − offensivenessBias)` fading as the ball nears the force distance; predicted ball 700 ms ahead; opponent possessor position + 0.15·movement + 4 m caution; and (slacking teammates) offside line − 4 m tolerance. `ApplyOffsideTrap` compresses positions within a 2.0 m band around the trap line onto it — [teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp).
- `AI_GetOffsideLine` projects each opponent forward by `movement·futureSim_ms·0.001`, takes the "one-but-deepest opponent", lets the ball raise the line, clamps at halfway and pitch — [AIfunctions.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp).

**Dynamic role (slot) assignment — Hungarian every 400 ms**
- `CalculateDynamicRoles`: goalkeeper removed; each outfielder's adapted formation position computed with `useDynamicFormationPosition = false`; cost = distance from `(position + 0.5·movement)` to each target, **rounded to tenths**; a Hungarian solve runs over increasing cost thresholds (stepping by 5 entries) until a valid assignment exists; forbidden pairs cost 50000; output written via `SetDynamicFormationEntry` — [teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp). It runs when `(time + team_offset) % 400 == 0`, man-marking at `(time + team_offset + 100) % 400 == 0`, `team_offset = 200` for the second team — [team.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/team.cpp).
- Defenders blend static vs dynamic slot by distance to the possessor: `actionDistance = NormalizedClamp(dist, 15, 20)`; `staticPositionBias = curve(actionDistance, 1.0)`; `desired = static·bias + dynamic·(1 − bias)` ("lower bias values let players swap formation positions more easily"); then `attackBias = NormalizedClamp((fadingPossession − 0.5), 0.2, 0.9)` blends toward the force-field support position; `AddDefensiveComponent(desired, clamp(1.9 − mindset − fadingPossession, 0, 1)^0.7)`; `ApplyOffsideTrap(desired)`; speed = `distance·distanceToVelocityMultiplier`, passed through `GetLazyVelocity`, clamped to sprint — [default_def.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/strategies/offtheball/default_def.cpp).

**Ball-ownership arbitration — the designated possession player (only one chaser)**
- `GetBestPossessionPlayer()` = active player with minimal `GetTimeNeededToGetToBall_ms()`; `timeNeededToGetToBall_ms` for the team = min over players — [team.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/team.cpp).
- Switching the designated player uses hysteresis: `timeRating = (bestTime + 500)/(designatedTime + 500)`; `×0.5` if best has possession, `÷0.5` if designated has possession; `×0.8`/`÷0.8` for externally (human) controlled; if `designatedTime < oppTime − 100` and best is not human: `timeRating += 0.2; timeRating *= 1.2`; **switch iff `timeRating < 0.8`** — comment: "switch only if other player is somewhat better, to overcome possession-chaos" — [team.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/team.cpp).
- Team possession ratio `teamPossessionAmount = (oppTimeToBall + 1500)/(ownTimeToBall + 1500)`; faded as `fading·0.995 + clamp(amount, 0.5, 1.5)·0.005` with max change ±0.005 per 10 ms; forced to 1.5/0.5 during set pieces/held ball — [team.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/team.cpp). Per player: `possessionAmount = (oppTime + 200)/(ownTime + 200)`, `hasBestPossession = hasPossession && possessionAmount >= 1.0` — [playercontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/playercontroller.cpp).
- Only the designated possessor (non-GK) runs on-ball logic, gated by `GetTimeNeededToGetToBall_ms() < 1000`; everyone else runs `defenseStrategy` (LB/CB/RB), `midfieldStrategy` (DM/LM/CM/RM/AM) or `offenseStrategy` (CF) — [elizacontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp).
- Time-to-ball estimate `AI_GetTimeNeededForDistance_ms`: default estimate at `maxVelocity·0.75`, solver uses `adaptedMaxVelocity = maxVelocity·0.94`; reach radii `radius_usual = 0.28` (leg extension), `radius_optimistic = 0.9`; foot offset 0.1; `changeTime_ms = 700`, `timeStep_ms = 10`; optimistic = usual − 200 ms in the far branch; `AI_HasPossession` control radius 1.0 m, early reject > 5 m, ball height ≤ 0.5, ball-to-player speed gate 6.0 — [AIfunctions.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp).

**Pressing / hunting / marking**
- Hunting (off-ball, team lacks best possession, not man-marking): `huntDistanceThreshold = 10 + (1 − mindSet)·10`, scaled by `0.5·fatigueInv + 0.5·(1 − ...)` and by `0.3 + difficulty·0.7`; distance measured between opponent (predicted `+movement·0.12`) and self (`+movement·0.04`); only the **two closest** teammates (`huntingPlayersNum = 2`) may hunt; target = `GetDefendPosition(opp)`, speed `clamp(dist·distanceToVelocityMultiplier, 0, sprint)`; designated possessor with `possessionAmount > 0.8` gets `forceMagnet, extraHaste` ("don't give up battles too easily") — [elizacontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp).
- `GetDefendPosition(opp, distance)`: covering point on the opponent→own-goal segment found by intersecting it with the perpendicular bisector of the me→opponent segment (`u` clamped to [0,1]); then `+ oppToGoal.normalized·sprintVelocity·0.1 + oppMovement·0.14 + oppToGoal.normalized·distance` — [playercontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/playercontroller.cpp).
- `_MovementCommand` defensive blends: designated defender `actionBias = 1 − curve(NormalizedClamp(dist, 0, 5), 0.7)` (`actionRadius = 5.0`); man-marking blend `pow(1 − curve(NormalizedClamp(playerOppDistance, 0, 7), 0.7), 1.2)`; hunt target `oppPos + oppToGoalDir·playerOppDistance·0.3`; `AddDefensiveComponent` constants: `possessionPlayerShootThreshold = 24.0`, `genericOpponentShootThreshold = 8.0`, `minDistance = 0.4`, `bufferDistance = 4.0`, shooting point clamped at the offside-trap line; no teammate/opponent avoidance in movement itself — [playercontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/playercontroller.cpp).
- Man marking: opponents ranked by distance to a point 2 m short of goal blended 80/20 with the ball's 100 ms prediction, score ×0.95 (+0.05 for the possessor); the **three** most dangerous get the best-quality marker (quality by distance to a line perpendicular to goal, ×0.6 if already beaten). "Team pressure" assignment of the closest non-GK to the opponent possessor (500 ms window) exists but its trigger is commented out ("DISABLED") — [teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp).

**Off-ball attacking support, spacing and avoidance (force field)**
- `GetSupportPosition_ForceField`: base lane shift `amount = 22`, `laneY = −sign(possessor.y)·8`, scaled by `curve(1 − NormalizedClamp(|laneY − y|, 0, 30), 1)` and `pow(dynamicMindSet, 1.5)`; forward-support player shifts `−side·(0.3 + 0.7·mindSet)·12`. Attractors/repellers: base position weight 0.7 (power `0.3 + 0.7·NormalizedClamp(dist, 0, 20)`); run spot weight `2.0·runWeight`; **opponents** (3 closest) repel weight 0.3 × role multiplier (CB/LB/RB 2.2, DM 2.0, CM/LM/RM 1.6, AM 1.2, CF 1.0) from a point 2.0 m *behind* the opponent "because the pass-way must be cleared", scale 5.0 (2.0 when running); **teammates** (6 closest) repel weight 0.4, scale `14·0.75 = 10.5`, applied only when `fadingTeamPossession >= 1.02`; **ball** repels at predicted positions 200/350/500/650 ms when possession ≥ 1.06 and not the possessor; **designated possessor** attracts (weight 0.45, scale 21) and repels (scale 12, "..yet not too close"); resolved by `AI_GetForceFieldMovement(forceField, pos, 7)`, clamped to pitch with a 0.08 onside margin — [elizacontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp).
- Attacking runs (every 500 ms when team has best possession, <2 humans): runner = closest player to possessor position offset 26 m toward goal; `distanceRating = sqrt(1 − clamp(dist, 0, 40))`; opponent density starts 1.0, minus `0.3·curve(...)^0.5` for each of 4 nearest opponents within 15 m of a spot 10 m ahead; run if `distanceRating·density ≥ 0.5`; 4000 ms cooldown. Forward support every 1500 ms = closest player to a point 1.5 m behind the possessor — [teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp).
- Dribble path avoidance: opponents predicted 0.25 s ahead repel with power 2.0, scale 10; sidelines/backline repel power 4.0, scale 20, exponent 0.7 placed 5 m outside the lines — [AIfunctions.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp).

**Speed model ("laziness")**
- `GetLazyVelocity`: `startLazinessDistance = 20·(fatigueInv·0.8 + 0.2)`, `endLazinessDistance = 65·(fatigueInv·0.5 + 0.5)`; `lazinessByRole = mindSet + teamPossession·(1 − 2·mindSet)`; `lazinessByPosition = NormalizedClamp(actionDistance, start, end)`; `lazyFactor = lazinessByPosition·(0.5 + 0.5·lazinessByRole)`; `v = desired·(1 − lazyFactor)`; over-speed above sprint damped ×0.1; result never below `dribbleVelocity` if desired ≥ dribble; short-term stamina cap `breathLeftFactor = (1 − NormalizedClamp(avgVel10, idle, sprint))^(0.8 − 0.2·workRate)·1.2`; stand-still decays `v·0.95 − rand(0, 3.2)`; "offensive players are lazy when the team lacks possession, defenders are lazy when it has it" — [elizacontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp).
- Mental image (perception delay): `_mentalImageTime = reactionTime + (1 − difficulty)·100 ms`, zero if this player made the last intentional touch; sliding uses a fixed 20 ms image — [playercontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/playercontroller.cpp).
- Related reference design (PhysicsFC, SIGGRAPH 2025, a physics-based football controller): 4-3-1-2 formation whose per-player targets "expand or contract toward the user's team or the opposing team depending on the ball's position", more so the farther the ball is from halfway; an opposing player moves to the ball only if the ball is within 10 m of its current position **and** within 15 m of its formation target, otherwise the closest opponent is sent — Kim, Jung & Lee, "PhysicsFC", SIGGRAPH 2025 (retrieved via Lune full-text corpus; URL not retrieved).

### Inferences
- GRF's engine is the closest open analogue to a FIFA-era engine and is already deterministic-friendly: its costs are rounded (`tenths`), its decisions are rate-limited (400 ms slot re-assignment, 500/1000/1500 ms tactical timers), and its chaser hand-over uses a *ratio with a 0.8 threshold and +500 ms smoothing* rather than raw argmin. In a 60 Hz fixed-point sim the same structure becomes: integer ticks-to-ball, `switch iff (best + 30)·8 < (designated + 30)·10` (i.e. ratio < 0.8 with 500 ms ≈ 30 ticks), evaluated every tick but with slot assignment every 24 ticks.
- Anti-bunching in GRF comes from three independent layers, which is why its 11v11 shapes look plausible with no learned model: (1) exclusive slots via Hungarian; (2) teammate repulsion (0.4 weight, ~10 m scale) in the support force field, but only when the team has the ball; (3) defence-side "only the two closest may hunt" plus man-marking capped at three opponents. The notable omission is any avoidance in `_MovementCommand` itself, so collisions between a hunter and a slot-holder are possible — a separate local-avoidance pass (as in UT Austin Villa) would be needed.
- The offside-trap line is effectively the "defensive line height" controller: `30 + 20·offensiveness` metres from goal as a start line, pulled back by ball/possessor proximity with 4 m caution, never below 6 m. These are directly reusable as integer metres.
- Because the designated possessor is team-wide state, GRF never has two AI chasers; human-controlled players are deliberately disadvantaged (×0.8) so that the AI defers to the human — the inverse of what a FIFA-like assist should do, but the same mechanism.

### Gaps
- `default_mid.cpp`, `default_off.cpp`, `AI_GetForceFieldMovement`, `AI_GetMindSet`, `curve()`/`NormalizedClamp()` definitions and `distanceToVelocityMultiplier`, `sprintVelocity`, `walkVelocity` numeric values were not read; pitch unit is metres by convention in this engine but the files do not state it.
- `GetTimeNeededToGetToBall_ms` on the Player class (the per-player solver that `AI_GetTimeNeededForDistance_ms` serves) was not read.
- The Kurach et al. (AAAI 2020) paper text itself (arxiv.org) was unreachable; engine facts above come from the code and README.
- Note for implementers: the fetched `CalculateDynamicRoles` has a GK-removal loop that "never increments its iterator when the first entry is not a goalkeeper, so it can loop forever in that case" — a latent bug in the engine, not a design feature.

## Key Question 4 — Pitch control / space control (Spearman 2018; Fernandez & Bornn 2018), Voronoi dominance, xT → "where should the off-ball player stand"

### Takeaway
Both dominant pitch-control models are closed-form and deterministic: Spearman's integrates a per-player logistic arrival probability (time-to-intercept = 0.7 s reaction + distance/5 m·s⁻¹, sigma 0.45 s) with a control rate λ = 4.3 s⁻¹ (GK ×3) over a 15 m/s ball travel time; Fernandez & Bornn's sums per-player bivariate Gaussians (mean shifted 0.5·velocity, radius 4–10 m by distance to ball, anisotropy (speed/13)² ) through a sigmoid. Voronoi dominance is the zero-reaction, equal-speed special case. For positioning, these give a cheap scalar field to score candidate off-ball spots; RoboCup teams use Voronoi vertices as the candidate set.

### Cited Findings
- Spearman's model (as documented in the Friends-of-Tracking reference implementation): `max_player_speed = 5` m/s; `reaction_time = 0.7` s ("roughly vmax/amax"); `tti_sigma = 0.45` s ("standard deviation of the sigmoid in Spearman 2018 that sets uncertainty in arrival time"); `lambda_att = 4.3` s⁻¹; `lambda_def = 4.3·kappa_def` with the comment that the paper uses `kappa = 1.72` for the defending team's advantage (set to 1 here); `lambda_gk = 3·lambda_def` so keepers claim faster; `average_ball_speed = 15` m/s; `int_dt = 0.04` s; `max_int_time = 10` s; `model_converge_tol = 0.01`; `max_player_accel = 7` m/s² (unused) — [Metrica_PitchControl.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Friends-of-Tracking-Data-FoTD/LaurieOnTracking/master/Metrica_PitchControl.py).
- Time to intercept: `r_reaction = position + velocity·reaction_time; time_to_intercept = reaction_time + |target − r_reaction| / vmax`. Arrival probability: `f(T) = 1 / (1 + exp(−π/√3/σ · (T − time_to_intercept)))`. Integration (per time step): `dPPCF_i/dT = (1 − PPCF_att − PPCF_def)·f_i(T)·λ_i`, accumulated `PPCF_i += dPPCF·dt` until total control > 0.99 — [Metrica_PitchControl.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Friends-of-Tracking-Data-FoTD/LaurieOnTracking/master/Metrica_PitchControl.py).
- Spearman's overall framing: "a physics-based pitch control model" quantifying "the quality of players' off-ball positioning"; scoring probability decomposed over spatial cells as `P(G|D) = Σ_r P(S_r|C_r,T_r,D)·P(C_r|T_r,D)·P(T_r|D)` (score given control and transition, control given transition, transition probability) — [Dop MSc thesis, UvA](https://staff.science.uva.nl/a.visser/education/masterProjects/Thesis_Ramon_Dop_MsDS.pdf), [tracking lecture slides](https://saberpowers.github.io/teaching/smgt432/lecture/tracking/slides.pdf) (search excerpts).
- A second independent implementation lists Spearman defaults `reaction_time 0.7 s`, `sigma 0.45 s`, `lambda_gk 3.0×`, `average_ball_speed 15 m/s`, grid 50×32 over 105×68 m — [silly-kicks _params.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/karsten-s-nielsen/silly-kicks/main/silly_kicks/tracking/pitch_control/_params.py).
- Fernandez & Bornn (2018) "player influence area" as implemented: `mu = pos + 0.5·vel`; `alpha = clip((speed/max_speed)², 0, 0.99)` with `max_speed = 13` m/s ("elite sprint ceiling"); isotropic below 0.1 m/s; `theta = atan2(vy, vx)`; `radius = clip(min_radius + dist_to_ball³/972, 4, 10)` m (note: "derived from visual inspection of a figure in the paper's appendix, not from the paper's text"); `sx = radius·(1 + alpha)`, `sy = radius·(1 − alpha)`; `Σ = R_θ·diag(sx², sy²)·R_θᵀ`; influence = `exp(−0.5·mahalanobis²)` normalised to peak 1; pitch control `= 1/(1 + exp(−(Σ_att − Σ_def)))` — [silly-kicks _fernandez_bornn.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/karsten-s-nielsen/silly-kicks/main/silly_kicks/tracking/pitch_control/_fernandez_bornn.py), [silly-kicks _params.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/karsten-s-nielsen/silly-kicks/main/silly_kicks/tracking/pitch_control/_params.py).
- Use in team-positioning optimisation: Everett et al. (AAAI 2025) discretise the pitch into a 25×16 grid, value zones with Expected Threat (xT, probability of a goal within the next 5 events), and define defensive team utility `U_t = −Σ_zones V_zone·(1 − Pr(defenders win possession if ball arrives in zone))`, where that probability is Spearman's (2018) ball-control model evaluated at zone centroids; MCTS + MIP then picks sub-team actions — Everett, Beal, Matthews, Norman & Ramchurn, "Optimising Spatial Teamwork Under Uncertainty", AAAI 2025 (retrieved via Lune full-text corpus; URL not retrieved).
- Pressure on the ball carrier is commonly computed with Andrienko et al.'s method (closer defender → higher pressure); defensive "covered area" is the polygon of defender positions, smaller = better defence — Xie et al., "PassVizor", IEEE VIS 2020 (retrieved via Lune; URL not retrieved). StatsBomb's pressure events use a 4–5 yard radius — [Pressing Intensity arXiv 2501.04712](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04712v1) (search excerpt).
- "Dominant region" (reaction/acceleration-aware Voronoi) was used for 3D-league positioning by Ulusoy & Sariel-Talay — [ICAART 2012](https://web.itu.edu.tr/~sariel/publications/OnuralpICAART2012.pdf) (search excerpt).

### Inferences
- Fixed-point friendly variants: (1) Voronoi/dominant-region control = assign each grid cell to the team of the player with minimal `ticksToReach`, which with equal max speed reduces to nearest-player (integer squared distances); (2) a 1-D logistic can be replaced by a lookup table of `f(Δticks)` with ~64 entries; (3) Fernandez-Bornn's Gaussian needs only `exp(−½·q)` of an integer Mahalanobis quadratic form — also a LUT.
- Translating to positioning: for an off-ball attacker, score candidate spots (formation slot ± jitter, or Voronoi vertices of opponent positions) by `pitchControl_att(spot) × passability(ballCarrier→spot) × xT(spot)` and move toward the best one with hysteresis (switch only if better by ≥ some margin for N ticks). This is exactly what CYRUS's unmarking "decisioning + positioning" split and Gliders2d's Voronoi vertex selection do, with hand-tuned rather than learned scores.
- "Keep spacing" falls out of control-maximisation: two teammates close together control almost the same cells, so the marginal control of the second is ~0; a greedy per-player maximisation of *team* control therefore spreads players without an explicit repulsion term — but it is more expensive than GRF's explicit repulsion, so use repulsion at runtime and pitch control for tuning.

### Gaps
- Original Spearman (2018, MIT Sloan) and Fernandez & Bornn (2018, MIT Sloan) PDFs were unreachable (sfu.ca, lukebornn.com, arxiv.org blocked); the parameters above are from two reference implementations whose comments cite the papers. The F&B radius-vs-ball-distance curve in particular is an implementer's fit, not the paper's equation.
- No source quantifies how pitch-control-based positioning compares against formation-only positioning in a game engine; Everett et al. is offline analytics.

## Key Question 5 — Goalkeeper–defender coordination: who takes the ball, keeper starting position vs defensive line, line stepping up, communication, simulation implementations

### Takeaway
Coaching practice reduces to a small vocabulary of binary calls — "Keeper!" (GK claims; nearest defender covers the goal; shouted *before* leaving the line) vs "Away!" (GK stays; defenders clear) — plus line-management calls ("Step"/"Step 2", "Hold"), with the GK's decision keyed to how close the nearest defender is to the attacker. Elite tracking shows keepers typically start ~5–8 m off their line with the back line 17–25 m ahead of them, and FIFA finds no single ideal starting position. The only open simulation with an explicit keeper-vs-defender rule I could read is GRF's goalie strategy: the keeper comes out when its teammate is farther from the shooting point than the attacker by more than 1.0 m, otherwise it holds a bisector position 0.7 m+ off the line scaled by ball distance.

### Cited Findings

**Simulation implementations**
- GRF `goalie_default.cpp`: default target 10 m in front of the goal line (`lineDistance = 10`), ball predicted `600 ms + 0.2 × keeperTimeToBall`; tactical branch only when the predicted ball is in the keeper's half. Position = along the **bisector** of the lines from ball to the posts at y = ±3.7, ending at a back line `pitchHalfW − 0.7`; distance off the line `awayFromGoalOffset = clamp(distance·awayFromGoalBias, 0.7, pitchHalfW)` with `distance = max(|ballToGoal| − 0.5, 0)` and base `awayFromGoalBias = 0.3 × NormalizedClamp(fadingTeamPossession, 1.0, 1.5)` (farther ball → farther off the line). **Come-out rule** (team lacks possession): start point = `opp·0.6 + ball·0.4` (or `0.4/0.6` if the opponent has possession), `shootThreshold = 20 m`; the keeper comes out (`awayFromGoalBias = 1.0`) iff `mateToThresholdDistance > oppToThresholdDistance + 1.0` (nearest teammate, extrapolated `+0.24·movement`, is farther from the shooting point than the attacker, extrapolated `+0.32·movement`); a secondary helper (`helperShootThreshold = 24`) reduces the bias via `1 − NormalizedClamp(ratio, 1.0, 1.5)·0.7`; a team-level `KeeperRush` timer forces bias 1.0; when walking back within 1.0 m of the ball–goal line speed drops to walk. Shot branch: project keeper onto the ball→goal-line segment, stand at `u` (offset `minGoalLineDist = 0.4`), run to the goal-line point if the predicted ball height > 2.5 m. `CalculateIfBallIsBoundForGoal`: within 32 m of the 250 ms ball prediction, intersect the 0→800 ms ball line with the goal line, bound iff `|y| ≤ 3.7 × panic`, `panic = 1.02 + (1 − (defensivePositioning·0.6 + vision·0.4))·0.5` — [goalie_default.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/strategies/offtheball/goalie_default.cpp).
- GRF keeper deflect/claim trigger: role GK; `ball.Predict(400)` within `ballDistanceOptimizeThreshold + 10 m`; not a deliberate back-pass; no ball retainer; `|predicted y at 160 ms| ≤ 20.05`; predicted ball inside the box (`−pitchHalfW + 16.4`) — [playercontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/playercontroller.cpp). Outfielders discount passes to the keeper (`×0.7`, "don't like playing back to goalie"); the GK is excluded from auto-switch and from dynamic slot assignment; the keeper only runs on-ball logic when it is the designated possessor with `timeToBall < 1000 ms` — [elizacontroller.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp), [team.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/football/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/team.cpp).
- UvA Trilearn's intercept table explicitly computes "fastest teammate **without goalie**" as a separate feature, so outfield decisions can exclude the keeper from "who is fastest" — [keepaway WorldModelHighLevel.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tjpalmer/keepaway/master/player/WorldModelHighLevel.cc).
- NimbRo humanoid: the defender takes over the ball only by *requesting* a task change when it estimates itself closest; the humanoid league's "no more than one robot in own area for 10 s" rule forces an explicit defender/keeper clear-out — [NimbRo arXiv 1809.05361](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.05361) (search excerpt). Tech United (MSL): the fastest robot intercepts "even if the fastest is a defender" — [TU/e InterceptCoordination](https://robocup.wtb.tue.nl/svn/techunited/trunk/dev/Koen/InterceptCoordination.pdf) (search excerpt).

### Inferences
- A deterministic "Keeper!/Away!" arbiter can be built from the GRF rule: compute `ticksToBall` for the keeper and for the nearest defender (both extrapolated), and for the attacker; the keeper claims iff `keeperTicks < attackerTicks` **and** `defenderTicks > attackerTicks + margin` (GRF uses a 1.0 m margin at the shooting point); once the keeper has committed (set a `keeperClaiming` flag for ~0.3–0.5 s, GRF's rush timer is 300 ms), defenders must not enter the ball's line and the nearest defender covers the goal-line point — this reproduces the coaching rule that the shout precedes the first step.
- Keeper starting position as a function of the defensive line: FIFA's measured gaps (≈17–25 m line-to-keeper; keeper 5–8 m off the line when the ball is in the attacking third; ~29 m from goal when the team attacks) suggest a two-anchor model: `gkX = clamp(lineX − gapByBallX, minOffLine, maxOffLine)` with `gapByBallX` ≈ 20 m when the ball is in the opponent half, shrinking to ≈ 12 m as the ball crosses halfway, and the bisector/angle rule taking over inside ~30 m (GRF uses offset = 0.3 × ball distance, min 0.7 m).
- "Line steps up when the keeper has the ball" is implied by the "Step after a clearance" call and by GRF's offside-trap rule that the back bound follows the ball and possession (start line 30–50 m from goal when in possession); a simple integer implementation is `lineX = max(lineX, ballX − 20 m)` once the keeper has controlled the ball, ramped over ~1 s.
- Line-of-sight: no quantitative source was found; coaching texts only imply that the nearest defender covers the *goal* (not the ball) when the keeper comes, which keeps defenders out of the keeper's path.

### Gaps

- No peer-reviewed study reporting keeper-to-defensive-line distance distributions across a large sample was found; the FIFA figures are clip- or tournament-level descriptive numbers.

## Key Question 6 — Sports-science spacing numbers: inter-player distances, centroid/stretch index, line synchronisation, defensive width/depth

### Takeaway
A 13-study systematic review of professional matches gives the reference envelope: team length 31–46 m, team width 35–48 m, stretch index (mean player distance to team centroid) 7–16 m, team surface area ≈ 900 m², with teams in possession consistently more dispersed than teams defending; dispersion is largest in build-up. Bundesliga tracking (153 games) shows that compactness of the *five defenders nearest the ball*, not whole-team compactness, predicts defensive success. Explicit nearest-teammate-distance norms by phase were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Systematic review (professional matches, 13 studies from Spain, Portugal, England, Brazil, Champions League): "the team length and width ranged from 31 to 46 m and from 35 to 48 m, respectively"; "the stretch index ranged from 7 to 16 m"; mean team area ≈ 900 m²; "teams in possession covered more distance and area than teams out of possession in all studies" — [Reference values for collective tactical behaviours... systematic review (Biology of Sport 2021)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/350650966_Reference_values_for_collective_tactical_behaviours_based_on_positional_data_in_professional_football_matches_a_systematic_review), [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/7a7ea0fcb9c2448bbea95d777b73d88f) (search excerpts).
- Stretch index definition: "the average distance (m) of each player to the team centroid"; length = distance between deepest and most advanced player, width = distance between widest players; surface area = convex hull of outfield players — [Griffith A-League study](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/8e97b8ae-11d9-48b6-ac11-b49c77ba5146/content), [Leeds Beckett rugby dispersion paper (definitions)](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/11937/1/CloseTheGapContextualInfluencesOnDefensiveDispersionInRugbyLeaguePV-COLLINS.pdf) (search excerpts).
- A-League team: "team dispersion significantly increased in attacking phases of play, being greatest for 'build-up play' phases" — [Griffith A-League study](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/8e97b8ae-11d9-48b6-ac11-b49c77ba5146/content) (search excerpt).
- Bundesliga 2020/21, 153 games: "The defending subgroup of five defenders closest to the ball showed a higher compactness (smaller surface area and smaller spread) in successful defensive plays compared to unsuccessful ones, while there were almost no differences in the compactness of the whole team" — Forcher & Forcher, "Is a compact organization important for defensive success in elite soccer?" — [Univ. Vienna record](https://ucrisportal.univie.ac.at/en/publications/is-a-compact-organization-important-for-defensive-success-in-elit/), [KIT record](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000173445/154142318) (search excerpts).
- High pressing increases longitudinal dispersion "explained longitudinally by further inter-line distances, which opened more space for the opposing forward and attacking midfielder" — [Low, PhD dissertation, German Sport University Cologne](https://fis-db.dshs-koeln.de/ws/portalfiles/portal/13553143/4.1_PhD_dissertation_for_publishing_in_DSHS_Koeln_library_Low_Zhi_Wei_Benedict.pdf) (search excerpt).
- Pitch size effect (11v11 GPS study): full-size pitch gave "significantly greater values of centroid in goal-to-goal axis, centroid in lateral-to-lateral axis and total stretch index" and "increased the distances between teammates and the distances to the centroid" — [Redalyc article](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/710/71055516003/71055516003.pdf) (search excerpt).
- LaLiga tracking (60 matches, <10 cm accuracy): time spent within 2 m of an opponent analysed by playing position (framed as SARS-CoV-2 exposure, but a usable proximity statistic) — [Garrido et al., Frontiers in Psychology 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.723414) (search excerpt). A separate automatic-tracking study reports nearest-opponent dyad distances by competition level, period and position — [PMC6942461](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6942461) (search excerpt; values not retrieved).
- Inter-player distance "due to its simplicity and ease of computation, is a commonly assessed collective tactical variable" — [Frontiers (netball) via search](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.723414) (search excerpt).
- Small-sided-games review (21 studies): centroid used in 14, surface area in 6, stretch index and length-per-width ratio in 5 — [Coito et al. review via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7996382) (search excerpt).
- Review of tactical-expertise findings: "higher movement regularity; wider dispersion in youth players and shorter readjustment delay between teammates may indicate better tactical expertise" — [PubMed 31571155](https://www.pubmed.gov/31571155/) (search excerpt).
- Portuguese Liga 2022/23 defensive transitions: "greater variation in distances between the first two defenders and the ball hindered progression" (interpersonal distances, angles and Voronoi areas) — [ISCTE repository article](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/34915/1/article_112396) (search excerpt).

### Inferences
- Target envelope for an 11v11 sim (estimates derived from the review's ranges): out of possession, aim for length ≈ 31–36 m, width ≈ 35–40 m, stretch index ≈ 7–10 m; in possession/build-up, length ≈ 40–46 m, width ≈ 42–48 m, stretch index ≈ 12–16 m. With 10 outfielders in a ~900 m² hull, the mean nearest-teammate spacing is roughly √(900/10) ≈ 9–10 m (geometric estimate, not a measured value); GRF's teammate repulsion scale (10.5 m) and its 15–20 m "action distance" band are consistent with this.
- Forcher's result argues for a *ball-local* compactness controller (pull the 4–5 nearest defenders toward a ball-side block, e.g. GRF's micro-focus + "two closest may hunt") rather than compressing the entire team, which would collapse width.
- Line synchronisation metrics in the literature (centroid readjustment delay, inter-line distance) suggest implementing lines as shared integer state (`lineX` per unit) that individuals track with a small lag, which matches the agent2d `max_x` offside clamp and GRF's `ApplyOffsideTrap` 2 m band.

- No source with inter-line (defence–midfield) distances in metres was found; the Cologne dissertation and the Forcher paper would be the places to read.
- Davids/Duarte/Folgado "synergies" papers were not retrievable; only their derived variables (centroid, stretch index, readjustment delay) appear via secondary excerpts.
