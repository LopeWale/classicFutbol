## Key Question 1 — How does PES choose support positions and runs for teammates (triggers, counts, spacing, onside line), and what do the instructions change?

### Takeaway
PES generates support as a ring of candidate receiving positions around the ball carrier at a tunable reference distance (the "Support Range" slider), tests each candidate for "open space" (no player nearby AND a teammate can reach it before any opponent), picks the candidate farthest from opponents, and sends the nearest eligible teammate toward it before the pass; a second slider ("Player Support") biases whether those options are ahead of, around, or behind the carrier. Per-player participation (Attack/Defence Awareness in PES 2010-2011, Playing Styles and "Attacking/Defensive/Counter Target/Anchoring" instructions in PES 2015-eFootball) decides who is allowed to make forward runs at all, and Advanced Instructions (PES 2017+) add scripted group patterns (False 9 drop-and-fill, Wing Rotation, Centring Targets, Tiki-Taka suppressing runs in behind).

### Cited Findings

**Candidate-based support positioning (patent, PES 2012 "Active AI" era, priority 14 Jun 2011)**
- Konami's US 9,180,371 B2 (inventor Shin Tsuchiya, assignee Konami Digital Entertainment, priority JP 2011-132728, 14 Jun 2011) sets "a plurality of positions in an area around the position of the user character as the receiving position candidates" where a teammate can receive a pass. The claims require that the candidates are "set around a perimeter of a geometric shape" with the ball holder at the centre, "on respective straight lines extending from the position of the character holding the moving object in a plurality of predetermined directions", at least two in opposite directions, and that "a relative positional relationship between the character holding the moving object and the plurality of positions is maintained in accordance with the movement of the character holding the moving object" — i.e., the ring moves with the carrier. — [USPTO US9180371](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9180371)
- Spacing numbers: candidate 70B "is set at a position away from the position of the player character 48A in the direction 72B by the reference distance L"; diagonal candidate 70E is at "(L² + L²)^1/2" (L√2). The distance L may itself be "a parameter concerning the distance between the position of the player character holding the ball and the receiving position candidate" stored in game data. — [Google Patents US9180371B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9180371B2/en)
- Open-space test: a candidate is in open space when "(1) neither of player characters 48, 50 is positioned around the receiving position candidate" and "(2) a player character 48 belonging to the user team (other than the ball holder) is able to reach the receiving position candidate earlier than a player character 50 belonging to the opponent team"; the arrival-time check reads "the running parameter of the player character" and the distance to the candidate. — [Google Patents US9180371B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9180371B2/en)
- Selection: "a receiving position candidate having the longest distance to a player character belonging to the opponent team ... may be selected. Alternatively, a receiving position candidate which imposes on a player character belonging to the opponent team the longest period of time to reach ... is selected." The candidate may be modified by "displacement ... in the direction opposite to the direction of a player character belonging to the opponent team, to thereby ensure a longer distance". — [Google Patents US9180371B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9180371B2/en)
- Assignment and gating: the teammate "positioned closest to the receiving position candidate" is chosen; but "in a case in which the distance between a teammate player character closest to the receiving position candidate and the receiving position candidate is equal to or longer than a predetermined distance, the control unit may select no teammate player character", and likewise if "the distance between the base position of a teammate player character and the receiving position candidate is equal to or longer than a predetermined distance" (base position = formation slot). The chosen teammate is moved "toward a position based on the selected potential receiving position before the pass is performed". — [Google Patents US9180371B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9180371B2/en); [USPTO US9180371](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9180371)
- An earlier Konami patent (US 9,132,355 B2, Terada & Mimura, priority 22 Aug 2008) defines the vocabulary used throughout: "a player character suitable as a pass target ... refers to ... a player character having no player character of the opponent team positioned nearby (... a 'player character which is free')" and "an area suitable as a movement destination refers to an area having no player character positioned nearby (... an 'open space')", both computed "based on the positions of the player characters belonging to the second team". — [USPTO US9132355](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9132355)

### Inferences
- The US 9,180,371 structure (ring of candidates at L and L√2 around the carrier, carried along with him, open-space test = exclusion radius + arrival-time race, pick max opponent distance, assign nearest teammate subject to a current-distance cap and a base-position cap) is almost certainly the code path behind "Active AI" in PES 2012 (priority June 2011, four months before release) and behind the "Support Range" slider (the stored distance parameter). The Player Support slider plausibly weights which "predetermined directions" are enabled (behind/around/ahead).
- The two gating distances (teammate too far from the candidate; teammate's formation base too far from the candidate) are what keep PES support local and formation-anchored; this is why community tuning talks about slot positions and Attacking Style interacting ("he will tend to move there when your team has possession").
- The eFHUB "post-regain window" (3 s or 5 s) implies PES/eFootball runs a distinct transition-attack movement mode for a fixed duration after a turnover, after which normal possession positioning resumes; this is inference from a fan source, not Konami documentation.

### Gaps
- No Konami source gives the actual value of L, the exclusion radius in condition (1), or how many candidates are sampled per frame; the patent leaves them as parameters.
- Team Spirit's effect on run timing is undocumented: the only guide found (videogamer.com/?p=130309) now 404s; its search excerpt said Team Spirit is "a numerical value out of 100", lowered by swapping key players or changing formation ("big changes possibly dropping a 90+ score into the low 80s") and raised by repeatedly using the same instructions, but gave no on-pitch effect. A fan site's claim that Team Playstyle Level "feeds into Team Spirit, the number that quietly scales how sharply your side executes" is unsourced. — [gamemarket.gg](https://gamemarket.gg/news/efootball/efootball-team-playstyles-explained-how-each-tactic-works)
- No source quantifies how many teammates make forward runs simultaneously under "Numbers in attack: Few/Medium/Many" or under Attack/Defence Level notches.

## Key Question 2 — How does PES decide which teammates press and which hold shape, and how does the block shift and stay compact?

### Takeaway
PES selects pressers by distance to the ball (one elected presser; "multiple pressure" adds 2-3), and Konami's 2008 patents add two game-state modulators: a contain-then-close timer whose length (in 1/60 s units) grows when the pressing team is winning and shrinks when it is losing, and a no-press strip near the opponents' goal line whose depth likewise grows/shrinks with the score and expires after about 10 s. The rest of the block is positioned by Pressing/Compactness/Defensive Line/Containment Area team settings: Compactness narrows the team horizontally toward the ball, Containment Area chooses whether to funnel the carrier inside or outside, Defensive Line sets depth, and Frontline Pressure vs All-Out Defence decides whether the nearest group presses or everyone drops. eFootball's "Call for Pressure" makes the rule explicit: exactly one teammate presses, the others either block lanes or push the line depending on distance to the ball.

### Cited Findings

**Who presses (selection and count)**

**Marking logic (patents) and zonal defence (PES 2012)**
- Konami patent US 8,597,094 B2 (Taya, priority 2 Nov 2007; EP 2208517 is the same family): "'to mark' means to defend against a specific opponent player while keeping close to that player (while keeping the distance to the specific opponent player shorter than a predetermined distance)". "Normal mode": the marker is positioned "between the marking target player character and the predetermined position" (the goal). "Intercept mode": "the position (M) of the marker player object is controlled so as to be located on the straight line 62 connecting the position (T) of the marking target player object and the position (B) of the ball holding player object", at "a predetermined distance" from T. Switching to intercept mode requires (a) "the angle (θ) between the representative direction 80 of the ball holding player object and the direction 82 from ... (B) to ... (T) is equal to or smaller than a predetermined reference angle (θa)" — "a case in which the ball holding player object is directed to a marking target player object and highly likely to make a pass" — and (b) a teammate (not the GK) "is located within a distance from the straight line 60 [marked target → own goal], equal to or shorter than the predetermined reference distance (La)", i.e., cover exists behind the marker. A variant uses the human opponent's pass cursor instead of facing. — [USPTO US8597094](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8597094); [Google Patents EP2208517A4](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP2208517A4/en)
- Konami patent US 8,216,037 B2 (Terada, priority 9 Jun 2008): the defender marking the ball holder is "located on the straight line 62 connecting the player character 46a (or the ball 48) and a representative point 60 of the goal ... away from the player character 46a ... by a predetermined distance (R1)", and the GK "away from the representative point 60 of the goal ... by a predetermined distance (R2)" on the same line. In an optional "high score mode" (user-selected, or auto-switched "when neither team has made any score after elapse of a predetermined period of time after ... the second half starts"), the marker is instead placed R1 along the ball holder's forward direction and the GK R2 along the goal's forward direction, so "an arrangement for making scoring relatively easy be effected". — [USPTO US8216037](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8216037)

### Inferences
- Combined, US 8,333,663 and US 8,398,487 describe a press controller with (a) one or more pressers elected by distance to ball, (b) a contain phase of T frames before closing, (c) T and a goal-line no-press strip both scaled by score difference (ahead → longer contain, bigger no-press strip; behind → shorter, smaller), (d) a ~10 s escape hatch. This matches reviewers' later complaint that PES AI "back off, back off" (PES 2018) and "instantly retreat after giving away possession" (PES 2020) — containment is a tuned default, not a bug.
- The "zonal defence / hold the line" language from PES 2010-2012 and the eFootball "Call for Pressure" text together imply the block is positioned in two tiers: the elected presser(s) leave shape; everyone else is placed by distance to ball into either a lane-blocking role (near) or a line-pushing role (far). Compactness is a horizontal gather toward the ball side; Containment Area flips the lane-blocking bias (inside vs outside).
- The Taya marker patent shows PES marking is not pure man-marking: it is a between-man-and-goal default that switches to an interception line only when the carrier is facing the marked man (θ ≤ θa) and a covering teammate exists behind — a cheap, deterministic "jump the lane only if covered" rule.

### Gaps
- No source gives the frame values T1-T5, the distances Da/Db, θa, La, R1, R2 or the "threshold distance" of the contain phase; the patents use placeholders.
- Konami never documents the exact criteria for which teammate is "the cursor change target" alternative presser, nor the Call-for-Pressure timeout length ("a certain period of time").
- The eFHUB and eFootball Explained numbers are fan datamines/reverse-engineering with no methodology published; I could not corroborate them.

## Key Question 3 — After a turnover, which PES players recover at sprint and which stay forward, and how do attributes and instructions decide this?

### Takeaway
PES's default transition is a team-wide retreat into shape (Konami's own PES 2018 lead admitted "As soon as the teams lost the ball they tended to go into a shape"), overridden by Frontline Pressure/Gegenpress for an immediate counter-press by the nearest group, and by per-player exemptions (Counter Target, Attack/Defence Awareness "OFF", Classic No.10 and other Playing Styles) that keep designated forwards high. Which individuals track back is driven by Defensive Awareness/Prowess (reaction speed to the ball when defending), Defensive Engagement (eFootball, participation in defence), Teamwork (PES 2008-2013, positional sense) and Stamina, with Attack/Defence Level and Fluid Formation changing the out-of-possession formation itself.

### Inferences
- Across PES 2015-2021 the recovery decision is layered: (1) Defensive Style picks the team mode (press from front vs drop); (2) Gegenpress/Counter Target/Defensive/Wing Back and the player's Playing Style or Awareness flag exempt or compel individuals; (3) Attack/Defence Level and Fluid Formation set how deep the out-of-possession slots are; (4) Defensive Awareness/Prowess scales reaction latency; (5) Stamina scales whether the sprint is sustained. There is no evidence of a FIFA-style per-player "recovery urgency" score; PES appears to use binary role exemptions plus a team mode, which is why reviewers describe transitions as "synchronized" and "rigid".
- The PES Kings Edition reading of Response (high-Response defenders "abandon the last line" to attack loose balls; low-Response wait) suggests PES 2012/2013 used one reaction-speed stat to decide both who steps out to press and who holds the line — consistent with Konami later naming the stat "Defensive Awareness" and explicitly tying Match-up reaction timing to it in v6.0.0.

### Gaps
- No Konami source states the number of recovering players, their speed (sprint vs jog), or latency as a function of Defensive Awareness; only "how quickly a player can respond to the ball" is stated.
- Team Spirit's effect on transitions is undocumented.
- The PES 2012/2013 stat definitions are from a fan stat-making community, not Konami.

## Key Question 4 — Konami patents on teammate positioning, support movement, defensive marking or formation logic (beyond US 2010/0240429)

### Takeaway
Konami Digital Entertainment holds a cluster of 2006-2012 soccer-AI patents (all with 1/60 s update loops) that together specify: open-space support candidates around the carrier (US 9,180,371); marker positioning between man and goal with an intercept-mode switch on carrier facing angle plus cover check (US 8,597,094 / EP 2208517); contain-then-close pressing timed by score (US 8,333,663); a score-scaled no-press strip with a ~10 s timeout (US 8,398,487); defender-on-line-to-goal at fixed distances with a late-game "high score mode" (US 8,216,037); a 90-frame lose-sight-of-runner penalty (US 2009/0291724); and several control/assist patents (field-of-view passing, off-ball switching, pass-delay for a human off-ball runner).

- I did not find a 2008-2013 review that explicitly says FIFA's own AI teammates were "passive" as a contrast point; FIFA-side criticism of that era is outside this note's sources.

## Key Question 6 — What PES does that a FIFA-10-12-style deterministic 60 Hz sim (formation slots with phases, presser + cover, marks, elected chaser, destination reservation, 70% cruise speed, support spacing) is likely still missing

### Takeaway
The sim already has the skeleton PES uses (slots by phase, one elected presser, cover, marks, reserved run destinations). What the PES sources add are concrete, deterministic rules the sim probably lacks: an open-space candidate ring for support with arrival-time racing and formation-anchor gating; a contain-then-close timer and no-press zone scaled by score; marker mode switching on carrier facing angle with a cover check; a frame-counted lose-sight penalty for defenders; team knobs with explicit semantics (Player Support ahead/behind, Compactness as horizontal gather, Containment Area funnel, Numbers in attack, Attack/Defence Level incl. CB joining); a short fixed post-regain transition window; per-player participation exemptions; and scripted group patterns for instructions like False 9 and Wing Rotation.

### Inferences
- The two PES ideas most likely absent from the described sim are (a) score/time-aware containment (contain timer + no-press strip) and (b) the open-space race test with a formation-anchor cap for support runs; both are cheap, integer-friendly and were explicitly designed around a 1/60 s loop.
- PES's documented failure modes (synchronised retreat, three defenders on one striker, wing-backs stranded high, over-deep Deep Defensive Line) are the predictable result of binary role flags plus a team-wide mode; a sim that already elects a single chaser can avoid them by adding a per-player recovery urgency rather than copying PES's exemption flags wholesale.

### Gaps
- Nothing in the sources quantifies PES's cruise/jog speed for uninvolved players or its destination-reservation rules for runs, so no direct comparison with the sim's 70% cruise speed or reservation layer is possible.
- No PES source describes how PES avoids two teammates choosing the same support candidate beyond the v4.0.0 cross-run de-duplication note.
