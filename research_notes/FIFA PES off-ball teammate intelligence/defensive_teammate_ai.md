# FIFA (EA) defensive behaviour of AI-controlled teammates and the defensive transition (FIFA 10-12 focus, later titles labelled by year)

Scope note on sources. EA's own FIFA 10-12 material on *AI teammate* defending is almost entirely in the EA "Backpage" column (Darren Cross, 2011-2014), the David Rutter / Aaron McHardy launch interviews, the FIFA 09 manual (which defines the Custom Tactics sliders that FIFA 10-13 reused unchanged) and the FIFA 14 "Teammate Intelligence" feature post. EA publishes no numbers for FIFA 10-12 defensive AI; the only official numbers found are from FIFA 19-FC 25 (press window ≈ 7 s, Team Press 30 s → 20 s, stamina ×2.2 → ×3, FC 25 line-height bands) and are labelled by year below. No Electronic Arts patent on soccer defensive positioning/marking was found; three Konami (PES-side) patents that do specify mechanisms with thresholds are included and clearly labelled as non-EA. The session's network allow-list blocked direct fetches of ea.com, godisageek.com, futbin.com, fifauteam.com and web.archive.org; the EA pages were read through a server-side fetch tool instead, so all EA quotes below are verbatim from the live pages.

## Key Question 1 — After a turnover, what is the "recovery urgency" window, and who sprints back (by role, work rate, distance behind the ball)?

### Takeaway
FIFA 10 introduced a per-player "urgency" state (relaxed when uninvolved, sprinting the moment a threat is detected) and FIFA 11 added Attacking/Defensive Work Rates that decide whether a player tracks back at all; EA never published a FIFA 10-12 recovery-time number. The first official time window is FIFA 19+'s "Pressure After Possession Loss" style: AI teammates press for approximately 7 seconds after losing the ball, then revert to the balanced shape (FIFA 19/20), and FIFA 21 capped the D-pad Team Press at 20 s (down from 30 s) with stamina draining 3× faster (up from 2.2×) while any pressing style is active.

That works for both defenders and attackers." Defenders also got a reworked threat-analysis system: "if there's a gap somewhere behind the defenders, then they won't switch out their positions." — [GameSpot, FIFA 10 Impressions, 3 Jun 2009](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/fifa-10-impressions/1100-6211111)
- FIFA 10 (2009) feature list: "On defense, new concepts like position priority enable defenders to multitask so dangerous spaces left open by teammates out of position are covered. - Real-football reference used only as context for the 7-s figure: FIFA's Women's World Cup 2023 analysis found "61% of ball recoveries across the tournament occurred within seven seconds of the ball being lost." — [FIFA Training Centre](https://fifatrainingcentre.com/en/game/tournaments/fifa-womens-world-cup/2023/post-tournament-analysis/the-significance-of-direct-pressure-and-forced-turnovers.php)

### Inferences
- FIFA's recovery is not a single team-wide "urgency timer". It is (a) a per-player threat test (FIFA 10 urgency: run hard only when *you* see a threatening position, e.g. a gap behind you or a runner you own) layered on (b) a per-player work-rate zone that fixes *where* each player retreats to and whether he retreats at all, and (c) from FIFA 19 an optional team-wide ~7 s counter-press window during which the nearest players chase rather than retreat.
- The 7-s window is a *press* window, not a *sprint-back* window: after it ends the team "reverts to Balanced", i.e. players fall back to their work-rate-defined defensive slots at normal urgency. Nothing in EA's text says forwards sprint back during those 7 s; the sources say the ball is pressed.
- Low-DWR forwards (Berbatov in EA's own example) are explicitly designed *not* to come back, so a sim where "forwards jog back too slowly" is only wrong for medium/high-DWR forwards; for low-DWR ones FIFA deliberately keeps them high.
- Stamina is the balancing cost for urgency: every EA pressing lever (High Pressure, Team Press, Constant Pressure) is documented with a stamina penalty, and FIFA 21 quantified it at 3×.

### Gaps
- No EA source gives a FIFA 10-12 number for how long urgency lasts after a turnover, the sprint-vs-jog speed split, or the distance-behind-the-ball threshold that triggers a recovery sprint.
- No source states how FIFA ranks *which* player sprints back first (e.g. nearest to own goal vs nearest to ball vs by DWR) other than the FIFA 20 Striker Drop Back rule (DWR then Marking attribute).

## Key Question 2 — How does FIFA decide the one or two players who engage the carrier versus the ones who hold shape, and how do the holders position?

### Takeaway
In FIFA 10-11 the engagers were whoever the human pressed plus, on demand, the single "closest AI defender" summoned by Secondary Press; FIFA 12 kept the same single-second-man rule (Teammate Contain) but changed what he does from auto-tackle to "cover off" — track the carrier at a distance, shepherd him, never launch a tackle. Holders are described as staying in their formation zone, goal-side, facing the ball, showing the carrier infield toward numbers and only taking the ball when it comes close enough; FIFA 14 then replaced "mark the nearest opponent" with a multi-frame search for the most dangerous player.

### Cited Findings
- FIFA 09/10 (2009): Operation Sports users describe the Second Defender Press: "When you press the SDP button, the defender closest to the CPU ball handler will try to take the ball from the CPU." and warn "don't overuse it." — [Operation Sports, Question about defense in human vs. AI games, Feb 2009](https://forums.operationsports.com/forums/ea-sports-fifa-last-gen/297835-question-about-defense-human-vs-ai-games.html)
- FIFA 10 (2009) Darren Cross: "Initiating a Secondary Press (hold the shoot button while the other team has the ball) will get one of your AI-controlled team-mates to press the ball while leaving you in control of a separate defender, ideally the deepest one... should the striker get past the AI defender, the player you're in control of will be ready to mop up." — [Darren Cross FIFA 10 defending guide (repost), Oct 2009](https://www.xboxachievements.com/forum/topic/160180-tips-n-tricks-ultimate-guide-to-fifa-10)
- FIFA 11 (2010): "the press and standing tackle feature take care of all that for you, tracking down the player with the ball and pressing them constantly until your team win it back... all you are doing is holding one button to press – or two to double press." — [EA Backpage, Tactical Defending Explained, 3 Jul 2011](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/the-backpage-tactical-defending-explained)
- FIFA 11 (2010) failure mode EA wanted to remove: "In FIFA 11 pressing wasn't really controlled, especially if a player was using secondary press, and formations would be all over the place at times because the player closest to the ball would follow it to the other side of the pitch if the button was held for long enough." — [EA Backpage, Tactical Defending Help](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/the-backpage-tactical-defending-help)

- FIFA 20 (2019) style descriptions of the AI press: "At Drop Back, the player approaches the ball but doesn't provoke a challenge. He'll surround the opponent, keep the team in place... If the slider is at Constant Pressure, the AI will call for other players to chase the one who is possessing the ball." — [FIFAUTeam, FIFA 20 Custom Tactics Guide](https://fifauteam.com/fifa-20-custom-tactics-guide)
- FC 25 (2024) role that formalises a designated engager: Stopper — "A ball-winning centre-back who will step forward from the defensive line to make tackles, intercept passes, and lead the press"; Defender — "excels at the basics of defending, including maintaining the defensive line." — [FIFAUTeam, FC 25 Player Roles](https://fifauteam.com/fc-25-player-roles)
- Non-EA mechanism with numbers (Konami, PES side, US patent granted 2012, priority 2008): on a "press defense" instruction the game selects one or more defenders ("For example, a player object 46 located closest to the ball object 48... may be selected"), who first "mark the player... while keeping a predetermined distance or longer" (first operation mode) for a reference period T, then "approach the ball object 48 and try to take the ball" (second mode). T is read from a table T1 < T2 < T3 < T4 < T5 "in units of 1/60th of a second" indexed by score difference: longer when winning (T4 one goal up, T5 two or more), shorter when losing (T2, T1). Early switch to the tackle mode occurs "if the number of player objects 46 selected... is equal to or larger than a predetermined number", and T may also scale with CPU level or team ability. — [US 8,333,663 B2, Konami](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8333663)

### Inferences
- The FIFA 10-14 engagement count is effectively "one plus one": the human's player plus at most one AI presser (the closest AI defender), and in FIFA 12+ that presser is a shadow, not a tackler. Everyone else is a holder whose default is to stay in his formation zone; nothing in EA's text describes a third automatic presser outside the High Pressure/Team Press styles.
- The holder archetype EA describes is: goal-side of his man or his zone, facing the ball, showing the carrier infield (wide players) or onto the weak foot, intercepting passing lanes, and tackling only on proximity. That is a cover-shadow by construction (standing between carrier and the protected area) rather than a computed shadow cone.
- Konami's two-mode press (stand off at ≥ threshold distance for T frames, then close) is the closest documented analogue of FIFA 12's "invisible wall" contain; FIFA never published its distance or any duration.

### Gaps
- No EA source quantifies the contain stand-off distance, the auto-tackle trigger radius, or how FIFA 12 chose the second man when two AI defenders were equidistant (CBs were evidently *not* protected: see KQ4).
- No EA source describes a formal cover-shadow or passing-lane geometry for holders; the behaviour is described only in football terms.

## Key Question 3 — How does the back line decide its height, and when does it step up (offside trap) or drop (runner in behind, carrier facing forward with space)?

### Takeaway
In FIFA 09-13 line height was set by three levers: the formation's base positions (editable per player, including dropping one defender deeper), the team Mentality (Ultra-defensive to Ultra-attacking moves the whole team as a unit), and the Custom Tactics "Defender Line" toggle Cover vs Offside Trap, with Offside Trap also available as a one-shot D-pad surge; in-match the AI line tracked the ball and the human was expected to drop CBs manually when a counter started. FC 25 (2024) finally published the rule set: four Defensive Approaches tie line height (1-100) to run tracking (Deep: line drops with runs; Aggressive: never drops, steps up to the highest legal line, traps).

Aggression A team's approach to tackling and pressure on the ball carrier. Team Width How far across the pitch a team shifts towards the ball. Defender Line The shape and strategy (e.g., offside trap vs. defensive depth) of the defensive line." Presets: "Counter Attack, High Pressure, Possession, and Long Ball". — [FIFA 09 Xbox 360 manual (PDF)](https://videogamemanual.com/xbox360/FIFA%2009.pdf)

- FIFA 19 (2018) Depth: "1 to 3 / 10 [Deep]... - FIFA 20 (2019) D-pad Offside Trap: "This tactic tells your defenders to quickly push up as a unit, with the goal of catching an attacker offside... Note how the defenders push up to form a straight vertical line." It is "the only D-Pad Tactic that doesn't need to be deactivated... a temporary action". — [EA Pitch Notes, D-Pad Tactics Deep Dive](https://ea.com/news/pitch-notes-d-pad-tactics)
- FC 25 (2024) Defensive Approach rule set: "Deep... Line Height values: 1 - 30, default: 25, Run Tracking: line will drop with runs, Pressure: none. - Goalkeeper-defender interplay (only where it bears on line height): "keeping the space between your back line and your goalkeeper to a minimum when you're defending... If there's a big gap between your defenders and your goal then you're just asking for a through ball over the top." — [EA Backpage, FIFA 13 Defend Against Pace](https://ea.com/en-gb/news/fifa-13-defend-against-pace). FIFA 10 added a keeper "ability to scramble back to the line and scoop the ball off the line in emergency situations." — [GameSpot, FIFA 10 Impressions](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/fifa-10-impressions/1100-6211111)

### Inferences
- FIFA 10-13 had no published in-match "drop because the carrier faces forward with space" rule; the line's dynamic height came from (i) the Pressure slider (how high the team starts pressing, which carries the line with it), (ii) the mentality shift, and (iii) Cover vs Offside Trap, with the runner-in-behind problem handled by *Cover* (full-back tucks in, line sits deeper) and otherwise by the human dropping a CB. The repeated Backpage advice to retreat manually suggests the AI line's drop on transition was slower than players wanted.
- The FC 25 table is the first explicit statement of the trade-off FIFA has always made implicitly: the higher the line, the less it tracks runners in behind, and the more it steps up to hold the offside line.
- "Line Length" (FIFA 12-14 slider) is the compactness lever between lines; low values make the forwards' defensive slots sit close to the midfield, which is how slider communities got "defenders always back" without touching speed.

### Gaps
- No FIFA 10-12 source gives metres for line height, the trap trigger (e.g. pass-wind-up detection), or the drop rate when a carrier faces forward.
- No source explains whether the FIFA 10-13 AI line stepped up automatically without the D-pad Offside Trap when set to "Offside Trap" in Custom Tactics, beyond "they will push up the field".

## Key Question 4 — How are runners tracked: who picks up a runner, handover rules, and what stops two defenders following the same runner?

### Takeaway
In FIFA 10-13 an unengaged AI centre-back "automatically tracks the run" of the second striker, and the Marking attribute governs how well AI players do this off the ball; the chief documented failure was that Teammate Contain would make that same CB "abandon his man", so EA's advice was to make a midfielder the presser. FIFA 14 moved from nearest-opponent marking to a multi-frame "most dangerous player" assignment to reduce double-covering and open men; FIFA 19+ Width settings decide whether far-side players mark tightly or pinch in to cover. Konami's marking patent (non-EA) shows one concrete handover test: switch a marker from goal-side shadowing to intercepting only if the carrier is facing the target within a reference angle *and* no teammate is already within a reference distance of the target-to-goal line.

### Cited Findings
- FIFA 10 (2009): "Defenders also do a better job of covering the field, thanks to some reworking of the threat analysis system–if there's a gap somewhere behind the defenders, then they won't switch out their positions." and "position priority enable defenders to multitask so dangerous spaces left open by teammates out of position are covered." — [GameSpot, FIFA 10 Impressions](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/fifa-10-impressions/1100-6211111); [FIFPlay, FIFA 10 Features](https://www.fifplay.com/fifa-10-features)
- FIFA 11 (2010) Be A Pro marking indicator: "A red ring appears underneath any player with open room that you should be marking. Close in on them as soon as possible!" — [FIFA 11 (EUR) PSP manual](https://ia601607.us.archive.org/13/items/SonyPSPManualsv1.2/FIFA%2011%20%28EUR%29.pdf)
- FIFA 11 (2010) covering swap behaviour (attacking phase, but shows the slot-cover logic): "a central midfielder could make a run wide, the wide player would move into the centre to cover him and then the midfielder would stay out wide for a few moments until they get the chance to swap back." — [EA, FIFA 11: Inside Personality+, 22 Jul 2010](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/fifa-11-inside-personality)
- FIFA 12 (2011) Marking attribute: "This determines your player's ability to track and defend against an opposing player. AI players will do this off the ball, and when you're in control of a defender their Marking stat will affect how good they are at containing." — [EA Backpage, FIFA 12 Attributes Guide](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/the-backpage-fifa-12-attributes-guide)
- FIFA 12 (2012) automatic run tracking by the free CB, and how contain breaks it: "there's a striker running at you with the ball and you're containing him with one of your centre-backs. The other centre-back is automatically tracking the run of his strike partner. As the player with the ball gets closer to your goal you panic and hold secondary contain, and so your other centre-back comes across to help out… leaving the striker's team-mate totally free for an easy tap in." Also the drag-out exploit: "Usually my opponent will be tracking him using contain and so his centre-back will often follow my striker up the pitch, leaving a hole behind him in the worst place possible." — [EA Backpage, FIFA 12 – Slow Down. Win More](https://www.ea.com/en-au/news/backpage-fifa12-back-to-basics)
- FIFA 13 (2013): "The absolute worst time to do this is when you have an opposition player running at two centre-backs who already have a couple of strikers for company… as soon as you hit Teammate Contain one of them will abandon his man... If it's a centre-back then make sure he doesn't already have a man to mark... Ideally you want one of the midfielders to apply pressure on the ball so that you can take manual control of a central defender." Manual tracking rule: "track their runs by staying in line between them and the goal at all times." — [EA Backpage, FIFA 13 Defending Tips](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/fifa-13-defending-tips-the-backpage); [FIFA 13 Defend Against Pace](https://ea.com/en-gb/news/fifa-13-defend-against-pace)
- FIFA 14 (2013): "Rather than always covering the player closest to them, AI defenders will better position themselves to reduce the chance of someone being left wide-open. Defensive decision-making will be based on multiple frames enabling defenders to continually analyze the play to search for the most dangerous player. This will improve run tracking and man marking." — [EA, FIFA 14 Teammate Intelligence](https://www.ea.com/news/fifa-14-teammate-intelligence)
- FIFA 14 (2014) release-to-recover: "If you're using secondary contain and notice a CB stepping out, just release the secondary contain button and try again when a different defending player is closer to the ball than your centre-backs." — [EA Backpage, FIFA 14 How Not To Defend](https://www.ea.com/en-gb/news/fifa-14-tips-how-not-to-defend)
- FIFA 19 (2018) Width and marking: Narrow (1-3): "your players will pinch in on the far side to cover teammates and allow for ball side pressure"; Balanced (4-7): "You may find that one-to-one situations are more common when you don't have the ball"; Wide (8-10): "The team will not pinch as much. Players on the far side will mark opponents tightly, sacrificing cover in favor of making it difficult for the opposition to pull them out of shape." — [FIFPlay, FIFA 19 Tactics](https://www.fifplay.com/fifa-19-tactics/)
- FIFA 20 (2019) Overload Ball Side: "your team shape will try to shift towards the ball, potentially ignoring defensive Player Instructions and marked opposition players." — [EA Pitch Notes, D-Pad Tactics Deep Dive](https://ea.com/news/pitch-notes-d-pad-tactics)
- FC 25 (Jan 2025): "Defenders will focus more on man marking when defending counter attacks starting from corners." — [EA, FC 25 Gameplay Refresh Update](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/fc-25-pitch-notes-gameplay-refresh-update)
- Non-EA mechanism (Konami, US patent granted 2013, priority 2007): a marker has two control modes — first mode positions him "based on a position of the marking target player character and a predetermined position" (goal-side shadowing), second mode "based on the position of the marking target player character and a position of a moving object or a position of a player character holding the moving object" (intercept). The switch happens when "the angle (θ) between the representative direction of the ball holding player object and the direction from the position (B) of the ball holding player object to the position (T) of the marking target player object is equal to or smaller than a predetermined reference angle (θa)" AND, checking cover, whether "the length (L) of a normal from the position (F) of the teammate player object to the straight line [from the target to the own goal] is equal to or shorter than a predetermined reference distance (La)" — i.e. only step to intercept if someone else already covers the goal-side lane. "To mark" is defined as "keeping the distance to the specific opponent player shorter than a predetermined distance." — [US 8,597,094 B2, Konami](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8597094)
- Non-EA mechanism (Konami, US patent 2001): off-ball defenders are given "follow level 1/2/3" by where the ball is ("whether the ball is kept in the defender's area... midfield... [or attack]"), with an explicit man-to-man override ("If there is a man-to-man order, a man-to-man command is set"), and "the positions of the defensive halfs are set in relation to the position of the ball such that the defensive halfs progressively approach the ball when the ball is within the alliance area". — [US 6,280,323, Konami](https://uspto.report/patent/grant/6280323)

### Inferences
- FIFA 10-13's documented runner rule is slot-based with a single owner: the free centre-back nearest the runner tracks him; the only way a second defender joined was the human's Teammate Contain, which EA repeatedly describes as the cause of unmarked strikers. That suggests the engine had no "protect the CB who owns a runner" exception until at least FIFA 14's "most dangerous player" re-assignment.
- "Multiple frames" in the FIFA 14 text reads as temporal smoothing/hysteresis of the mark target, i.e. the same fix a deterministic sim uses to stop two defenders flipping onto the same runner.
- Width settings are FIFA's zonal-vs-man dial: narrow = zonal pinch and cover, wide = man-tight on the far side.

### Gaps
- No FIFA 10-12 source states the handover rule between CBs, the distance at which a full-back passes an inside runner to a CB, or any tie-break when two defenders are equidistant from a runner.
- No EA description of a "runner in behind" trigger (e.g. attacker's velocity vector toward goal) exists for FIFA 10-14; FC 25's run-tracking policy is the only explicit statement and is tied to line height, not to individual defenders.

## Key Question 5 — What did players criticise about FIFA 10-12 defensive teammate AI, and what did EA change?

### Takeaway
The recurring FIFA 09-12 complaint was asymmetry: the user's AI teammates were passive ("you basically have to control every player"), stood off, did not intercept, and were dragged out of shape by Secondary Press, while CPU teams defended aggressively and "played people onside instantly"; FIFA 12's Tactical Defending answered the shape-destroying auto-press (and was criticised in turn for the "invisible wall" and defensive lines making "senseless vertical runs"), FIFA 13 added Teammate Contain guidance, and FIFA 14 rewrote marking assignment and added contextual defensive pressure. Later EA titles continued to tune the same asymmetry (FIFA 21 Team Press affects "only your AI teammates"; FC 25 made competitive-mode AI defenders mark "less tightly" to force manual defending).

### Inferences
- The human-side/CPU asymmetry is partly design, not only a bug: EA's FIFA 21 and FC 25 notes show that pressing tactics act on AI teammates only and that human-side AI marking is intentionally loosened in competitive modes so the user must defend manually. - The FIFA 12 "defensive line making senseless vertical runs" complaint is consistent with a line that followed the ball's depth (Pressure/line-height logic) rather than a runner-aware drop, which FC 25 later made explicit and tunable.

## Key Question 6 — Team-level pressure settings and player instructions (what each lever does)

### Takeaway
FIFA 09-13 Custom Tactics exposed four defence sliders (Pressure = where pressing starts, Aggression = tackle/press intensity incl. "double" second-man pressing, Team Width = how far the block shifts to the ball side, Defender Line = Cover vs Offside Trap) on a 0-100 scale, plus D-pad styles (High Pressure persistent, Team Pressing contextual, Offside Trap one-shot, mentality unit-shift); FIFA 15+ (labelled) replaced some of this with per-player instructions (Stay back while attacking, Come back on defence/Stay forward, Cover centre/wing) and FIFA 19+ with five Defensive Styles and Width/Depth 1-10 bands, whose official text contains the only published numbers.

### Cited Findings
- FIFA 09-13 slider definitions (official, FIFA 09 manual): Pressure "How high up the pitch players start to pressure the opposition"; Aggression "A team's approach to tackling and pressure on the ball carrier"; Team Width "How far across the pitch a team shifts towards the ball"; Defender Line "The shape and strategy (e.g., offside trap vs. defensive depth) of the defensive line." — [FIFA 09 Xbox 360 manual](https://videogamemanual.com/xbox360/FIFA%2009.pdf)

- FIFA 11 (2010) controls: "Press A button (press and hold); Jockey < button (tap and then hold); Call secondary defender < button (press and hold)" and in Be A Pro "Defensive Pressure (when defending)". — [FIFA 11 (EUR) PSP manual](https://ia601607.us.archive.org/13/items/SonyPSPManualsv1.2/FIFA%2011%20%28EUR%29.pdf)
- FIFA 12 (2011) D-pad: "High Pressure is a style of play and, once selected, stays on until you choose to do something else. - FIFA 20 (2019) Drop Back: "attempts to keep your formation shape when you lose the ball and players will stick to their positions unless you manually control them"; Constant Pressure: "every player pressing including strikers and wingers." — [Goal.com, FIFA 20 custom tactics](https://www.goal.com/en/news/fifa-20-custom-tactics-complete-guide/1y14i5zedovsi1jgydmgc145o4)
- FIFA 21+ (2020-2023) player instructions (fan guides; EA's instruction text is in-game only): "Stay Back While Attacking" stops forward runs when in possession; "Come Back on Defence" makes strikers/wingers track back without exception; "Basic Defence Support" returns only when needed; "Stay Forward" does not drop back. — [FIFAUTeam, FIFA 23 Player Instructions Guide](https://fifauteam.com/fifa-23-player-instructions-guide/); interaction with work rate: "Setting this Center Mid to 'stay back while attacking' will cause him to delay forward runs during an attack and stay in the midfield, but it won't stop him from pushing up into the attack." — [FIFAUTeam, FIFA 23 Work Rates](https://fifauteam.com/fifa-23-work-rates/)
- FIFA 20 (2019) priority order: "D-Pad Tactics have a higher priority than custom tactics and player instructions." — [FIFAUTeam, FIFA 20 D-Pad Tactics Guide](https://fifauteam.com/fifa-20-d-pad-tactics-guide); and Fullback Positioning slider "overwriting the custom tactic command" — [r/EASportsFC sliders thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/EASportsFC/comments/5kxryd/lets_discuss_sliders)
- FC 25 (2024) removed "Defensive Width, Pressure" as separate sliders and "Transformed Defensive Style, Defensive Depth" into the four Defensive Approaches. — [EA, FC 25 FC IQ Deep Dive](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)

### Inferences
- Across all eras the FIFA levers decompose cleanly into: a pressing *start line* (Pressure/Depth), a pressing *intensity and head-count* (Aggression contain/press/double; later Styles), a *ball-side shift* amount (Team Width/Overload Ball Side), a *line policy* (Cover vs Trap; later Approach), and *per-player return rules* (work rate, then instructions). A FIFA-faithful sim needs each of these as an independent parameter.
- Priority order observed in EA's titles: D-pad/quick tactic > custom tactic > player instruction > work-rate default.

### Gaps
- No FIFA 10-12 official text was retrieved for the in-game slider tooltips (only the FIFA 09 manual definitions, which the FIFA 12 community confirms were unchanged in category).
- Work-rate and player-instruction descriptions for FIFA 15-23 come from fan sites, not EA.

## Key Question 7 — What does FIFA do that the reader's sim (slots with phases, one presser + one cover with hysteresis, man-marks, committed-keeper rule, fixed-height back line, 70% cruise for uninvolved) is still missing, especially for recovery runs?

### Takeaway
Measured against the sourced FIFA behaviour, the sim lacks five things: a per-player threat-driven urgency state (not a flat 70% cruise), per-player defensive work-rate zones that decide who comes back and how deep, a time-boxed counter-press window (~7 s) that defines what the nearest players do before the team re-forms, a zone-handover rule for the presser, and a line-height policy coupled to run tracking and ball-side pinch. The 70% cruise is the direct cause of slow forwards: FIFA's equivalent is "relaxed until threatened", with medium/high-DWR forwards treated as threatened the moment the ball is lost behind them.

### Cited Findings
(All findings below are cited in KQ1-6; the URLs are repeated for the report writer's convenience.)
- Urgency is threat-based and per player: "When players aren't involved in the action, they're a little more relaxed, but as soon as they see a threatening position, they'll run with a lot more urgency." — [GameSpot, FIFA 10 Impressions](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/fifa-10-impressions/1100-6211111)
- Who comes back is a work-rate property, with EA's own examples of a forward who does (Rooney) and one who does not (Berbatov). — [Game Informer, FIFA 11](https://gameinformer.com/games/fifa_soccer_2011/b/ps3/archive/2010/06/09/fifa-11%3A-the-players-take-the-pitch.aspx); zones per DWR band — [FIFAUTeam, FIFA 23 Work Rates](https://fifauteam.com/fifa-23-work-rates/)
- Counter-press window: "press the ball for approximately 7 seconds... your players could be out of position if you don't win it back during this time." — [FIFPlay, FIFA 19 Tactics](https://www.fifplay.com/fifa-19-tactics/); Team Press capped at 20 s, AI teammates only, stamina ×3 — [EA, FIFA 21 TU5](https://www.ea.com/games/fifa/pitch-notes/news/fifa-21-pitch-notes-title-update-5)

### Inferences (design gaps, in the order the sources suggest they matter)
1. Replace the flat 70% cruise with a per-player urgency flag computed from threat: a player is "threatened" on turnover if the ball is between him and his own goal, if his defensive slot is goal-side of the ball and unoccupied, or if he owns a runner. Threatened players run at (or near) sprint; only genuinely uninvolved players cruise. This is the FIFA 10 urgency system and is the direct fix for forwards jogging back.
2. Add a Defensive Work Rate per player (Low/Medium/High) that (a) sets how deep his defensive slot sits (FIFA 23 bands: High → own third/box, Medium → midfield to edge of own box, Low → no deeper than the attacking midfield) and (b) gates whether he is ever marked "threatened" on a turnover. Low-DWR forwards staying high is correct FIFA behaviour and should not count against the recovery metric.
3. Add a counter-press window (default ≈ 7 s from FIFA 19+, tunable; FIFA 12's "Team Pressing" is the qualitative ancestor) during which the nearest one or two players to the ball press/shadow while everyone else recovers to shape; when it expires, the pressers also fall back to their slots. Charge stamina at a multiplier (FIFA 21: ×3) while the window is active so it self-limits.
4. Give the presser a zone-handover rule: he is released (and the nearest player in the new zone takes over) when the carrier leaves his lateral/longitudinal zone, instead of following across the pitch. This directly reduces the "swarming is low but one player drags" failure that FIFA 11 had.
5. Make the second man a shadow ("cover off"), not a tackler, and never pull a centre-back who currently owns a runner (the FIFA 12-14 failure EA documented); prefer a midfielder as presser when the ball is in the middle third.
6. Couple the back line to a run-tracking policy and a mentality offset: at Deep settings the line drops with any runner in behind; at High/Aggressive it holds and steps up; under Cover the ball-far full-back tucks inside when a CB is beaten; the whole block shifts toward the ball side by an amount set by Width.
7. Reassign marks by "most dangerous" with temporal smoothing (FIFA 14) rather than nearest-opponent, which is what stops two defenders converging on one runner.

### Gaps
- None of the above thresholds (urgency trigger distances, sprint fraction, zone sizes, 7-s window, ×3 stamina) are documented for FIFA 10-12 specifically; the numbers available are FIFA 19-FC 25 and must be treated as later-era calibration, not FIFA 10-12 fact.
- No EA patent on soccer defensive AI was found in Google Patents/USPTO searches; the EA patents surfaced concern hockey AI emulation ([US 10,839,215](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10839215B2/en)), distributed rule-set training ([US 11,110,353](https://patents.justia.com/patent/11110353)), real-data replay ([US 2020/0038757](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/20200038757)) and small-sided slot assignment ([US 2026/0295440](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20260295440)); none specify defensive marking or pressing rules.
- No source quantifies FIFA's goalkeeper-defender spacing beyond the qualitative "keep the space between your back line and your goalkeeper to a minimum."
