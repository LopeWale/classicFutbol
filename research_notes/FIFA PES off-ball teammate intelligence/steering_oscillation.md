# Eliminating steering oscillation (side flips / dithering / "reciprocal dance") in locomotion and local avoidance

Scope note for the report writer: this covers how game AI, crowd simulation and robotics suppress frame-to-frame lateral steering flips, with formulas and parameter values aimed at a deterministic fixed-point 60 Hz football sim that already uses a DetourCrowd-style sampled reciprocal velocity selection (17 samples x 3 adaptive levels, Detour weights, 1 s horizon), a first-order low-pass on the AI velocity command, a two-regime acceleration cap and a speed-dependent turn-rate cap. The sim's metric is "side flips per player-minute" (lateral component of velocity change relative to previous heading changing sign on consecutive ticks while above 0.02 m/s).

## Key Question 1 — Velocity-obstacle family: why RVO/HRVO/ORCA and sampled methods oscillate, and the standard remedies

### Takeaway
Plain velocity obstacles oscillate because each agent assumes the other will not react; RVO fixes the symmetric head-on by having each agent take half the avoidance effort, but still produces the "reciprocal dance" when the two agents disagree on the passing side; HRVO fixes the side disagreement geometrically (asymmetric cone, apex shifted, side chosen from the preferred/current velocity), ORCA fixes it with a convex half-plane per neighbour ("v + u/2") and a closest-to-preferred-velocity linear program, and sampled methods (Detour) oscillate for a third reason, discrete switching between candidate samples, which Detour's author says cannot be fully removed, only reduced by more samples and by penalties that bias toward the current velocity.

### Cited Findings

**Why VO oscillates and how RVO fixes the symmetric case**
- The RVO paper's authors "show that the standard approach generates oscillations when it is used for multi-agent navigation, and that RVO generates safe and oscillation-free motions"; under RVO "each agent takes responsibility for avoiding collision, so both robots were assumed to select a velocity outside the RVO induced by the other robot." — [van den Berg, Lin, Manocha, ICRA 2008 (paper PDF; text summarised via search because the host was blocked)](https://www.cs.unc.edu/~geom/RVO/icra2008.pdf); [RVO project page](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/RVO)
- Plain VO "assumes the other agent does not react", which is why "each keeps adjusting" and a reciprocal dance appears; RVO "assumes every other agent runs the same algorithm and will share the avoidance work." — [Knepper & Rus, RO-MAN 2012, pedestrian-inspired reciprocal avoidance (summary of RVO/HRVO)](https://rpal.cs.cornell.edu/docs/KneRus_ROMAN_2012.pdf)
- Formula (from the ICRA 2008 paper as commonly stated; I could not read the text this session, so treat the exact notation as unverified): RVO_B^A(v_B, v_A) = { v'_A | 2 v'_A − v_A ∈ VO_B^A(v_B) }, i.e. the agent picks the velocity that is the average of its current velocity and a velocity outside the VO ("half the effort"); a generalised effort factor α replaces the 1/2 and oscillation-freedom is proven for the reciprocal split. — [ICRA 2008 PDF](https://www.cs.unc.edu/~geom/RVO/icra2008.pdf) (location only; wording not retrieved)

**Why RVO still dances, and the HRVO remedy (side choice)**
- RVO "frequently causes agents to end up in a 'reciprocal dance' as they cannot reach agreement on which side to pass each other." HRVO "was introduced to eliminate direction ambiguity and with it the reciprocal dance." — [Snape, van den Berg, Guy, Manocha, "The Hybrid Reciprocal Velocity Obstacle", IEEE T-RO 2011 (PDF; summarised via search)](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/HRVO/HRVO-T-RO.pdf); [Douthwaite et al. review (White Rose eprints)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/141072/1/Manuscript.pdf)
- HRVO "enlarges the reciprocal velocity obstacle on the side that the robots should not pass by, swapping the RVO edge for a plain VO edge"; the authors "take into account uncertainty in radius, position, and velocity"; one review notes "the HRVO is not capable of guaranteeing that trajectories will be smooth." — [Douthwaite et al. review](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/141072/1/Manuscript.pdf); [HRVO project page](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/HRVO)
- HRVO's stated purpose: it "avoids oscillations by explicitly taking into account that the other robots" also react. — [snape/HRVO README](https://github.com/snape/HRVO)
- HRVO reference code (primary): the passing side is chosen from the **preferred** velocities: `if (det(relativePosition, prefVelocity_ - other->prefVelocity_) > 0.0F)` selects one apex branch (`apex_ = other->velocity_ + s * side1_ - ...`), else the other (`... + s * side2_ - ...`); overlapping agents use `0.5F * (other->velocity_ + velocity_)` as the apex. The `uncertaintyOffset_` is subtracted from the apex scaled by `(uncertaintyOffset_ * abs(relativePosition) / combinedRadius) * normalize(relativePosition)`, and in the overlapping branch it is added to a penetration term divided by `timeStep_`. Candidate velocities are stored in a multimap keyed by squared distance to `prefVelocity_` (`candidates_.insert(std::make_pair(absSq(prefVelocity_ - candidate.position_), candidate))`) and the first candidate that violates no HRVO is taken; the fallback is the candidate violating the highest-indexed obstacle. Velocity smoothing lives in `update()`: `dv = abs(newVelocity_ - velocity_)`; if `dv < maxAccel_ * timeStep_` then `velocity_ = newVelocity_`, else velocity moves toward `newVelocity_` by at most `maxAccel_ * timeStep_`. — [snape/HRVO src/Agent.cc](https://github.com/snape/HRVO/blob/main/src/Agent.cc)

**ORCA: half-plane with half responsibility, closest-to-preferred LP, no smoothing step**
- RVO2 (ORCA reference code, primary): `invTimeHorizon = 1.0F / timeHorizon_`; no-collision case `w = relativeVelocity - invTimeHorizon * relativePosition`; "Project on cut-off circle": `u = (combinedRadius * invTimeHorizon - wLength) * unitW`; otherwise "Project on legs" choosing left leg if `det(relativePosition, w) > 0.0F`, else right leg, with `u = (relativeVelocity * line.direction) * line.direction - relativeVelocity`; collision case "Project on cut-off circle of time timeStep" with `invTimeStep = 1.0F / timeStep`; the constraint line is `line.point = velocity_ + 0.5F * u` (each agent moves half of u). The new velocity is found by `linearProgram2(orcaLines_, maxSpeed_, prefVelocity_, false, newVelocity_)` ("Optimize closest point and inside circle"), with `linearProgram3(...)` as the dense/infeasible fallback. `update()` then sets `velocity_ = newVelocity_; position_ += velocity_ * timeStep;` with no acceleration limiting. — [snape/RVO2 src/Agent.cc](https://github.com/snape/RVO2/blob/main/src/Agent.cc)
- RVO2 example parameters: `sim->setTimeStep(0.25f)` and `sim->setAgentDefaults(15.0f, 10, 10.0f, 10.0f, 1.5f, 2.0f)` (neighborDist 15, maxNeighbors 10, timeHorizon 10 s, timeHorizonObst 10 s, radius 1.5, maxSpeed 2 in that argument order); preferred velocity is the unit vector to the goal (`goalVector = normalize(goalVector)` when |goal−pos|² > 1). — [RVO2 examples/Circle.cpp (v2.0.2)](https://github.com/snape/RVO2/blob/v2.0.2/examples/Circle.cpp); [RVO2 examples/Circle.cc (main)](https://github.com/snape/RVO2/blob/main/examples/Circle.cc)
- Smoothness under kinematic limits (Snape et al., IROS 2010, "Smooth and Collision-Free Navigation for Multiple Robots Under Differential-Drive Constraints"): the method "builds on optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA)", "guarantees both smoothness in the trajectories of the robots and locally collision-free paths", and "enlarges the radius used in navigation in a precise way, which provides additional maneuverability"; validated on iRobot Create robots. — [ORCA-DD project page](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ORCA-DD/); [ORCA-DD PDF](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ORCA-DD/ORCA-DD.pdf) (text not retrieved; host blocked)
- Limitation of VO-family methods stated in later work: "these methods are susceptible to measurement errors and often exhibit short-sighted behavior." — [Rutherford et al., NeurIPS 2024, related-work review](https://cdn.luneresearch.com/pdfs/neurips/2024/60e33039-1bbd-4b60-aea0-dd85bb8a7165.pdf)

**Formal "same side" (consistent orientation) constraint — the velocity-space version of per-pair side memory**
- ClearPath Type-II constraint: "A sufficient condition to avoid collisions among multiple agents is to ensure that each agent chooses a velocity that is on the same side of the line bisecting their RVO cone [BPS*08]. We have each agent pass on the side closest to its current velocity, imposed by: FVO_C(v) = ( φ(v, (v_A + v_B)/2, φ(v_A, v_B, p_AB^⊥) p_AB^⊥) ≥ 0 ). This is also a conservative formulation to guarantee collision-free motion." ClearPath then computes "a new, constraint satisfying velocity for each agent which minimizes the deviation from v_des." — [Guy et al., ClearPath, SCA 2009](http://gamma-web.iacs.umd.edu/CA/ClearPath.pdf)

**Asymmetric responsibility (right of way) as a deadlock/dance breaker**
- "Right of Way: Asymmetric Agent Interactions in Crowds" (Curtis & Manocha, The Visual Computer 2013) "modifies the optimization constraints in the velocity-based collision avoidance algorithm, ORCA, so that one agent can yield while the other keeps its course", providing "a well-disciplined mechanism for modeling asymmetric relationships between pedestrians"; it was applied to "two based on social forces and one on velocity obstacles" and "can be used ... to improve simulation results in several situations (such as flow through narrow passages)." — [Curtis dissertation record (UNC)](https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/concern/dissertations/sn009z16b); [paper PDF (core.ac.uk mirror; text not retrieved)](https://core.ac.uk/download/210598571.pdf)

**Passing-side conventions and topological commitment (robotics)**
- Reciprocity resolves symmetric cases without a unique rule: "social norms are the emergent behaviors from a time-efficient, reciprocal collision avoidance mechanism ... reciprocity does not require a unique set of navigation rules, since both the left-handed and the right-handed rules can resolve path conflicts." The learned CADRL policy "shows a preference to the right as two agents pass each other in a symmetrical test case", a preference "robust to a small offset in the initial condition", and a right-hand norm can be induced by penalising "states (configurations) with another agent approaching from the undesirable side." — [Chen, Everett, Liu, How, "Socially Aware Motion Planning with Deep RL", 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.08862)
- Topological view: "in a narrow hallway, two agents must either agree on passing from the left or the right side to avoid collisions"; the sign of the pairwise angular momentum "encod[es] the right and left passings respectively" and can be used "as a tool to monitor an emerging avoidance protocol (right/left passing)" and "to generate ... avoidance maneuvers in compliance with the preferences of the other agent and in consistency with previous behaviors of the agents." — [Mavrogiannis, Social Collision Avoidance via Topological Inference, 2020](https://chrismavrogiannis.com/pdfs/mavrogiannis2020socialcollisionavoidance.pdf)
- The winding-number planner "yields a robot trajectory ... that maximizes (on average) the winding numbers between the robot and human agents ... This corresponds to robot motion that avoids switching between passing sides as it approaches a human, expediting and facilitating collision avoidance." — [Mavrogiannis et al., "Winding Through: Crowd Navigation via Topological Invariance"](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.05084v4)

**Sampled methods (Detour) oscillate because of discrete sample switching**
- Detour's author Mikko Mononen (2012 mailing list): "the sampled velocity obstacle will always have some jitter, as the velocity is chosen from one of the samples. The more samples you have, the less jitter"; "the end of the path is especially nasty"; jitter appears with "crossing crowd units, moving units passing stationary ones, and choke points." — [recastnavigation Google Group thread "How to avoid agent jitter" (via search excerpt; host blocked)](https://groups.google.com/g/recastnavigation/c/daSIkbEiByo)
- Detour's velocity-bias terms (the "velBias"/"weightCurVel" family) are the sampled method's hysteresis: see Key Question 2 for the exact formulas.

**Force-based anticipatory models (power law) and their time-step sensitivity**
- Power law: "E(τ) = k/τ² e^(−τ/τ0)" where "k is a constant that sets the units for energy", force "F = −∇_r [k/τ² e^(−τ/τ0)]"; the intrinsic range estimate is "τ0 ≈ 3 s, which is consistent with previous research demonstrating an interaction time horizon of 2−4 s"; observed screening cut-offs t0 ≈ 1.4 s (dense bottleneck, ρ = 2.5 m⁻², u = 0.55 m/s) and 2.4 s (sparse outdoor, ρ = 0.27 m⁻², u = 0.86 m/s). — [Karamouzas, Skinner, Guy, PRL 113, 238701 (2014)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.1082); [APS full text](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.238701/fulltext)
- "Because the PowerLaw approach relies on explicit integration, like other existing force-based models used in crowd simulation, it needs very small time steps to provide collision-free motion. For very complex dense scenarios, such as Evacuation, the method produces collisions even at very small time steps (Δt < 5 ms)." ORCA "plans motions directly in velocity space ... solving a linear program for each agent." The implicit integrator uses backward Euler `(1/Δt) M (v_{n+1} − v_n) = f(x_n + v_{n+1} Δt, v_{n+1})`, which "has much better stability properties than forward Euler." — [Karamouzas, Sohre, Narain, Guy, "Implicit Crowds", SIGGRAPH 2017](https://motion.cs.umn.edu/pub/ImplicitTTC/implicit_crowds.pdf)
- Game AI Pro 2, ch. 19 "Guide to Anticipatory Collision Avoidance" (Guy & Karamouzas) describes "a driving force, which pushes an agent to its goal, and a collision avoiding force, which resolves collision with neighboring agents in an anticipatory fashion", and also covers ORCA "which works by defining a set of formal constraints on an agent's velocity." Chapter PDF is free on the publisher's site, which was blocked here. — [O'Reilly chapter page](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/game-ai-pro/9781482254792/K23980_C019.xhtml); [Taylor & Francis entry](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/9781482254808/chapters/10.1201/b18373-25); [Game AI Pro site](https://www.gameaipro.com/)

**Social-force oscillation is a damping problem, not (only) a numerical one**
- Linearised one-dimensional social force model reduces to "ξ̈ + r ξ̇ + ω0² ξ = 0, with r = 1/τ and ω0² = a/b"; "the system ... does not oscillate if ... r² − 4ω0² > 0 or (a/b) τ² < 1/4." Empirical τ ≈ 0.61 s / 0.54 s, simulation τ = 0.5 s; the common parameters "a = 2000 N and b = 0.08 m lead to oscillations." — [Chraibi, "Oscillating behavior within the social force model", 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.1133)
- Kretz: the SFM "in a special and one-dimensional case and around equilibrium reduces to the equations of the damped harmonic oscillator ... there are parts in parameter space in which there are not only just small oscillations, but where there are exactly no oscillations"; approaching a destination "a pedestrian oscillates forever" under some conditions, with "simple, pragmatic solutions". — [Kretz, "On Oscillations in the Social Force Model", Physica A 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.02566)
- Köster et al.: "Oscillations, collisions, and instabilities occur even for very small step sizes" in the Helbing–Molnár model; they propose "a very simple mollified version." — [Köster, Treml, Gödel, Phys. Rev. E 87, 063305 (2013)](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevE.87.063305)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** The three oscillation mechanisms need three different cures: (1) symmetric head-on → reciprocity (Detour's `vab = 2*vcand − vel − other.vel` already is RVO-style) plus a deterministic asymmetry (right-of-way/priority) when the geometry is exactly symmetric; (2) side disagreement → a per-pair committed side (HRVO's det-test on relative preferred velocity, ClearPath's "same side as current velocity", Mavrogiannis' winding sign) remembered across ticks; (3) discrete sample switching → penalise changing the lateral sign and bias the pattern toward the current velocity (Detour weightCurVel/velBias), or add a final continuous blend.
- **[inference]** The RVO2 "perfect symmetry" problem is normally broken with a tiny random perturbation of the preferred velocity; in a deterministic sim replace this with a deterministic tiebreak: lower player id, or the team/role priority of the Right-of-Way model, or ClearPath's "side closest to current velocity".
- **[inference]** ORCA's smoothness comes from the LP's continuity in its inputs (the half-planes move continuously with positions/velocities), so the chosen velocity does not jump unless a constraint becomes active/inactive; a sampled method cannot inherit this, which is why Detour needs explicit hysteresis terms.

## Key Question 2 — DetourCrowd specifics: nvel/dvel/vel, integrate(), separation, avoidance presets, adaptive sampling, community jitter fixes

### Takeaway
Detour computes a desired velocity `dvel` from the path corridor (optionally smoothed by `calcSmoothSteerDirection` and a separation force), picks `nvel` by minimising `penalty = weightDesVel·|vcand−dvel|/vmax + weightCurVel·|vcand−vel|/vmax + weightSide·side + weightToi·1/(0.1 + tmin/horizTime)` over an adaptive pattern centred at `velBias·dvel` with radius `vmax·(1−velBias)` halved per depth level, then `integrate()` rate-limits `vel` toward `nvel` by `maxAcceleration·dt`; quality presets differ only in divs/rings/depth (11/22/45/66 samples) and `velBias` (0.4 in dtCrowd::init, 0.5 in the demo).

### Cited Findings

**Agent fields (semantics of dvel, nvel, vel)**
- `float dvel[3]; ///< The desired velocity of the agent. Based on the current path, calculated from scratch each frame.` `float nvel[3]; ///< The desired velocity adjusted by obstacle avoidance, calculated from scratch each frame.` `float vel[3]; ///< The actual velocity of the agent. The change from nvel -> vel is constrained by max acceleration.` `float disp[3]; ///< A temporary value used to accumulate agent displacement during iterative collision resolution.` — [DetourCrowd.h](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Include/DetourCrowd.h)
- `dtCrowdAgentParams`: `maxAcceleration` "Maximum allowed acceleration", `collisionQueryRange` "Defines how close a collision element must be before it is considered for steering behaviors", `separationWeight` "How aggresive the agent manager should be at avoiding collisions with this agent", `obstacleAvoidanceType` "The index of the avoidance configuration to use for the agent. [Limits: 0 <= value <= #DT_CROWD_MAX_OBSTAVOIDANCE_PARAMS]"; `DT_CROWD_MAX_OBSTAVOIDANCE_PARAMS = 8`, `DT_CROWDAGENT_MAX_NEIGHBOURS = 6`, `DT_CROWDAGENT_MAX_CORNERS = 4`; flags `DT_CROWD_ANTICIPATE_TURNS = 1, DT_CROWD_OBSTACLE_AVOIDANCE = 2, DT_CROWD_SEPARATION = 4, DT_CROWD_OPTIMIZE_VIS = 8, DT_CROWD_OPTIMIZE_TOPO = 16`. — [DetourCrowd.h](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Include/DetourCrowd.h)

**Default avoidance parameters (dtCrowd::init)**
- `params->velBias = 0.4f; params->weightDesVel = 2.0f; params->weightCurVel = 0.75f; params->weightSide = 0.75f; params->weightToi = 2.5f; params->horizTime = 2.5f; params->gridSize = 33; params->adaptiveDivs = 7; params->adaptiveRings = 2; params->adaptiveDepth = 5;` set identically for every slot `i < DT_CROWD_MAX_OBSTAVOIDANCE_PARAMS`. — [DetourCrowd.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourCrowd.cpp)

**Penalty function (dtObstacleAvoidanceQuery::processSample)**
- `vpen = m_params.weightDesVel * (dtVdist2D(vcand, dvel) * m_invVmax)`; `vcpen = m_params.weightCurVel * (dtVdist2D(vcand, vel) * m_invVmax)`; per circle obstacle `side += dtClamp(dtMin(dtVdot2D(cir->dp,vab)*0.5f+0.5f, dtVdot2D(cir->np,vab)*2), 0.0f, 1.0f)`, then `side /= nside`, `spen = m_params.weightSide * side`; `tpen = m_params.weightToi * (1.0f/(0.1f+tmin*m_invHorizTime))`; `penalty = vpen + vcpen + spen + tpen`. The RVO term is `dtVsub(vab, vab, vel)` after `vab = 2*vcand`, i.e. vab = 2·vcand − vel − other.vel. `tmin` starts at `horizTime` and is lowered to the nearest positive time-of-hit. Early-out: `tThresold = (m_params.weightToi / minPen - 0.1f) * m_params.horizTime; if (tThresold - m_params.horizTime > -FLT_EPSILON) return minPenalty;`. — [DetourObstacleAvoidance.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourObstacleAvoidance.cpp)

**Adaptive sampling pattern (sampleVelocityAdaptive)**
- "Build sampling pattern aligned to desired velocity." `nd = dtClamp(ndivs, 1, DT_MAX_PATTERN_DIVS)`; ring radius `r = (float)(nr-j)/(float)nr`; "Always add sample at zero"; search origin `dtVset(res, dvel[0] * m_params.velBias, 0, dvel[2] * m_params.velBias)`; initial radius `cr = vmax * (1.0f - m_params.velBias)`; after each depth pass the best sample becomes the new centre and `cr *= 0.5f`; final `dtVcopy(nvel, res)`. — [DetourObstacleAvoidance.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourObstacleAvoidance.cpp)
- In `dtCrowd::update` the adaptive sampler is always used: `bool adaptive = true;` → `sampleVelocityAdaptive(ag->npos, ag->params.radius, ag->desiredSpeed, ag->vel, ag->dvel, ag->nvel, params, vod)`; without the avoidance flag, `dtVcopy(ag->nvel, ag->dvel)`. — [DetourCrowd.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourCrowd.cpp)

**Desired velocity: steering direction, separation, slow-down**
- `if (ag->params.updateFlags & DT_CROWD_ANTICIPATE_TURNS) calcSmoothSteerDirection(ag, dvel); else calcStraightSteerDirection(ag, dvel);` where the smooth version blends the first two corners: `ip1 = dtMin(1, ag->ncorners-1)`, `dir[0] = dir0[0] - dir1[0]*len0*0.5f; dir[2] = dir0[2] - dir1[2]*len0*0.5f; dtVnormalize(dir);` and the straight version is `dtVsub(dir, &ag->cornerVerts[0], ag->npos); dir[1] = 0; dtVnormalize(dir);`. — [DetourCrowd.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourCrowd.cpp)
- Slow-down near the goal: `slowDownRadius = ag->params.radius*2; speedScale = getDistanceToGoal(ag, slowDownRadius) / slowDownRadius; dtVscale(dvel, dvel, ag->desiredSpeed * speedScale);`. — [DetourCrowd.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourCrowd.cpp)
- Separation: `separationDist = ag->params.collisionQueryRange; separationWeight = ag->params.separationWeight; weight = separationWeight * (1.0f - dtSqr(dist*invSeparationDist)); dtVmad(disp, disp, diff, weight/dist); w += 1.0f;` then `dtVmad(dvel, dvel, disp, 1.0f/w);` and the speed is clamped to `desiredSpeed`. — [DetourCrowd.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourCrowd.cpp)

**integrate(): first-order rate limit from nvel to vel**
- `maxDelta = ag->params.maxAcceleration * dt; dtVsub(dv, ag->nvel, ag->vel); ds = dtVlen(dv); if (ds > maxDelta) dtVscale(dv, dv, maxDelta/ds); dtVadd(ag->vel, ag->vel, dv);` then `if (dtVlen(ag->vel) > 0.0001f) dtVmad(ag->npos, ag->npos, ag->vel, dt); else dtVset(ag->vel,0,0,0);`. There is no direct `vel = nvel` assignment. — [DetourCrowd.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourCrowd.cpp)
- Collision resolution: `COLLISION_RESOLVE_FACTOR = 0.7f`, `for (int iter = 0; iter < 4; ++iter)`, `pen = (1.0f/dist) * (pen*0.5f) * COLLISION_RESOLVE_FACTOR`, `pen = 0.01f` only when `dist < 0.0001f`, averaged with `iw = 1.0f / w`, applied via `dtVadd(ag->npos, ag->npos, ag->disp)`. — [DetourCrowd.cpp](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Source/DetourCrowd.cpp)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** Sample counts per depth level are `1 + divs·rings` (centre sample plus the rings): 5·2+1 = 11, 7·2+1 = 15 (×3 = 45), 7·3+1 = 22 (×3 = 66), matching the demo's comments. The sim's "17 samples × 3 levels" therefore corresponds to 8 divs × 2 rings (+1) and lies between Detour's "Good" and "High".
- **[inference]** The two Detour knobs that act as hysteresis are `weightCurVel` (continuous penalty for leaving the current velocity) and `velBias` (shrinks the search disc to radius `vmax(1−velBias)` around `velBias·dvel`); raising either reduces how far a single tick can jump, but neither penalises a *lateral sign change* specifically, which is why side flips survive at any sample count.
- **[inference]** `horizTime` enters the ToI penalty as `1/(0.1 + tmin/horizTime)`; with the sim's 1 s horizon a 0.1 s threat has penalty ≈ weightToi/0.2 = 5·weightToi, a 0.5 s threat ≈ 1.67·weightToi, so threats far inside the horizon dominate `vpen+vcpen` (which are ≤ weightDesVel+weightCurVel = 2.75 in Detour units); near-symmetric threats therefore make the minimum jump between mirror-image samples. A lateral-sign penalty of the order of `weightSide` (0.75) to `weightCurVel` (0.75) is the natural scale to try first.
- **[inference]** Detour's `slowDownRadius = 2·radius` is tuned for 3.5 m/s walkers with maxAcceleration 8 m/s²; a sprinting footballer at 8–9 m/s needs a stopping-distance based slow radius (v²/(2a), see Key Question 3) or it will overshoot and oscillate around static targets.

- Detour has no `updateAgentVelocity` function; the smoothing is entirely `integrate()` (rate limit) plus the sampler weights. Unreal's `FCrowdAvoidanceConfig` numeric defaults and its Low/Medium/Good/High preset table were not retrievable (docs host blocked; engine source requires login).
- The full text of Mononen's thread (any concrete parameter advice) was not retrievable; only the quoted sentences from the search excerpt are available.

## Key Question 3 — Classic steering literature: arrival, dead-zones, force smoothing, turn limits, "don't re-plan every frame", oscillation around a seek target

### Takeaway
The classic texts all reach the same fixes: ramp speed down inside a slowing radius (Reynolds/Buckland/Millington arrival), stop steering inside a target radius (Millington `targetRadius`), bound force and speed (`truncate(max_force)`, `max_speed`), choose the avoidance side once from a nearest-approach prediction (Reynolds/OpenSteer `steerToAvoidNeighbors`), average the displayed heading over ~10 frames (Buckland/Robin Green), and keep paths stable rather than re-planning while avoiding (Hitman ADL).

### Cited Findings

**Arrival behaviour (slowing radius, ramped speed)**
- Reynolds' arrival as commonly quoted: `target_offset = target - position; distance = length(target_offset); ramped_speed = max_speed * (distance / slowing_distance); clipped_speed = minimum(ramped_speed, max_speed); desired_velocity = (clipped_speed / distance) * target_offset; steering = desired_velocity - velocity`. — [Game Dev StackExchange answer quoting Reynolds' arrival](https://gamedev.stackexchange.com/questions/44400/arrive-steering-behavior); original: [Reynolds, "Steering Behaviors For Autonomous Characters", GDC 1999 (PDF; host blocked)](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/papers/1999/gdc99steer.pdf)
- A lecture derivation sets the slowing distance from kinematics: slowing distance = (max speed)² / (2 · max acceleration), then scales speed by distance/slowing distance (lecture's derivation, not Reynolds' text). — [Charles University AI for games lecture 4](https://ksvi.mff.cuni.cz/~dingle/2025-6/ai_games/lecture_4.pdf)
- Buckland's Arrive: `const double DecelerationTweaker = 0.3; double speed = dist / ((double)deceleration * DecelerationTweaker); speed = min(speed, m_pVehicle->MaxSpeed()); Vector2D DesiredVelocity = ToTarget * speed / dist; return (DesiredVelocity - m_pVehicle->Velocity());` with `enum Deceleration{slow = 3, normal = 2, fast = 1};`. — [Buckland Chapter 3 SteeringBehaviors.cpp (book source mirror)](https://github.com/wangchen/Programming-Game-AI-by-Example-src/blob/master/Buckland_Chapter3-Steering%20Behaviors/SteeringBehaviors.cpp); [SteeringBehaviors.h](https://github.com/wangchen/Programming-Game-AI-by-Example-src/blob/master/Buckland_Chapter3-Steering%20Behaviors/SteeringBehaviors.h)
- Millington's Arrive: "Seek ... If the character arrives at the target, it will overshoot, reverse, and oscillate through the target, or it will more likely orbit around the target without getting closer." Pseudocode: `targetRadius` ("radius for arriving at the target"), `slowRadius` ("radius for beginning to slow down"), `timeToTarget = 0.1`; `if distance < targetRadius: return None`; `if distance > slowRadius: targetSpeed = maxSpeed else targetSpeed = maxSpeed * distance / slowRadius`; `steering.linear = targetVelocity - character.velocity; steering.linear /= timeToTarget`; clip to `maxAcceleration`. — [Millington & Funge, AI for Games 2nd ed., §3.3.4 (PDF mirror)](https://dl.booksee.org/genesis/826000/3dd67e19636fe692b294142e3b1d014b/_as/%255BIan_Millington,_John_Funge%255D_Artificial_Intelligen%28BookSee.org%29.pdf); [AI for Games 3rd ed. preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781351053297_A36789489/preview-9781351053297_A36789489.pdf)
- Seek's overshoot: "Amount of overshoot determined by ratio of maxSpeed to maximum force applied"; Arrive "Decelerates linearly" (worked table: max speed 4, DECELERATION 2: speeds 4,4,4,3.5,3,2.5,2,1.5,1,0.5,0 as distance falls 10→0). — [WPI IMGD 4000 movement slides](http://web.cs.wpi.edu/~imgd4000/d16/slides/movement.pdf)
- Oscillation around an Arrive target in practice: "If your mass is too high or your acceleration is too low the object will oscillate. The object won't have the power it needs to stop its self before overshooting the target. This can be solved by increasing the acceleration available for stopping, or by increasing the stopping distance." — [Game Dev StackExchange](https://gamedev.stackexchange.com/questions/44400/arrive-steering-behavior)

**Vehicle model, force/speed truncation**
- Seek = `desired = truncate(target - position, maxSpeed); return desired - velocity`; agent integration `v1 = trim(v0 + Δt·f0/m, smax); p1 = p0 + Δt·v1; f = trim(smax·unit(q − p) − v, fmax)`. — [WPI slides](http://web.cs.wpi.edu/~imgd4000/d16/slides/movement.pdf); [Goldsmiths Ludic Computing lecture 9](http://ccg.doc.gold.ac.uk/ccg_old/teaching/ludic_computing/ludic9.pdf)
- Buckland's params: `SteeringForce 2.0`, `MaxSpeed 150.0`, `VehicleMass 1.0`, `SeparationWeight 1.0`, `ObstacleAvoidanceWeight 10.0`, `ViewDistance 50.0`, `MinDetectionBoxLength 40.0`; prioritized-dithering probabilities `prObstacleAvoidance 0.5`, `prWallAvoidance 0.5`, `prSeparation 0.2`, `prAlignment 0.3`, `prCohesion 0.6`, `prWander 0.8`, `prSeek 0.8`, `prFlee 0.6`, `prEvade 1.0`, `prHide 0.8`, `prArrive 0.5`; `NumSamplesForSmoothing 10`. — [Buckland params.ini](https://github.com/wangchen/Programming-Game-AI-by-Example-src/blob/master/Buckland_Chapter3-Steering%20Behaviors/params.ini)

**Heading smoothing (Buckland / Robin Green)**
- "Sometimes a vehicle can twitch or jitter slightly when it finds itself in a situation with conflicting responses from different behaviors ... One simple alternative suggested by Robin Green of Sony is to decouple the heading from the velocity vector and to average its value over several update steps. The average value is used by the world transform function in the render call." — [Buckland ch.3 "Smoothing" (reader notes quoting the book)](http://lc1995.github.io/2018/03/13/Game%20Ai%20Chapter%203/); [WPI slides "Judder Solution – Smoothing": "Decouple heading from velocity vector; Average heading over 'several' ticks; Tune number of ticks ... Not perfect solution, but produces adequate results at low cost"](http://web.cs.wpi.edu/~imgd4000/d16/slides/movement.pdf)
- Naive wander "Calculate random steering force each update step – Produces unpleasant 'jittery' behavior"; Reynolds' fix projects a circle ahead and steers to a target constrained on it. — [WPI slides](http://web.cs.wpi.edu/~imgd4000/d16/slides/movement.pdf)

**Choosing the avoidance side once (Reynolds / OpenSteer)**
- OpenSteer `steerToAvoidNeighbors`: threats are considered only if `(time >= 0) && (time < minTime)` with `minTime = minTimeToCollision` ("many frames into the future") and `computeNearestApproachPositions(other, time) < collisionDangerThreshold` where `collisionDangerThreshold = radius() * 2`. Side selection: `angle = 0.707f`; anti-parallel (`parallelness < -angle`): `offset = threatPositionAtNearestApproach - position(); sideDot = offset.dot(side()); steer = (sideDot > 0) ? -1.0f : 1.0f;`; parallel (`parallelness > angle`): same rule on `threat->position() - position()`; perpendicular: `if (threat->speed() <= speed()) { sideDot = side().dot(threat->velocity()); ... }` else steer stays 0; output `return side() * steer;`. `predictNearestApproachTime`: `relVelocity = otherVelocity - myVelocity; if (relSpeed == 0) return 0; relPosition = position() - other.position(); projection = relTangent.dot(relPosition); return projection / relSpeed;`. Close-range `steerToAvoidCloseNeighbors(0, others)` takes priority. — [OpenSteer SteerLibrary.h](https://github.com/meshula/OpenSteer/blob/master/include/OpenSteer/SteerLibrary.h)
- Path following uses a look-ahead: `futurePosition = predictFuturePosition(predictionTime)`; `steerToStayOnPath` returns zero when "inside the tube" (`outside < 0`), else `steerForSeek(onPath)`; `steerToFollowPath` uses `pathDistanceOffset = direction * predictionTime * speed()` and `targetPathDistance = nowPathDistance + pathDistanceOffset`. — [OpenSteer SteerLibrary.h](https://github.com/meshula/OpenSteer/blob/master/include/OpenSteer/SteerLibrary.h)
- Pursue/evade look-ahead variants: `tpred = k`, `tpred = k·|q−p|`, `tpred = k·|q−p|/|v|`, or `tpred = k·|q−p|/(|v|+|v_q|)` to "Reduce for high speeds". — [Goldsmiths lecture 9](http://ccg.doc.gold.ac.uk/ccg_old/teaching/ludic_computing/ludic9.pdf)

**"Don't re-plan every frame" / path stability (animation-driven locomotion)**
- Hitman: Absolution: "Path needs to be stable for planning to work"; "Our NPCs MUST follow their paths exactly (on-rails)"; "We can't constantly modify paths while performing CA"; "We make use of animation metadata to create a local collision horizon; NPC motion is predicted for that collision horizon along the NPC's path; Collisions are detected by performing moving sphere intersection tests"; NPCs "don't have turn animations – we blend in a banking animation when turning", with "Discrete movement speeds – walk, jog, sprint". Footstep planning requires being "precisely on our foot branching position: Before a pivot, Before our goal, Before interactions". — [Anguelov, Leblanc, Harris, "Animation-Driven Locomotion For Smoother Navigation", GDC 2012 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2012/slides/Summit_AI/Anguelov_Bobby_AnimationDrivenLocomotion.pdf)
- Unity NavMeshAgent exposes `autoRepath` ("Should the agent attempt to acquire a new path if the existing path becomes invalid?") and `autoBraking`. — [Unity NavMeshAgent](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.3/script-reference/unityengine/ai/navmeshagent)

**Combination strategies that themselves cause flicker**
- Buckland's three summing methods are `weighted_average, prioritized, dithered`; prioritized dithering evaluates behaviours in priority order with per-behaviour probabilities (the `pr*` values above). — [SteeringBehaviors.h](https://github.com/wangchen/Programming-Game-AI-by-Example-src/blob/master/Buckland_Chapter3-Steering%20Behaviors/SteeringBehaviors.h); [params.ini](https://github.com/wangchen/Programming-Game-AI-by-Example-src/blob/master/Buckland_Chapter3-Steering%20Behaviors/params.ini)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** Buckland's `NumSamplesForSmoothing = 10` at his demo's frame rate is a ~10-frame moving average of heading; at 60 Hz the equivalent window is ≈ 0.17 s. A moving average is integer-friendly (ring buffer, sum, shift) and deterministic, and it only touches the *rendered* heading, so it cannot cause collisions.
- **[inference]** OpenSteer's side rule is a one-shot per-threat decision from predicted nearest approach; because the prediction uses current velocities, two agents reacting symmetrically can both re-decide next frame (same failure as VO). Making the sign sticky per pair (store `steer` for the threat id until the threat passes, i.e. until `predictNearestApproachTime` < 0) is the obvious extension and matches HRVO/ClearPath/Mavrogiannis.
- **[inference]** Prioritized dithering (Buckland) is a randomised scheme and should not be used in a deterministic sim; use prioritized weighted truncated sum instead.
- **[inference]** Millington's `targetRadius` is the dead-zone that stops oscillation at the goal; Detour's equivalent is the `slowDownRadius` plus the `0.0001` speed cut-off in `integrate()`, which is far smaller than a footballer's step length, so an explicit dead-zone of about half a body radius for off-ball targets is warranted.

### Gaps
- Reynolds' original 1999 text (arrival, unaligned collision avoidance wording, remarks on blending/dithering) could not be read (red3d.com and the UU mirror both blocked); formulas above are from secondary quotations and OpenSteer code.
- Millington's `targetRadius`/`slowRadius` numeric examples are not given in the book excerpts retrieved; the Unity cookbook derivative sets `slowRadius = targetRadius + 25` (units unspecified) and `timeToTarget = 0.1`. — [Kodeco tutorial](https://www.kodeco.com/2808-introduction-to-ai-programming-for-games?page=2)

## Key Question 4 — Animation-driven locomotion constraints in sports games (motion matching, foot-plant/turn budgets, path look-ahead, EA/2K talks and patents)

### Takeaway
Sports titles suppress wobble on the animation side by (a) feeding the animation system a *smoothed, predicted* trajectory rather than the raw per-frame steering (spring-damper simulation in motion matching; 1 s past/future trajectory samples), (b) blending physics-driven and animation-driven root motion ("root motion weight"), (c) warping turn animations to hit a point at a time instead of re-steering each frame (FIFA 2010), and (d) generating/manipulating a path first so the motion request is coherent with captured data (UFC 3 RPM); patents from EA and Take-Two cover route/path generation with motion data (position, orientation, speed, acceleration) and locomotion building blocks selected by turn/accelerate context.

### Inferences
- **[inference]** The common pattern across EA's talks is a two-layer design: a gameplay "motion model"/path layer that is smooth and predictable (spring-tracked velocity, 1 s trajectory, root-motion weight), and an animation layer that is allowed to *warp* to hit a point in time rather than re-steer every frame. For the sim, this means the AI steering output should be filtered into a trajectory (position/heading at +0.33/+0.66/+1.0 s) and the renderer should follow the trajectory, so per-tick avoidance jitter never reaches the feet.
- **[inference]** Foot-plant/turn budgets (UFC "no foot sliding", Hitman "banking animation when turning", FIFA turn warping) imply a maximum heading change per stride; at 3–9 m/s with ~0.3–0.45 s stride period this is a natural *turn-rate* cap already present in the sim, and the budget should also cap *reversals*: a turn started this stride should not reverse before the next foot plant (≈ 0.15–0.25 s), which is a dwell time on the lateral sign.

## Key Question 5 — Decision-side target smoothing: hysteresis on target changes, dwell times, rate limits, alpha-beta/Kalman filters, intent locks

### Takeaway
The decision layer is smoothed with four standard devices: a two-threshold switch (Schmitt/hysteresis or deadband) sized above the noise, a minimum dwell/commit time before a re-decision, a score bonus for the incumbent option (utility "inertia"), and a low-gain tracking filter (alpha-beta with small α, β, or a critically damped spring) on the moving target itself.

### Cited Findings
- Hysteresis/deadband (control practice): give the output two thresholds "one to switch on and one to switch off"; "The gap between the two thresholds is the hysteresis. It must be bigger than the noise on the signal"; "Size the band from the noise ... set the band at several times the peak-to-peak noise"; "Add a minimum run or off time for motors and compressors that have a starts-per-hour limit"; pitfalls: "Band smaller than the noise. It still chatters, just less often." Deadband on a value: `IF ABS(Speed_Raw - Speed_Sent) > 0.5 THEN Speed_Sent := Speed_Raw`. — [plccode.ai, Hysteresis and deadband in structured text](https://plccode.ai/hysteresis-and-deadband-in-structured-text)
- Dwell-time switching (control theory): "consecutive switching instants are separated by (at least) a prespecified time interval, called the dwell time, which is sufficiently large so that the switching does not destabilize the system"; downside: "the performance of the currently active controller might deteriorate to an unacceptable level before the next switch is permitted", motivating hysteresis-based switching instead. — [Hespanha, Liberzon, Morse, "Hysteresis-based switching algorithms for supervisory control", Automatica 2003](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0005109802002418)
- Utility-AI inertia: a "current-behavior bonus that multiplies the active behavior's score, which prevents flip flopping between behaviors"; the Borealis "Inertia Weight" is instead "a time duration that limits how often a new behavior should be chosen, acting like a cooldown to switch between behaviors." — [Project Borealis IAUS wiki](https://github.com/ProjectBorealis/IAUS/wiki); a practitioner guide suggests multiplying the running action's score by "a bonus (e.g. 1.2x)" plus "a minimum commit time so a marginally higher score cannot interrupt an in-progress action." — [bugnet.io, fixing utility AI oscillation](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-utility-ai-oscillating-between-actions)
- Alpha-beta filter: predict `x_p = x_old + v_old·Δt`, correct with residual r: `x_new = x_p + α·r`, `v_new = v_old + (β/Δt)·r`; "For convergence and stability, the values of the alpha and beta multipliers should be positive and small"; "larger alpha and beta gains tend to produce faster response for tracking transient changes, while smaller alpha and beta gains reduce the level of noise"; it "becomes a steady-state Kalman filter if filter parameters are calculated from the sampling interval, the process variance and the noise variance." — [Wikipedia, Alpha beta filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_beta_filter); worked examples use α = 0.6, β = 0.2 — [radartutorial.eu](https://www.radartutorial.eu/10.processing/en/sp60.en.html) and α = 0.2, β = 0.1 — [kalmanfilter.net](https://kalmanfilter.net/alphabeta.html)
- Alpha-beta(-gamma) stability region (Jury test): `0 < α < 2, 0 < β, β < 4 − 2α` (and `γ < 4αβ/(2 − α)` for the γ filter). — [Tenne & Singh, Optimal design of α-β-(γ) filters](https://code.eng.buffalo.edu/tracking/papers/tenne_optimalFilter2000.pdf)
- Path stability as a decision-side rule: "Path needs to be stable for planning to work"; "We can't constantly modify paths while performing CA" (Hitman ADL). — [Anguelov et al., GDC 2012](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2012/slides/Summit_AI/Anguelov_Bobby_AnimationDrivenLocomotion.pdf)
- Long-range look-ahead reduces local reactivity: lookahead "results in smoother crowd movement and exhibits an agent's tendency to avoid congestion", with agents reaching goals "in less than half the time". — [Golas, Narain, Lin, Hybrid Long-Range Collision Avoidance](http://gamma-web.iacs.umd.edu/lookahead/golas-2013-hybridcrowd.pdf)
- Pursuit look-ahead choices (constant k; distance-proportional; time-to-target `k·|q−p|/|v|`; reduced for high speeds `k·|q−p|/(|v|+|v_q|)`). — [Goldsmiths lecture 9](http://ccg.doc.gold.ac.uk/ccg_old/teaching/ludic_computing/ludic9.pdf)
- Learned policies show a committed passing side "robust to a small offset in the initial condition" and only "change passing direction" at a larger offset. — [Chen et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.08862)

### Inferences
- **[inference]** For a moving off-ball target (a support slot that slides with the ball), the two-filter structure is: (1) *target selection* with incumbent bonus 1.1–1.2× and a minimum dwell of 0.25–0.5 s unless the incumbent becomes invalid (occupied, out of play); (2) *target position* with an alpha-beta filter at 60 Hz using small gains (α ≈ 0.1–0.25, β ≈ 0.01–0.05, well inside the Jury region) or a critically damped spring with smoothing time 0.2–0.3 s, so the goal moves at most a few cm per tick. Alpha-beta is two multiply-adds per axis and is exact in fixed point.
- **[inference]** A deadband on the *target* ("re-target only if the new slot differs by more than one body radius") directly implements the plccode "deadband on a value" pattern; a band smaller than the slot's own jitter will still chatter.
- **[inference]** Hespanha's warning applies to the avoidance side: a dwell time on the passing side must have a safety override (time-to-collision below ~0.3 s or penetration) or the committed side can be held into a collision.

### Gaps
- No game-specific published source gives numeric dwell times for AI target locks in football games; the values above are inferred. The Game AI Pro dual-utility chapter (Dill) likely discusses inertia/commitment but was not retrieved.

## Key Question 6 — Control-theory view: critically damped second-order filters for heading, deadbands, Schmitt triggers; parameters for no overshoot at 60 Hz for a 3–9 m/s human-sized agent

### Takeaway
A critically damped spring (damping ratio ζ = 1) is the fastest filter that never overshoots; its closed-form per-tick update needs only four precomputed coefficients (so it is exact and fixed-point friendly), its lag equals the smoothing time (Unreal) or half-life/ln 2 (Holden), and implicit-Euler spring updates are unconditionally stable; the social-force literature gives the same ζ-based oscillation criterion for agents approaching a goal.

### Cited Findings
- Critically damped definition and closed form: "For critically damped motion, ζ = 1. A critically damped spring will reach equilibrium as fast as possible without oscillating." With repeated root z = −ω: `x(t) = ((v0 + x0 ω) t + x0) e^{−ωt}`, `v(t) = (v0 − (v0 + x0 ω) ω t) e^{−ωt}`; the implementation precomputes coefficients and updates `pos = oldPos*posPosCoef + oldVel*posVelCoef + equilibriumPos; vel = oldPos*velPosCoef + oldVel*velVelCoef` (positions relative to equilibrium). — [Ryan Juckett, Damped Springs](https://www.ryanjuckett.com/damped-springs)
- The same coefficients spelled out (Go port of Juckett): critically damped: `expTerm = exp(−ω·dt); timeExp = dt·expTerm; timeExpFreq = timeExp·ω; posPosCoef = timeExpFreq + expTerm; posVelCoef = timeExp; velPosCoef = −ω·timeExpFreq; velVelCoef = −timeExpFreq + expTerm`. — [charmbracelet/harmonica spring.go](https://github.com/charmbracelet/harmonica/blob/master/spring.go)
- Holden's half-life parameterisation: damping ratio `r = d / (2√s)` (d damping, s stiffness), `damping_ratio_to_stiffness(ratio, damping) = (damping/(ratio·2))²`; critically damped branch: `j0 = x − c; j1 = v + j0*y; eydt = fast_negexp(y*dt); x = j0*eydt + dt*j1*eydt + c; v = −y*j0*eydt − y*dt*j1*eydt + j1*eydt;` with `y = d/2`; `halflife_to_lag(h) = h / 0.69314718056`; "pre-compute y and eydt for a given halflife" when many springs share dt. — [Holden, Spring-Roll-Call](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)
- Implicit (backward) Euler spring, unconditionally stable: `Δ = (1 + 2hζω) + h²ω²; x_{i+1} = ((1 + 2hζω) x_i + h v_i + h²ω² x_t)/Δ; v_{i+1} = (v_i + hω²(x_t − x_i))/Δ`; "A damping ratio of 1 signifies a critically damped system ... converging to the target value exponentially." — [Allen Chou, Precise Control over Numeric Springing](https://allenchou.net/2015/04/game-math-precise-control-over-numeric-springing)
- Unreal: smoothing time = lag for constant motion; approximation valid for `dt < 0.5·SmoothingTime`; max velocity reached at `0.5·SmoothingTime` from rest. — [Unreal FMath::CriticallyDampedSmoothing](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Core/FMath/CriticallyDampedSmoothing)
- Game Programming Gems 4 "SmoothCD" (as used by Unity's SmoothDamp): `omega = 2/smoothTime; x = omega·dt; exp = 1/(1 + x + 0.48x² + 0.235x³)`; clamp the change so "we do not overshoot our target"; `maxDelta = maxSpeed·smoothTime`. — [Graeme Potts, critically damped spring smoothing (citing Kirmse, GPG4)](https://graemepottsfolio.wordpress.com/tag/damped-spring)
- Agents approaching a goal under a second-order (force) model obey `ξ̈ + rξ̇ + ω0²ξ = 0` with `r = 1/τ`, `ω0² = a/b`; no oscillation iff `r² − 4ω0² > 0`, i.e. `(a/b)τ² < 1/4`. — [Chraibi 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.1133); [Kretz 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.02566)
- Hysteresis/Schmitt-style two-threshold switching and deadband sizing ("several times the peak-to-peak noise"; add minimum run time). — [plccode.ai](https://plccode.ai/hysteresis-and-deadband-in-structured-text)
- Alpha-beta stability region `0 < α < 2, 0 < β < 4 − 2α`. — [Tenne & Singh](https://code.eng.buffalo.edu/tracking/papers/tenne_optimalFilter2000.pdf)
- Humanoid locomotion controllers in research clip actions to bounded linear/angular acceleration (e.g. a_v ∈ {−0.4, 0, 0.4} m/s², a_ω ∈ {−π/6, 0, π/6} rad/s²) and apply velocity damping `v(t+dt) = (1 − damping)·v(t) + f/m·dt` before clamping to max speed. — [Li et al., AAAI 2026 (GUIDER)](https://cdn.luneresearch.com/pdfs/10dcb726-de9c-40d5-bc01-55969c9f9847/ab01a37452c894da34692f3f1bf1b05fec1038ae5575a422d573aed74f9ecd9d.pdf); [Pshenitsyn et al., CAMAR, AAAI 2026](https://cdn.luneresearch.com/pdfs/aaai/2026/1de9917c-8a8e-473f-86a3-d6d5ac0fe21d.pdf)

### Inferences (parameter proposals for 60 Hz, 3–9 m/s human agent)
- **[inference]** Heading/velocity-direction filter: critically damped spring on the *steering direction* (unit vector or angle) with smoothing time T = 0.12–0.20 s (lag per Unreal's definition) → ω = 2/T ≈ 10–17 rad/s; at dt = 1/60 s, `e^{−ω dt}` = 0.76–0.85 and `dt < 0.5T` holds, so the GPG4/Unreal approximation is valid. Precompute the four Juckett coefficients as Q16.16 constants per (T, dt) pair (no exp/sqrt at runtime); the closed-form update is then deterministic and exact to the fixed-point rounding.
- **[inference]** Why no overshoot matters for side flips: an under-damped (ζ < 1) heading filter converts a single lateral decision into a decaying oscillation whose zero-crossings all count as flips; ζ = 1 makes each decision a monotone approach. The existing *first-order* low-pass is already overshoot-free but has no velocity state, so it cannot pre-empt reversals; the second-order form lets the acceleration cap and turn-rate cap be applied to a continuous rate variable instead of to a jumpy per-tick command.
- **[inference]** Equivalent time-domain budget: a footballer's stride at 3–9 m/s lasts ≈ 0.3–0.45 s; a lateral decision should persist at least one half-stride (0.15–0.2 s = 9–12 ticks) unless safety overrides, which is also the smoothing-time range above and Buckland's 10-sample window.
- **[inference]** Deadband for the flip metric and the controller: the metric's 0.02 m/s lateral threshold is well below the per-tick quantisation of an 8 m/s² acceleration cap (0.13 m/s per tick), so many "flips" may be sub-quantum noise; a Schmitt band of ±0.05–0.10 m/s (lateral) with a 4–6 tick dwell is the control-practice analogue of "several times the peak-to-peak noise".

### Gaps
- No published source gives tuned filter constants specifically for 60 Hz football locomotion; the ranges above are derived from the cited definitions and the stride-timing argument, not measured.
- Holden's `halflife_to_damping` constant (4·ln2/halflife in his code, from memory) was not visible in the retrieved excerpt; the lag relation and critically damped update are quoted.

## Concrete recommendations for the sim (ordered; everything integer/fixed-point and deterministic)

Context: DetourCrowd-style sampler (17 samples × 3 adaptive levels, Detour weights, 1 s horizon), first-order low-pass on the AI velocity command, two-regime acceleration cap, speed-dependent turn-rate cap; metric = side flips per player-minute. Established facts cited above; the specific numbers below are **[inference]** starting points to A/B against the stored baseline.

1. **Instrument before changing.** Split the flip metric by cause: (a) sampler output `nvel` flips with `dvel` steady, (b) `dvel` (target/steering direction) flips, (c) flips only after the low-pass/caps. Report flips only when |lateral| exceeds a Schmitt band (suggest ±0.05 m/s with 4-tick dwell) in addition to the current 0.02 m/s raw count, so sub-quantum noise is visible separately. (Basis: hysteresis sizing — plccode.ai; Detour `nvel`/`dvel`/`vel` semantics — DetourCrowd.h.)

2. **Lateral-sign hysteresis inside the sampler (cheapest, most targeted).** Add a fifth penalty term to Detour's `processSample`: `fpen = weightFlip · [ sign(lat(vcand)) ≠ sign(lat(prev_nvel)) ∧ |lat(prev_nvel)| > ε ]`, where `lat()` is the component perpendicular to the previous heading (a 2-D cross product, integer). Start with `weightFlip` = 0.75 (equal to Detour's `weightSide`/`weightCurVel` = 0.75, below `weightDesVel` = 2.0 and `weightToi` = 2.5 so safety still wins), ε = 0.05 m/s. Also break exact penalty ties deterministically toward the sample nearest `prev_nvel` (lexicographic compare on (penalty, dist²)), never by evaluation order. (Basis: Detour penalty structure; Mononen on sample switching; ClearPath "side closest to current velocity".)

3. **Per-pair passing-side memory (the HRVO/ClearPath/winding-number idea, deterministic).** For each (agent, neighbour) pair within the horizon store `side_ij ∈ {L, R}` = sign of `det(p_j − p_i, v_pref_i − v_pref_j)` (HRVO's test) at first contact, or sign of the pairwise angular momentum (Mavrogiannis). Penalise candidate velocities whose relative velocity would pass on the other side (`det(p_j − p_i, 2·vcand − v_i − v_j)` of opposite sign) with `weightSideMem` ≈ `weightSide` (0.75), and when the pair is exactly symmetric choose the side by a deterministic priority (ball-side/role priority as in Right-of-Way; fall back to lower player index). Clear the memory when the pair has passed (`predictNearestApproachTime < 0`, OpenSteer) or after 1 s without interaction. No randomness is needed: this replaces RVO2-style symmetry perturbation.

4. **Dwell time with a safety override.** Hold the committed lateral sign (from 2–3) for a minimum of 9–12 ticks (0.15–0.2 s, half a stride) unless time-to-collision < 0.3 s or penetration occurs (Hespanha's caveat about fixed dwell). Implement as a per-agent tick counter; no floating point.

5. **Shrink the discrete jumps (Detour knobs).** Try `velBias` 0.5 → 0.6 and `weightCurVel` 0.75 → 1.25 while keeping `weightDesVel` 2.0; this narrows the search disc (`cr = vmax·(1 − velBias)`) and penalises leaving the current velocity, which reduces mirror-sample switching before any new term is added. If the metric improves but agents stall, revert `weightCurVel` first. (Basis: Detour pattern geometry; Detour/demo presets 0.4/0.5; Mononen.)

6. **Replace the first-order low-pass with a critically damped second-order filter on the steering direction** (keep the acceleration and turn-rate caps as hard limits after it). Smoothing time T = 0.15 s (lag) → coefficients from the Juckett/harmonica closed form precomputed in Q16.16 for dt = 1/60 (e^{−ω dt} ≈ 0.80 with ω = 13.3 rad/s). Expect fewer zero-crossings because the filter never overshoots and carries a rate state. If a smaller lag is needed for the on-ball player, use T = 0.08–0.10 s for the ball carrier only.

7. **Arrival/dead-zone fixes for static or slow targets.** Use a stopping-distance slow radius `d_slow = v² / (2·a_brake)` (lecture derivation of Reynolds' arrival; e.g. 8 m/s with 8 m/s² → 4 m) rather than Detour's `2·radius`, and a Millington-style `targetRadius` ≈ 0.4–0.5 m inside which steering is zero and the agent only decelerates. This removes end-of-path jitter, which Detour's author calls "especially nasty".

8. **Decision-side target smoothing.** (a) Incumbent bonus 1.15× and minimum dwell 0.3 s (18 ticks) on target/slot selection; (b) alpha-beta filter on the chosen target position with α = 0.2, β = 0.02 at 60 Hz (inside `0<α<2, 0<β<4−2α`), or a critically damped spring with T = 0.25 s; (c) re-target deadband of one body radius (≈ 0.4 m). All are a few integer multiply-adds.

9. **Animation-side decoupling.** Render heading from a 10-sample (≈ 0.17 s) moving average of the sim heading (Buckland/Robin Green), and drive turn animations from the filtered trajectory (position/heading at +0.33/+0.66/+1.0 s, as in EA's 1 s trajectory features) with a per-stride turn budget; residual sim jitter then never reaches the feet.

10. **Only if flips persist in dense symmetric situations:** add a deterministic Right-of-Way asymmetry (responsibility split ≠ 1/2 by role priority: ball carrier > runner > marker > idle), which also resolves true head-on deadlocks that side memory alone cannot.

Suggested order of experiments: 1 → 2 → 5 → 3 → 4 → 6 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 10, measuring side flips per player-minute and the collision/contact metrics after each step against `docs/match-analytics-*.json`.
