# Defensive transitions and off-ball support positioning: numbers for a 60 Hz football sim

Scope: (1) the defensive transition after a turnover (who recovers, how fast, for how long, counter-press vs. recover-shape) and (2) principled, implementable models of off-ball support positioning in attack. Sources are real-football tracking/event studies, coaching literature, and football-AI/robotics papers. Every number carries its source; inferences are separated. The final section translates findings into rules and parameter ranges for a deterministic fixed-point 60 Hz simulation (formation slots, presser + cover, marks, elected chaser, destination reservation, GRF-style spacing, 70% cruise speed for uninvolved players).

Note on access: in this session several primary PDFs (Sloan papers, Springer, PMC) were reachable only via an MCP fetch proxy that rate-limited late in the session; where a number comes from a secondary description or a search excerpt rather than the primary text, that is stated.

## KQ1. How long does the defensive transition last after a turnover, and who sprints (tracking-data studies)?

### Takeaway
Labelled Bundesliga tracking data puts the average defensive transition at about 9.3 s (counter-pressing phases 9.9 s, pure "fall-back" phases 18.3 s), while GPS studies of elite matches put mean transition activities at about 10 s with peak episodes of 22-27 s; during these phases high-speed running is 7-10 times the whole-match rate, full-backs and central midfielders sprint most in defensive transitions, attackers run least, and centre-backs accelerate/decelerate most.

### Cited Findings
- Expert-labelled Bundesliga transitions (25 Hz positional + event data): "The average duration of all transitions phases is 9.34 s, 9.89 s for counterpressing, whereas all non-counterpressing turnovers took in average 9.11 s." — [Bauer & Anzer 2021, Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Same study: "fallback situations last on average 18.30 s, whereas undefined situations were the shortest with 7.83 s on average"; of 11,108 relevant defensive turnovers, 3,196 were labelled counterpressing, 970 fallback, 6,942 "uncontrolled"; "around 62.5% of all ball losses ... cannot be assigned to either defensive strategy (counterpressing or fallback)". — [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Fallback is defined as "a defensive transition phase, where all players' intention is to either react inactively, or move backwards to their defensive line-up without exerting pressure on the ball." — [Bauer & Anzer 2021, Appendix A](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Per team per match the detector finds "around 20 to 30 counterpressing situations, out of around 90 to 200 transition situations." — [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Vogelbein, Nopp & Hökelmann (2014), all 306 Bundesliga 2010/11 matches: "defensive reaction time" = time to regain possession after losing it; "Top teams recovered ball possession quickest ... and demonstrated lower defensive reaction times (approximately 1 s in each match status) compared to the remaining teams. Moreover, all groups showed the lowest defensive reaction times when trailing." — [Vogelbein et al. 2014, J Sports Sci (abstract via ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260128322_Defensive_transition_in_soccer_-_are_prompt_possession_regains_a_measure_of_success_A_quantitative_analysis_of_German_Fussball-Bundesliga_20102011)
- GPS (10 Hz) on 23 elite players over 10 matches: "Positive transitions (PT) lasted significantly longer than other periods (around 11 secs), while mean duration of transitions was around 10 secs. Peak duration of all transitions lasted between 22-27 secs." — [Bortnik, Burger & Rhodes 2022, Biology of Sport](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9536371)
- Same study, intensity: "High-velocity activities were 7-9-fold greater than the 90-min demands and nearly half of the game SD and HSRD occurred during TA's"; "Over 90 minutes, players cover TD of around 119 m·min-1, HSRD of 7.5 m·min-1 and SD of 1.5 m·min-1"; thresholds HSRD > 19.8 km/h, SD > 25.2 km/h, high-intensity accel/decel > 3 m/s². Negative (defensive) transitions had the highest total distance per minute; positive transitions the highest sprint distance per minute. — [Bortnik et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9536371)
- Same group, by position (4,249 observations, Ekstraklasa 2020/21), per-minute outputs during transitional activities: total distance 206.2 m/min overall (CB 166.8, FB 204.2, CDM 214.9, CM 212.3, CAM 234.4, W 211.7, A 203.6); HSRD 79.8 m/min overall (CB 21.7, FB 61.2, CDM 44.5, CM 45.6, CAM 67.4, W 63.4, A 53.0); sprint distance 13.2 m/min overall (CB 5.0, FB 24.2, CDM 5.5, CM 12.9, CAM 13.5, W 20.0, A 10.9). — [Bortnik et al. 2024, Biology of Sport, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10955741/)
- Same study, defensive transitions specifically: "SD (m·min-1) was highest for fullbacks (FB) ... during defensive transitions (DT) and fast attacks (FA). Similarly, central midfielders (CM) had high SD ... during defensive transitions"; "Attackers (A) had significantly lower TD and HSRD compared to all other positions ... during defensive transitions (DT)"; centre backs "achieved highest A+D (n·min-1) during defensive transitions". — [Bortnik et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10955741/)
- Phase end rules used by that study: "Defensive transitions ended if ... a) possession re-gained, b) ball out of play, c) shot on goal, d) decreased tempo of attack due to an effective defense by opposition." — [Bortnik et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10955741/)
- Offensive-transition durations (1,649 goals from offensive transitions, 1,151 games): in top leagues 31.1% of goal-scoring transitions were "Fast" (0-8 s), 29.5% "Medium" (9-12 s), 39.4% "Slow" (13 s+); "only 31% of successful transitions last up to 8 s"; Casal et al. (2011) is cited for offensive transitions after a direct recovery lasting 16 s on average. — [Eusebio et al. 2025, Frontiers in Sports and Active Living](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1462932/full)
- Peak-period analysis of 50 EPL matches: during peak 1-, 3- and 5-min windows players covered 28-34% of their high-intensity distance as "Recovery Run" and 22-25% as "Covering" (67 ± 19 m, 92 ± 28 m, 113 ± 36 m HI distance per window); at team level 9-10 players were involved collectively covering ~400 m, ~650 m, ~850 m of HI distance; in the following 1-min period players had a 48% deficit versus match average. — [Ju et al. 2022, Biology of Sport](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/15958/1/BS_Art_45951-10.pdf)

### Inferences
- A sim transition phase should be modelled as two nested windows: an intense ~5-6 s counter-press window inside a ~9-10 s re-organisation window; the fall-back branch (no pressure) legitimately takes up to ~18 s because the ball is still travelling/being circulated.
- The 7-10x jump in high-speed running means nearly every outfield player is in a high-speed band for part of the transition; a 70% cruise speed for "uninvolved" players is far below observed behaviour in the first seconds after a turnover.
- Attackers are the lowest-output group in defensive transitions, but "lowest" is still ~53 m/min of HSRD, i.e. roughly 7x their whole-match rate; forwards jog back less than others but still sprint for short bursts.

### Gaps
- Vogelbein et al.'s absolute defensive reaction times in seconds for top vs. bottom teams were not retrievable (only the ~1 s difference is in the abstract).
- No study found reports the number of players sprinting simultaneously as a function of ball location and each player's distance ahead/behind the ball; Bauer & Anzer's "speed of the regaining team two seconds after the turnover" and "players behind ball after 1 s" features are the closest proxies.
- Bortnik et al. do not publish the per-phase speed-band table split by position (only text comparisons); the per-position Table 1 pools all transitional activities.

## KQ2. Counter-pressing: the "5/6-second rule", players committed, when to stop, and pressing metrics (PPDA etc.)

### Takeaway
Coaching lore (Guardiola "five/six second rule"), data vendors (StatsBomb: pressure within 5 s of turnover) and peer-reviewed work (Bauer & Anzer: regain within 5 s = success; shots/goals credited within 20 s) converge on a 5-6 s counter-press window; data show four or more players behind the ball after one second is the decisive threshold, numerical superiority within 10 m of the ball raises 5-second regain rate from 30.2% to 36.2%, and roughly a quarter of turnovers are counter-pressed.

### Cited Findings
- "Pep Guardiola made the 'five second rule' for counterpressing famous"; Klopp "is generally accepted as the originator of the term 'Gegenpressing'." — [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Success definition: "we consider it as successful if the ball is regained within five seconds and shots and goals, scored or received, are accredited to the previous counterpressing phase if they occur within the following 20 seconds"; these thresholds were chosen after reviewing clips with regains after 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 s and shots 10-25 s after the loss with professional analysts. — [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Operational definition: "a team conducts counterpressing if at least one player exerts (spatio and/or temporal) pressure on the ball carrier, or on the opponents close to the ball"; a naive rule-based baseline classifies a counterpress "whenever one or more players are within a five-yard radius around the ball carrier during the first individual ball possession phase following a turnover." Analysts in the proof of concept excluded 10 of 11 flagged scenes "consisted of only one player exerting pressure", which some experts would classify as fallback. — [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Most predictive features: "the higher the absolute amount of individual ball possession time within the first three seconds and the higher the speed of the regaining team two seconds after the turnover, the more likely a defensive turnover is classified as counterpressing"; "If less than four players are behind the ball, this feature on average decreases the chance of a counterpressing classification. The number of four defenders—almost half of the team—seems to be a decisive threshold"; the local stretch index of the 5 nearest opposing players 2 s after the turnover increases the counterpress likelihood from ~600 cm up to a maximum at ~1,000 cm. — [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Rules of thumb tested: "counterpressing is ideal after ball losses close to the sideline or close to the corners in the opponent's half" (heatmaps support it); "Whenever the team out of possession has a numerical superiority within a 10 m radius around the ball, at the time of the turnover, they regain ball possession within 5 seconds 36.2% of the time, compared to only 30.2%, when the other team has more players in that area." — [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Frequency and consequences: home teams' "27.24% of all included defensive transitions" classified as counterpressing (labelled data 28.77%); "If one is successful, i.e. wins back the ball within five seconds the chance of scoring increases tremendously, but if unsuccessful one is far likelier to concede a goal"; the ratio of successful counterpresses to total transitions is the best predictor of final rank (r = -0.44). — [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- StatsBomb: "Five seconds is often used as a cut-off for the period where pressure on the opposition is most intensely applied during the counter-press"; in 177 EPL 2017/18 matches "a little under half (0.47) of open-play possessions come under pressure from their opponent within five seconds"; Burnley counter-pressed 18 possessions per game but regained within 5 s only 2.5 times (14% ratio); 13% of such possessions ended in a shot conceded vs. a 10% league average. — [StatsBomb, How StatsBomb Data Helps Measure Counter-Pressing](https://blogarchive.statsbomb.com/articles/soccer/how-statsbomb-data-helps-measure-counter-pressing/)
- StatsBomb's pressure event triggers when a defender is within a 4-5 yard radius of the ball carrier (as summarised by Pressing Intensity authors). — [Pressing Intensity, arXiv 2501.04712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.04712)
- Spielverlagerung's five-second rule: "Immediately after losing the ball the team should press at top speed and with maximum intensity for five seconds. If the team hasn't recovered the ball within five seconds and there is no chance of immediately winning it, they should fall back into their defensive formation"; a "flexible, situation-specific length" is recommended; prerequisite: "players must play as close to one another as possible"; a variant has the first presser deliberately run past the carrier to "hand off the opponent and retain his own original position in the cover shadow"; passing-lane-oriented variant: "set up between some opponents and then, after the ball is passed, to flexibly recover the ball, trap the pass, or ... have two people press the pass receiver" (Barcelona under Guardiola). — [Spielverlagerung, Counter- or Gegenpressing](https://spielverlagerung.com/?p=352)
- David Sumpter's FourFourTwo analysis: "Pep drilled his Barcelona team to get the ball back within six seconds, which he identified as a good rule of thumb for how long a team should counter-press before falling back"; Klopp's first three Liverpool games produced 17 six-second recoveries/tackles/interceptions in the opponent's half vs. 18 for Rodgers' last three. — [FourFourTwo](https://www.fourfourtwo.com/features/revealed-klopps-transition-more-important-press)
- Rule-based event-data study (StatsBomb 360): counterpress window treated as 6 s; "sustained pressure" = at least 50% of passes in the 6 s after the loss were pressured; success = regain within 6 s in the opposition final third; territory concession measured within 15 s and shot concession within 20 s; passes that beat the counterpress averaged 18 yards vs. 16.5 yards for those that did not (p < 0.001); counterpress success was lower against top-ranked opposition. — [Journal of Performance Analysis in Sport 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2025.2473799)
- Coaching guidance: "the players nearest the ball press the new ball-carrier within seconds"; failing to regain can still "force the ball out of play or delay the opposition's attack ... allowing the pressing team to recover their shape"; main risk is "space is left in behind the back line". — [Coaches' Voice counter-pressing explainer](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/counter-pressing-gegenpressing-football-tactics-explained-klopp-guardiola-bielsa-hasenhuttl/)
- FIFA Training Centre practice notes (search excerpt): the closest player applies aggressive pressure while two colleagues control the surrounding space and anticipate passes; FIFA distinguishes an individual counter-press (led by the player who lost the ball) from a collective one that targets the player behind the press. — [FIFA Training Centre, counter-pressing session](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/elite-sessions/transition-to-defending/counter-pressing.php)
- PPDA: "the number of passes made by the opposition is divided by the number of defensive actions made by the out-of-possession team", counted in the 60% of the pitch nearest the opposition goal; defensive actions = tackles, interceptions, challenges, fouls; 2021/22 top-five-league extremes Barcelona 7.26 (most intense) and Troyes/Norwich 16.93. — [Coaches' Voice, PPDA](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/?p=61775); Wyscout's version uses fouls, interceptions, won defensive duels and sliding tackles and reports a 2018/19 top-five-league average of 11.01. — [Wyscout data glossary](https://dataglossary.wyscout.com/ppda/); Premier League explainer. — [premierleague.com](https://www.premierleague.com/news/4250153)
- Pressing Intensity (tracking-based alternative to PPDA): defender-to-attacker time-to-intercept T = reaction time + distance/vmax + a penalty for velocity facing away, converted with Spearman's logistic with σ = 0.45 s and horizon T = 1.5 s; an "Active Pressing speed threshold" zeroes the pressing probability of a defender moving below it; the attacker target can be replaced by the nearest point on each passing lane to measure lane-closing. — [arXiv 2501.04712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.04712)

### Inferences
- A 5 s hard window with a 6 s soft tail reproduces both the coaching rule and the vendor/academic definitions; abort conditions used by analysts are: (a) ball regained, (b) 5-6 s elapsed with no immediate chance, (c) ball played beyond the pressing group (longer passes of ~18 yd / 16.5 m beat the press).
- "How many press" in the data is best expressed as a local count: numerical superiority inside a 10 m radius of the ball, and >= 4 teammates behind the ball within 1 s. These two counts are cheap at 60 Hz.
- Counter-pressing is the minority choice (~27% of transitions) even in the Bundesliga; most turnovers are "uncontrolled"/fallback, so a sim should not counter-press every turnover.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed count of how many players actively press (as opposed to being behind the ball) in a typical successful counter-press; coaching sources imply 1 presser + 2 supporters near the ball.
- Bauer & Anzer's per-team success rates and shot/goal probability table (Table 4) values were not extracted beyond the qualitative statement.

## KQ3. Recovery runs: speeds, distances, by position; effect of fatigue; which forwards stay high

### Takeaway
EPL tracking + video coding shows a high-intensity recovery run (>19.8 km/h, "run back towards own goal to be goal side when out of position") averages ~26-27 m over ~3 s, 4-8 times per match per player depending on position; forwards do the fewest recovery runs and the most closing-down (~15 m, ~1 s bursts), and after peak periods players cut covering/recovery high-intensity distance by 20-53%.

### Cited Findings
- Definitions (EPL, 244 players): "Recovery Run: Player runs back towards their own goal to be goal side when out of position"; "Covering: Player moves to cover space or an opposition player while remaining goal side"; "Close Down/Press: Player runs directly towards opposition player on or receiving the ball, or towards space or players not on/receiving the ball"; "Support Play: Player supports from behind/level by trying to engage in offensive/transition play (typically during fast transitions)"; high intensity threshold > 19.8 km/h. — [Ju et al. 2023, Biology of Sport](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/17199/1/BS_Art_46976-10.pdf)
- Per-action numbers (Table 2): Recovery Run ~26-27 m per action, 3 ± 2 s, with 4 ± 3 actions per match for central defensive players, 7 ± 4 for wide defensive players (wing-backs 8 ± 4, full-backs 5 ± 3); Close Down/Press ~15 m, 1 ± 1 s (CDP 1, WDP 3, WB 4 per match); Support Play 23-26 m, 3 s; overall average HI action ~20 m, 2 s, with 22-45 HI actions per match by position. — [Ju et al. 2023, Table 2](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/17199/1/BS_Art_46976-10.pdf)
- Position pattern: central offensive players cover the most HI distance for "Break into Box", "Run in Behind/Penetrate" and "Close Down/Press"; central/wide defenders the most "Covering"; wide defenders the most "Over/Underlap"; single centre forwards (CF1) cover ~270 m of HI closing-down per match vs. ~190 m for one of two forwards (CF2); figure notes mark more "Recovery Run" distance for defenders/midfielders than for CDP, WOP and COP. — [Ju et al. 2023](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/17199/1/BS_Art_46976-10.pdf)
- Fatigue/peak effect: "After peak periods, players/teams performed 20-53% less high-intensity distances for 'Covering' and 'Recovery Run'"; average distance per HI action during peak 1-min periods is ~35 m vs. ~20 m across the whole match; central offensive players' peak-period HI distance was dominated by Close Down/Press with 14-20% for Run in Behind and 12-20% for Support Play. — [Ju et al. 2022](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/15958/1/BS_Art_45951-10.pdf)
- Attackers have the lowest TD and HSRD of all positions during defensive transitions, but the highest TD, HSRD and SD during high-pressure activities; high pressure is defined as pressing near the opponent's penalty area "within the first 3 seconds of the possession (the defender(s) are always located within 1.5 m from the first attackers)". — [Bortnik et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10955741/); [Bortnik et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9536371)
- Off-ball HI runs with broadcast tracking (Big-5 2020/21): HI run = effort peaking above 21 km/h, segmented between speed "valleys"; "from 60% to 80% of the winger's HI efforts in the attack phase do not involve direct on-ball participation"; teams run more per 30 min of effective time out of possession than in possession; run value is measured as EPV gain from run start to 2 s after the peak ends. — [Llana et al. 2022, arXiv 2204.02313](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02313.pdf)
- Speed bands used across the literature: HSR > 19.8 km/h (5.5 m/s), sprint > 25.2 km/h (7.0 m/s); relative thresholds change results because 25 km/h is ~71% of a 35 km/h top speed but ~83% of a slower player's. — [search summary of Biology of Sport 2024/2025 EPL possession studies](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11474994/)
- Coaching guidance on rest defence vs. staying forward: with 3-2/3-1 build-up shapes "a team losing the ball normally has four or five players to handle a counter-attack"; "The fewer players you have in rest defence, the riskier your attack." — [Squawka rest-defence explainer](https://www.squawka.com/en/features/tactical-explainer-what-is-rest-defence/); Spielverlagerung notes the rest-defence line "don't join the attack and can't press directly after losing the ball, so the line is usually limited to two or three players." — [Spielverlagerung, forms of rest defence](https://spielverlagerung.com/2021/07/05/tactical-theory-the-various-forms-of-rest-defence/)

### Inferences
- 26-27 m in ~3 s implies recovery runs are run at or above the high-speed band (average ~8 m/s including acceleration), i.e. at or close to sprint for most of the run; they are short bursts (about 3 s), not sustained jogs.
- Forwards' defensive-transition profile is "few, short recovery runs plus frequent 1-second closing-down bursts": a forward should drop quickly for ~2-4 s to a holding line, then stop, rather than jog back for 10 s.
- The 20-53% drop in recovery/covering HI distance after peak periods supports a fatigue multiplier on recovery urgency (range roughly 0.5-0.8 for heavily loaded players).

### Gaps
- No study found that reports recovery-run peak speeds directly for forwards; distances and durations above are per-action means from Ju et al. and imply speeds only indirectly.
- No peer-reviewed rule on which of two forwards stays high; coaching sources are qualitative (rest defence 2-3 players held back; forwards nearest the ball press).

## KQ4. Rest defence: how many stay back, where, and what makes it work

### Takeaway
Bundesliga tracking (2,951 ball losses) finds a rest defence of 3.70 ± 0.76 defenders against 2.01 ± 0.93 attackers in the 10 m band in front of the deepest defender (superiority +1.69), positioned 43.6 ± 10.0 m from their own goal line and 28 m wide, with nearest defender 5.1 m from each attacker; success is a regain within 12 s and the strongest predictor is how short the opponent's counter is, with deeper and more compact rest defences doing better.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: "rest defense can be defined as the positioning of the deepest defenders during ball possession to prevent an opposing counterattack after a ball loss"; rest defence is analysed in "the area ten meters in front of the defender closest to the own goal line"; success = regain within 12 s; failure = opposing shot within 12 s. — [Forcher et al. 2023, JSSM](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf)
- Numbers at the moment of possession change: "3.70 ± 0.76 defenders and 2.01 ± 0.93 attackers in the area of rest defense, resulting in a defensive numerical superiority of 1.69 ± 1.00"; surface area 85.98 ± 60.09 m², 7.35 ± 1.92 m long, 28.21 ± 7.43 m wide; spread 13.93 ± 2.86 m; "positioned 43.56 ± 10.00 m ahead of their own goal line"; nearest defender-attacker distance 5.08 ± 2.23 m (longest 6.89 ± 4.19 m); attacking space control in that zone 11.51 ± 9.82%. — [Forcher et al. 2023](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf)
- Who is in it: 70.1% central defenders, 13.4% wide defenders, 9.5% central midfielders, 5.9% wide midfielders; attackers in the zone 78.5% central strikers. — [Forcher et al. 2023](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf)
- Outcome balance: 2,425 successful vs. 75 unsuccessful situations (451 others excluded); most important predictor "the duration of opposing counterattacks" (shorter is better), then number of opponent actions and passes; "a deeper rest defense (closer to the own goal line) is beneficial"; lower spread/surface/length/width (higher compactness) is advantageous; "regaining possession as fast as possible after a ball loss is the most important success factor in defensive transition." — [Forcher et al. 2023](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf)
- Experts interviewed: players "proceed into the rest defense when they no longer have tasks in the attack", e.g. when the ball is in the attacking third or the midfield line has been overplayed; marking distance should shrink as the ball comes closer and as pressure on the carrier rises. — [Forcher et al. 2023, interview tables](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf)
- Bauer & Anzer's "four or more players behind the ball" is cited as the benchmark for defensive-transition success. — [Forcher et al. 2023](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf); [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Event-based (partial-frame) estimate: mean number of players in the rest-defence position was 1.22 when a shot was conceded vs. 1.31 otherwise (StatsBomb 360 freeze frames, which omit off-camera players). — [JPAS 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2025.2473799)
- Common coaching shapes: "the most common rest-defence shapes are a 2-3 ... or a 3-2 shape, with the higher line responsible for shutting down space around the ball to stop counters at source" (search excerpt). — [Coaches' Voice rest defence](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/?p=71763)

### Inferences
- For the sim, "rest defence" = 3-4 players (CBs plus one FB/CM) that never go higher than ~10 m behind the deepest opposing attacker while their team attacks, centred laterally with ~28 m total width, ~44 m from their own goal when the team is in the final third.
- Marking inside the rest-defence zone is loose (~5 m) and tightens with ball proximity and pressure on the carrier; this is a parameter the sim can scale with ball distance.

### Gaps
- The study's machine-learning performance was weak (AUC 0.60), so feature importances are indicative, not strong effects.
- No source quantifies how many players should be *ahead* of the ball in attack beyond "11 minus rest defence minus ball-near players".

## KQ5. Pitch-control, space-value, EPV and pass-availability models: how they define a good support position

### Takeaway
The dominant models reduce "good support position" to the product of three things computable from positions and velocities: probability the team controls the ball if it is played there (time-to-intercept with reaction ~0.7 s, top speed ~5 m/s, logistic σ ≈ 0.45-0.54 s, control rate λ ≈ 4 s⁻¹, defenders ×1.72), probability the pass actually goes there (Gaussian around the ball, σ ≈ 14-24 m), and the value of that location (distance to goal or learned pitch value); pass-completion models add that the receiver's slack (range of pass speeds/angles that still reach him first) and nearest-defender distance are the two strongest predictors.

### Cited Findings
- Spearman et al. 2017 pass model: receiver "must have enough time to intercept the ball before it moves past him and ... be in the vicinity of the ball for sufficient time to control it"; intercept probability is the logistic CDF of (T - t_intercept) with scale s; control is an exponential process with rate λ; combined into a per-player receive probability integrated over the ball flight; fitted on 2015/16 EPL tracking data, the model "correctly predicts the receiving team with an accuracy of 81% and the specific receiving player with an accuracy of 68%"; players "would have about a 95% likelihood to control the ball within the first second" and "a temporal uncertainty of half a second"; 78.9% of passes completed vs. model mean 67.9%; worked example receive probabilities 17.8%, 56.0%, 26.2% for three players along a pass line. Ball flight uses air density 1.22 kg/m³, drag coefficient 0.25, cross-section 0.038 m². The value model is a negative exponential of distance to goal with constants 0.93, 0.14, 0.02. — [Spearman et al. 2017, MIT Sloan (via ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315166647_Physics-Based_Modeling_of_Pass_Probabilities_in_Soccer)
- Reference implementation parameters (used widely as "Spearman's parameters"): reaction time 0.7 s during which the player keeps current velocity, then straight-line running at 5 m/s; time-to-intercept sigma 0.45 s; control rate 4.3 s⁻¹. — [LaurieOnTracking Metrica_PitchControl.py](https://github.com/Friends-of-Tracking-Data-FoTD/LaurieOnTracking/blob/master/Metrica_PitchControl.py); [R-bloggers port](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2020/09/s3-classes-and-vctrs-to-create-a-soccer-pitch-control-model/); one GitHub implementation notes its parameter list was "quoted from memory, to be verified before citing". — [Amandusmk/pitch-control PR #2](https://github.com/Amandusmk/pitch-control/pull/2)
- Spearman 2018 OBSO: probability of scoring with the next on-ball event = sum over the pitch of Score(r) × Control(r) × Transition(r); the Potential Pitch Control Field treats a controlled touch as a Poisson process once the player can reach r within T, with λ set to zero for offside attackers and the defending team's rate scaled by a defensive-advantage factor; the transition model is a 2D normal around the ball modulated by the attacking team's PPCF ("the player making the pass aims to send the ball to a location less likely to be intercepted"); the empirical displacement between successive on-ball events has standard deviation 14 m; MAP parameter table: σ (temporal uncertainty) 0.54 s, λ (control) 3.99 Hz, defensive advantage 1.72, a 23.9 m length scale (transition kernel), and a 0.48 scoring-shape parameter; example scenes integrate to total OBSO of 1.1%, 2.31%, 2.43%, 3.38%, 3.51%; per-player OBSO predicts next-match goals (r = 0.26) better than shots (0.17) or goals (0.12). — [Spearman 2018, Beyond Expected Goals (via ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327139841_Beyond_Expected_Goals)
- Fernandez & Bornn 2018 "Wide Open Spaces" (as described by Martens et al.): each player's influence is a bivariate normal whose mean is shifted along the velocity vector, whose covariance is rotated to the velocity direction and scaled by speed relative to v_max, and whose radius r_i "depends on the Euclidean distance between a player and the ball. By referring to expert knowledge, the authors restrict r_i to be in a range of [4, 10] meters"; pitch control = tanh of (sum of attacking influences minus defending influences); pitch value = learned defensive influence scaled by distance to goal; space quality = pitch control × pitch value; "space generation gain ... quantifies the space that an attacker frees up by dragging the opponents into his direction"; the original paper showed, on one match, that "Lionel Messi or Andres Iniesta are able to actively occupy higher valued space than others." — [Martens, Dick & Brefeld 2021, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.676179/full)
- Martens et al. replace the Gaussian with data-driven movement models (54 Bundesliga games): influence areas are "much smaller, especially when a player is close to the ball"; at an actual shot location the baseline pitch control was -0.21 (defence) vs. 0.57 (attack) with the data-driven model; adding pitch value to pitch control improves prediction of successful possessions; receiver space generation SG_rec and passer SG_pas correlate with xG/xA and market value (10% more SG_total ≈ 6.5% higher market value). — [Martens et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.676179/full)
- EPV (Fernández, Bornn & Cervone 2021): "The EPV expression is decomposed into a series of subcomponents that model the influence of passes, ball drives and shot actions on the expected outcome of a possession", with pass-success and pass-selection surfaces estimated from tracking data; it is "the first EPV approach in soccer to incorporate the dynamics of the 22 players and the ball through tracking data." — [Fernández, Bornn & Cervone 2021, Machine Learning (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8570314)
- Expected passes (Anzer & Bauer 2022; 840,386 Bundesliga passes, 85.2% completion, ~915 passes per game): intended receiver = teammate who could first reach the ball under a physics model; pass-length classes short < 10 m, medium 10-30 m, long > 30 m; "the possible speed window in which the pass could be completed has by far the highest influence on the success prediction followed by the distance of the closest opponent to the receiver"; other features: DefendersBehindBall, PassAngle, AngleWindow, PossessionTime; successful passes had a direction window of 32.7° ± 16.3°; 3.12% of passes are blocked; AUC 93.4%; a worked example rates the chosen long diagonal at 52.7%. — [Anzer & Bauer 2022, DMKD](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00810-3); [open PDF](https://d-nb.info/1256848891/34)
- Pass risk/reward (Power et al. 2017; 571,287 EPL passes, 84.35% completed, 380.46 attempted per team per game): completion backward 95.78%, sideways 93.03%, forward 72.28%; "passes in the forward third being successfully made 58% of the time, and passes into the penalty area ... 37%"; risk features: passer/receiver speed, nearest defender speed toward passer and receiver, nearest defender distance, nearest defender angle to the passing line, first-time pass; reward = probability of a shot within 10 s; "dangerous pass" = top 25th percentile of reward (> ~6% shot probability); the safest cluster had 3% risk and 12% reward. — [Power et al. 2017, KDD](https://mirkwood.cs.edinboro.edu/~bennett/class/csci480/fall2017/notes/p1605.pdf)
- Learned positioning value (Dick, Tavakol & Brefeld 2021): a graph RNN rates game states by probability the possession reaches a zone within 2 m of the penalty box; 10,626 possession phases averaging 10.6 s; example values: counter-attack 4-v-defence or a 3-v-1 flank ≈ 0.60-0.65, defence organised with more players behind the ball 0.12-0.24, fully organised defence 0.02-0.04; a "good pass/dribble" is one raising V by ≥ 0.06. — [Dick et al. 2021, Frontiers](https://pubdata.leuphana.de/bitstream/20.500.14123/1581/1/Dick_Rating_Player_Actions_in_Soccer.pdf)
- OBPV (2025, La Liga 2023/24): extends OBSO with a pitch-wide field-value weight (longitudinal logistic weight centred 15 m behind halfway with 30 m scale, lateral Gaussian favouring corridors beside the box) and a kernel-density transition model from real pass distributions; successful counter-attacks had significantly higher OBPV than failed ones (p = 2.4e-6, d = 0.24); teams whose opponents gained less OBPV in the 3 events after a turnover won more balls within 40 m of goal (Spearman r = -0.67). — [arXiv 2505.14711](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14711)
- Team-defence optimisation (AAAI 2025): a 25×16-zone MMDP with xT zone values and Spearman's ball-control model, solved by MCTS + linear programming, "reduces opponent threat by 21%, outperforming optimised individual behaviour by 6%". — [Everett et al. 2025, AAAI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i22.34482)
- "Dangerousity" (Link et al. 2016, 64 Bundesliga matches): goal probability at each moment of possession from four components, Zone, Control, Pressure and Density. — [Link, Lang & Seidenschwarz 2016, PLOS ONE](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5201291/)

### Inferences
- All these models agree on the computable core for a support slot: (i) can my teammate reach point r before any defender (time-to-intercept with reaction time and top speed), (ii) is the lane open (no defender close to the segment or able to intercept the ball flight), (iii) how valuable is r (forward progress/distance to goal). A sim can evaluate this on a coarse candidate set instead of a full grid.
- The "speed window" and "angle window" features (how imprecise the pass may be and still arrive) are a natural fixed-point-friendly proxy for lane openness: count how many of a few sampled pass speeds/angles still reach the intended receiver first.
- Defensive advantage (1.72) and offside-zeroing are two cheap, principled corrections for support scoring.

### Gaps
- Spearman 2018's exact acceleration and max-speed values and the exact meaning of the 23.9 m parameter were garbled in the extracted PDF text; the 0.7 s / 5 m/s values are from reference implementations, not confirmed against the original text.
- The original Fernandez & Bornn equations (velocity offset constant, v_max value) were not retrievable; only the [4, 10] m radius range is confirmed via Martens et al.

## KQ6. Empirical support geometry: distances, pass lengths/angles, number of options, time options stay open

### Takeaway
Tracking studies place coordinated support at roughly 13-24 m from the carrier (three or more supporters appear from ~11 m; "diamond" support shapes rise from 12.7 m and saturate by 23.6 m), team average pass lengths run 15.7-24.4 m, passing lanes stay open on average only ~0.9-1.3 s (penetrative shortest), and the strongest determinant of who receives the pass is proximity to the carrier, then lane openness, then defensive coverage, then forwardness.

### Cited Findings
- Support distance (J1 League 2019, 1,945 received passes): "The percentage that three or more players will be present begins to increase nonlinearly at 10.9 m from the ball carrier"; the diamond-shape occurrence rate "begins to increase nonlinearly at 12.7 m from the ball carrier and begins to decrease at 23.6 m" (8.6% at 12.7 m, 79.9% at 23.6 m); "coordinated support play is possible when three or more players form a DS at a distance of approximately 13 to 24 m from the ball carrier." — [Matsubara, Nomura & Kida 2022, Advances in Physical Education](https://content.scirp.org/pdf/ape_2022053016232546.pdf)
- Team average pass length (InStat, 40 leagues, 2021/22): shortest Shakhtar Donetsk and PSG at 15.7 m; longest Livingston 24.4 m, Birmingham City 23.4 m, Motherwell 23.2 m; ball circulation distance 6.9-12.8 km per match. — [CIES Football Observatory Weekly Post 358](https://football-observatory.com/IMG/sites/b5wp/2021/wp358/en/)
- Bundesliga event taxonomy: short pass < 10 m, medium 10-30 m, long > 30 m; ~915 passes per game (both teams); 85.2% completion. — [Anzer & Bauer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00810-3)
- EPL completion by direction: backward 95.78%, sideways 93.03%, forward 72.28%; final third 58%; into penalty area 37%; ~380 pass attempts per team per game. — [Power et al. 2017](https://mirkwood.cs.edinboro.edu/~bennett/class/csci480/fall2017/notes/p1605.pdf)
- Passes beating a counter-press averaged 18 yards (16.5 m) vs. 16.5 yards (15.1 m) for those that did not. — [JPAS 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2025.2473799)
- Time a passing opportunity stays open (one competitive match, 4,128 opportunities: 1,643 penetrative, 1,254 support, 1,230 backward): "Penetrative: 0.91 ± 1.00; Support: 1.27 ± 1.21, Backward: 1.13 ± 1.16" seconds; durations were longer in the second half after a red card (0.99 ± 1.12 vs 1.13 ± 1.13). — [Gómez-Jordana et al. 2019, Barça Sports Analytics Summit](https://static.capabiliaserver.com/frontend/clients/barca/wp_prod/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/a41ec6ba-final_paper_barcelona-luis-gomez-jordana.pdf)
- Follow-up over 5 Spanish second-division matches: passing opportunities evaluated for current position and for "the players' estimated position a second later (taking into account the player current velocity) as long as the velocity was higher than 2 m/s"; defensive coverage areas built from running velocities with turning capability from 360° at 0 m/s to 40° at the 90th-percentile speed; polygons updated every 0.2 s, ball assumed to travel at 10 m/s; ordering of availability time penetrative < backward < support confirmed. — [Gómez-Jordana et al. 2021, Scientific Reports](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8105376)
- Who gets passed to (1,379 passes, LPM tracking): "short distances to the ball carrier, open passing lanes, loose defense by opposing players, and positions in front of the ball are situational features that are positively related to team members' odds of being passed the ball. The strongest effects were reported for team members' spatial proximity to the ball carrier, followed by the openness of the passing lanes to team members, team members' defensive coverage, and their relative proximity to the opponent's goal"; random choice among the 9 outfield options would be correct 1/9 = 0.11 of the time. — [Steiner et al. 2018, CISS](https://ciss-journal.org/article/download/7564/10559/29272)
- Alternative pass options from tracking (Eredivisie 2018/19, 286,151 passes): options are enumerated per pass with features "length and angle of the pass, position on the field and direction of movement of the passer and receiver, number of opposing players in the passing lane, pressure on the passer and coverage of the receiver"; large differences in risk-reward decision making by positional role. — [Goes et al. 2021, Science and Medicine in Football](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24733938.2021.1944660)
- "Safe configuration" idea: the ball carrier should be part of a "passing clique", i.e. a complete graph of teammates whose passing lanes are all inside the team's passable region (a modified Spearman pitch control); "it is most difficult to disrupt an attacking team's progression forward ... when this attacking team has multiple 'good' options of how to proceed at each moment." — [Controlling ball progression in soccer, arXiv 2210.16474](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.16474v2)
- Receiver pressure radius used by StatsBomb pressure events: 4-5 yards (3.7-4.6 m). — [arXiv 2501.04712](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.04712)
- Rest-defence marking distances as a reference for "loose" marking: nearest defender 5.08 ± 2.23 m. — [Forcher et al. 2023](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf)

### Inferences
- A support ring of 12-24 m from the carrier with a diamond (ahead, left, right, behind) is well supported; closer than ~10 m there is rarely more than one or two supporters in real play, so the sim should not pack three players within 10 m.
- Lanes close in about one second, so a support decision that is re-evaluated slower than ~2-4 Hz will be stale; conversely re-evaluating at 60 Hz without hysteresis will oscillate.
- Because forward passes complete ~72% vs ~95% backward, a support scorer must weigh value against completion or the carrier will always choose the safe option; the data suggests a "dangerous" option is one with ≥ 6% chance of a shot within 10 s.

### Gaps
- No study found reports the typical *count* of simultaneously open passing options for a carrier (Steiner et al. use 9 as the nominal option count; Goes et al. enumerate alternatives but do not report an average count in the accessible text).
- Pass-angle distributions relative to the carrier's facing direction were not found.
- Availability-time numbers come from one match (2019) and five second-division matches (2021); no top-league sample.

## KQ7. Football-AI and robotics heuristics for support positioning and run selection (RoboCup 2D/3D, GRF, SPL)

### Takeaway
Robot-soccer teams solve off-ball positioning with a ball-indexed formation (home slot plus a per-role ball-attraction vector, or a Delaunay-triangulated table mapping ball position to all 11 target positions with linear interpolation), then override near the ball with explicit active roles, add Voronoi-based "best point" search for receivers (far from opponents, near teammates, on the offside line), and use utility-based role exchange with a captain/consensus step to avoid two agents picking the same role; GRF's built-in bot is simpler (non-active players run at the ball out of possession and move forward with the carrier in possession).

### Cited Findings
- FC Portugal SBSP (RoboCup 2000 champions): strategic position = base slot in the formation adjusted by "ball position and velocity, situation (attack, defense, scoring opportunity, etc.) and player type"; per-type parameters are "ball attraction, admissible regions in the field, specific positional characteristics for some regions in the field, tendency to stay behind the ball, alignment in the offside line, and attraction by specific points in the field"; simplified algorithm: `SP = BSP + (BallP.X*BallAttraction.X(PT), BallP.Y*BallAttraction.Y(PT))`, then `adjustToDefenseLine`, `adjustToBehindBall`, `adjustToOnside`, `adjustToAdmissibleRectangle`; situations S1 Attack, S2 Defend, S3 Defense-to-Attack, S4 Attack-to-Defense each pick a formation; SBSP is used only in "strategic situations (in which the agent believes that it is not going to enter in active behavior soon)". — [Reis & Lau, FC Portugal Team Description, RoboCup 2000](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-45324-5_2.pdf)
- FC Portugal DPRE: players exchange positionings (and types) "only if the utility of that exchange is positive for the team. Utilities are calculated using the distances from the player's positions to their strategic positions, the importance of each positioning and the adequacy of each player to each positioning"; "FC Portugal with DPRE wins about 85% of the games against the team without DPRE." — [Reis & Lau 2000](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-45324-5_2.pdf); formal SBSP paper. — [Reis, Lau & Oliveira 2001](https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/handle/10216/443)
- Delaunay formation (HELIOS/agent2d): "a map from a focal point like a ball position in a soccer field to a desirable positioning of each player agent" approximated by Delaunay triangulation; "simple, fast and accurate" and "robust to uneven sample distribution". — [Akiyama & Noda, RoboCup 2007 Springer chapter](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-68847-1_38); HELIOS2010: "An input value of our positioning mechanism is a focal point on the soccer field, usually the ball position. And, output values are agents' strategic positions"; Constrained Delaunay Triangulation adds fixed edges; formations edited with the "fedit" tool; "our model is similar to Situation Based Strategic Positioning, but our model achieved more flexibility." — [HELIOS2010 TDP](https://wrighteagle2d.github.io/robocup/2010/2D_TDP_HELIOS.pdf)
- Gliders2d (agent2d derivative): formation files list a ball position followed by 11 player coordinates per triangulation vertex, e.g. `Ball -48.66 22.71` then `1 -50.72 6.07 ... 11 -22.62 5.8`; "when the ball takes any position within a triangle, each player's position is dynamically adjusted during the runtime"; positioning also samples candidate points from a Voronoi diagram of opponent positions, including intersections of Voronoi segments with the offside line, choosing a point "relatively close to the teammates' positions, and far from the opponents' positions"; agent2d's pressing level is hard-coded as 3 cycles; an added rule makes an agent dash at full power toward the offside line when the ball is in the opponent half, the agent is behind the offside line and within 25 units of the ball's x. Moving defenders/midfielders deeper in the formation files alone improved goal difference vs. agent2d from 2.387 to 3.210. — [Gliders2d, arXiv 1812.10202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.10202)
- Google Research Football: the opponent and non-controlled teammates are rule-based bots inherited from GameplayFootball; non-active teammates' default behaviour "corresponds to running towards the ball when we are not in possession, or move forward together with our active player"; the environment removed the action to switch control to non-active players on defence because "policies tended to exploit this action to return control to built-in AI behaviors." — [Kurach et al. 2020, GRF (arXiv 1907.11180)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11180)
- Game-AI formation heuristic in a physics-based 11v11 demo: each player has a formation target that "adjusts dynamically, expanding or contracting toward the user's team or the opposing team depending on the ball's position ... the farther the ball is from the halfway line, the greater the extent of this movement"; opponents "move towards the ball if the ball is within 10 m of their respective current positions and within 15 m of their respective target positions. If there is no opposing player who satisfies both conditions ... the closest opposing player [moves] towards the ball"; only the ball-chaser faces the ball, others face their movement direction. — [PhysicsFC, SIGGRAPH 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3731425)
- RoboCup SPL (B-Human/B-HULKs 2017): roles are Queen (plays the ball), Knight ("positioned offensively to help the Queen, jumping in when she looses the ball"), Bishop ("positioned far inside the opponent half to be able to receive a pass"), two Rooks (defensive, "moving mainly horizontally in front of the goal"); "every robot is calculating a role selection suggestion for itself and each connected teammate, but uses the role selection of the captain robot" to avoid robots "play[ing] with different role selections at the same time"; teammates are forced "to spread themselves over the field equally (by role selection)"; the authors warn that "Re-planning in each behavior cycle bears the risk of oscillations, i.e. repeatedly changing the decision". — [Röfer et al., B-Human 2017](https://www.informatik.uni-bremen.de/kogrob/papers/RC-Roefer-etal-18.pdf)
- Humanoid-league supporter: "The position of the supporter is chosen in a way to reduce the chances of the opponent team for scoring a goal and to increase the chances of the robot to take over the role of its teammate manipulating ball in case the teammate falls down or is being dodged"; parameterisation gives more defensive or offensive tactics. — [Petersen, Stoll & von Stryk 2011, RoboCup 2010 LNCS](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-3-642-20217-9_33)

### Inferences
- The common pattern is layered: (1) a ball-indexed formation gives every slot a default target; (2) a small set of active roles near the ball override it; (3) a receiver/supporter picks a concrete point by a cheap spatial search (Voronoi vertex, offside-line intersection) maximising distance from opponents and keeping moderate distance to teammates; (4) role assignment is done once per cycle with a utility and a single arbiter (captain or lowest id) to prevent duplicates.
- The reader's formation slots with attack/defence phases plus an elected chaser already implement layers (1), (2) and (4); what is missing from the GRF-style baseline is layer (3) and a situation-indexed formation table for the two transition situations (S3/S4 in SBSP terms).

### Gaps
- TU Darmstadt's supporter-positioning paper (potential-field terms and parameters) and the Northern Bites "Player positioning in the four-legged league" PDF could not be fetched (404 / rate-limit), so no numeric supporter offsets from the SPL are available.
- The GameplayFootball/GRF built-in AI source formulas were not inspected; a search excerpt claimed the bot "does not have programmed rules to prevent getting into an offside position" but the originating paper could not be confirmed.
- "Stochastic positioning" as a named RoboCup technique was not found.

## KQ8. Timing of runs and avoiding two players attacking the same space

### Takeaway
Evidence for timing is indirect but consistent: lanes stay open for ~1 s, availability is evaluated with a ~1 s look-ahead for players moving faster than 2 m/s, run value is measured from run start to 2 s after the peak, and the most valuable off-ball HI runs are the "inside-to-back" runs behind the defence; de-confliction in AI systems is done by one arbiter assigning roles/targets by utility with hysteresis and by spreading via Voronoi/Delaunay geometry.

### Cited Findings
- Availability horizon: passing opportunities are computed "for: (1) the players' current position and (2) to the players' estimated position a second later (taking into account the player current velocity) as long as the velocity was higher than 2 m/s"; polygons updated every 0.2 s. — [Gómez-Jordana et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8105376)
- Lane lifetimes ~0.9-1.3 s (penetrative shortest). — [Gómez-Jordana et al. 2019](https://static.capabiliaserver.com/frontend/clients/barca/wp_prod/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/a41ec6ba-final_paper_barcelona-luis-gomez-jordana.pdf)
- Run valuation window: EPV of the attacking team taken "when the player starts to increase the speed ... and two seconds after the peak of the effort ends"; run types defined by origin/destination relative to the opponent block (Inside, Wing, Back); Chelsea example: replacing Werner would cut disruptive runs from the inside from 17.6 to 14.4 per 30 effective minutes in possession and from the left wing from 9.8 to 7.5; in Havertz's UCL-final goal "two inside-to-back runs make the goal chance possible, the former by Werner to drag the central defender". — [Llana et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02313.pdf)
- Receivers' pre-reception speed differs by player: Messi receives while walking far more than other strikers; Werner is "in the top 5% of players at high intensities" when receiving; "strikers generate significant increases in the possession value at higher speeds." — [Llana et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02313.pdf)
- Off-ball "Move to Receive/Exploit Space", "Run in Behind/Penetrate" and "Support Play" are the key in-possession HI actions for offensive players; Support Play is when "the ball is played forward quickly and the players behind or level with the ball tend to produce high-intensity efforts to become involved." — [Ju et al. 2023](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/17199/1/BS_Art_46976-10.pdf)
- Pass intent modelled as sending the ball where interception is least likely (OBSO transition term weighted by attacking PPCF); offside attackers get zero control. — [Spearman 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327139841_Beyond_Expected_Goals)
- Space-generation gain credits a runner for "the space that an attacker frees up by dragging the opponents into his direction." — [Martens et al. 2021 describing Fernandez & Bornn 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.676179/full)
- De-confliction in robot soccer: single-arbiter role selection (captain), role exchange only when team utility is positive, equal spreading by role selection, explicit warning about per-cycle re-planning oscillation. — [B-Human 2017](https://www.informatik.uni-bremen.de/kogrob/papers/RC-Roefer-etal-18.pdf); [Reis & Lau 2000](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-45324-5_2.pdf)
- Gliders2d picks receiver points from Voronoi vertices of opponent positions and offside-line intersections, "relatively close to the teammates' positions, and far from the opponents' positions." — [arXiv 1812.10202](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.10202)

### Inferences
- "When to go": start a run when (a) the carrier can pass (low pressure, facing forward), (b) the runner's 1-s look-ahead position is reachable by the ball before any defender, and (c) the runner is onside at kick time; because lanes last ~1 s, a run should be committed for at least ~1 s (60 frames) before re-evaluation.
- "Dragging" runs (Werner-type inside-to-back) are valuable even when not targeted; a sim can credit them by letting a decoy run reserve a destination and lower the mark on a teammate.
- Two-runners-same-space is prevented in robot soccer by three mechanisms the sim can mirror: distinct role labels per slot (Knight vs Bishop), utility-based assignment by one arbiter, and geometric spreading (Voronoi/Delaunay) that makes the second-best point for player B differ from A's point by construction.

### Gaps
- No tracking study found that directly measures reaction latency between a passing opportunity opening and the run starting, or the optimal lead time of a run before the pass.

## Implementable recommendations for the 60 Hz deterministic sim

### Takeaway
Replace the flat 70% cruise with a transition-urgency state machine keyed to a 5 s counter-press window and a ~9 s re-organisation window, with role-specific speed bands and behind-ball/rest-defence counts; replace GRF-style "move forward with the carrier" with a cheap OBSO-like scorer over a 12-24 m support ring, re-evaluated every 15-30 frames with hysteresis and destination reservation.

### Cited Findings (parameter sources)
- 5 s regain window, 20 s shot window, 4+ behind ball after 1 s, numerical superiority within 10 m (36.2% vs 30.2%), speed of regaining team at 2 s, local compactness 6-10 m, ~27% of transitions counter-pressed, average transition 9.3 s, fallback 18.3 s. — [Bauer & Anzer 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-021-00763-7)
- Mean transition ~10 s, peak 22-27 s, HSR 7-9x match rate; HSR 19.8 km/h, sprint 25.2 km/h. — [Bortnik et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9536371); attackers lowest in defensive transitions, FB/CM sprint most. — [Bortnik et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10955741/)
- Recovery run ~26 m / ~3 s, 4-8 per match; Close Down ~15 m / ~1 s; 20-53% HI drop after peaks. — [Ju et al. 2023](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/17199/1/BS_Art_46976-10.pdf); [Ju et al. 2022](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/15958/1/BS_Art_45951-10.pdf)
- Rest defence 3.7 defenders vs 2.0 attackers in a 10 m band, 28 m wide, 44 m from own goal, 5 m marking, 12 s success window. — [Forcher et al. 2023](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf)

### Inferences (concrete rules; frame counts assume 60 Hz; all values are starting ranges to tune)
Transition urgency after a turnover (team loses ball at frame f0):
1. Phase timers: COUNTERPRESS = [f0, f0+300] (5 s); RECOVER = (f0+300, f0+540..600] (9-10 s); after that, normal defence. Exit COUNTERPRESS early on regain, ball out of play, or when the ball leaves the pressing cluster (pass longer than ~16 m away from the nearest presser).
2. Press-or-fall-back decision at f0+6..12 (0.1-0.2 s reaction): count own players vs opponents within 10 m of the ball (R_press = 10 m) and own players behind the ball. Counter-press if own_within_10m >= opp_within_10m AND behind_ball >= 4 (or will be >= 4 within 60 frames at sprint). Otherwise fall back. Expect this to fire on roughly 25-35% of turnovers; if it fires on most, tighten the superiority condition.
3. Counter-press roles: presser = elected chaser (sprint band, >= 7 m/s equivalent); two nearest teammates become lane-closers: targets = nearest points on the two best opponent passing lanes (Pressing-Intensity lane variant), high-speed band (5.5-7 m/s). Everyone else recovers (rule 4). After 300 frames without regain, presser and lane-closers drop into RECOVER.
4. Recovery speed by behind-ball deficit: for each non-presser, d = (player_x_in_attack_dir - ball_x). If d > 0 (ahead of the ball): run goal-side at sprint until d <= -3 m or until behind_ball >= 4 and own rest-defence line is set; cap each sprint burst at ~180-240 frames (3-4 s, 26 m) then fall to the high-speed band. If d <= 0 but the player is outside his defensive slot radius (> 10-12 m): high-speed band (5.5-7 m/s). Only players already inside slot radius and goal-side use cruise (70%). Fatigue: multiply the burst cap and speed band by 0.5-0.8 for players who have just completed a peak period (HI distance in last 60 s > team-typical ~70 m).
5. Rest defence (while attacking): 3-4 players (CBs + one FB/CM, chosen by slot) hold a line 10 m goal-side of the deepest opponent attacker, width ~28 m, never further than ~45 m from own goal line when the ball is in the final third; loose marks at ~5 m, tightening to ~2-3 m as ball distance falls below ~20 m or carrier pressure rises. These players never join the counter-press.
6. Forwards in defensive transition: the forward nearest the ball joins the press (rule 3); other forwards drop at high speed only until level with the ball or to a holding line ~10 m goal-side of the ball x, then stop and shadow the nearest passing lane (1-2 s closing bursts of ~15 m). This yields the observed "attackers lowest TD in defensive transitions but still 7x match HSR".
7. Transition formation: add two situation-indexed formation tables (SBSP S3 defence-to-attack, S4 attack-to-defence) blended by ball x; in S4 the table compresses toward own goal and the ball-attraction gain in x is higher (e.g. 0.5-0.7 of ball displacement) than in settled defence.

Support positioning in attack (team has ball):
8. Candidate set per supporter: 6-8 points = formation slot (ball-attracted), diamond points at 14 m and 20 m around the carrier (ahead/left/right/behind), the nearest Voronoi vertex of opponent positions within 25 m, and the point on the offside line nearest the slot. Reject points within 10 m of a teammate's reserved destination (destination reservation) unless it is the formation slot.
9. Score(p) = Control(p) × Value(p) × LaneOpen(p):
   - Control(p): time-to-intercept for self and every opponent, t = 0.7 s + dist/5.0 m/s (fixed-point LUT), logistic with σ = 0.45 s, control-rate λ = 4.3 s⁻¹ for attackers and 4.3×1.72 for defenders; set attacker term to 0 if p is offside. Use a 3-4 step time integration or the closed-form "first arrival" approximation.
   - Value(p): forward progress and distance to goal (negative exponential a·exp(-b·dist) + c with a 0.93, b 0.14, c 0.02 as a starting curve) or a coarse 25×16 xT table.
   - LaneOpen(p): fraction of 5 sampled pass speeds × 5 angles (±16° around the lane, matching the ~33° window of completed passes) that reach the supporter first; also require no defender within ~4.6 m of the supporter at arrival (pressure radius) unless Value is very high.
10. Timing/hysteresis: re-score every 15-30 frames (0.25-0.5 s, half a lane lifetime); switch target only if new score > current × 1.15 (or + fixed margin) and commit to a new run for at least 60 frames; evaluate candidate p at the 1-s look-ahead position when the supporter is moving > 2 m/s.
11. Run triggers: start a run-in-behind when the carrier faces forward, no defender within 4.6 m of the carrier, the runner is onside and his 1-s look-ahead point has Control > 0.5; at most one "penetrate" runner and one "support from behind" at a time, assigned by one arbiter (highest score wins, second-best takes the other role), mirroring Queen/Knight/Bishop.
12. Decoys: allow a run whose own Control is low but which pulls its marker (defender within 3 m moving with it) if it raises a teammate's Control by > 0.1; credit via the same reservation so a second runner does not take the vacated lane.

### Gaps
- All thresholds above are inferred starting points; none of the cited studies tested them in a simulation. The counter-press trigger rate (25-35%), the 10 m superiority radius and the 4-behind-ball rule are the only transition parameters directly supported by data; speed-band mapping to sim units must be calibrated against the sim's own top speed.
- The fixed-point costs of per-candidate time-to-intercept for 11 opponents × 8 candidates × 10 supporters (~900 evaluations per re-score) were not assessed; a LUT-based logistic and 0.25-0.5 s re-score cadence are suggested to keep this cheap.
